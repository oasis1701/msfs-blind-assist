The PMDG 777's panels are now laid out the way the flight deck is, so arrowing
down a panel walks it the way a hand crosses it.

Every control list used to follow the order the variables happened to be
declared in. The EFIS panel is the clearest example: it listed all four rotaries
and then all fifteen buttons, where the real panel puts each rotary next to its
own centre push -- MINS with RST, MODE with CTR, RANGE with TFC, BARO with STD.
The MCP now runs outboard to outboard, and the hydraulic panel reads as the
demand row then the primary row, each left to right.

Several controls were also on the wrong panel. The parking brake was listed
twice and is now only on the control stand, leaving the autobrake selector on
the forward panel where it belongs; emergency exit lights and the service
interphone move to a new Emergency and Service panel on the overhead, where they
sit beside passenger oxygen; the centre display control panel and the aileron
and rudder trims move to the forward and aft aisle stands; and master warning
moves from the pedestal to the glareshield. The old Lights panel splits into
Exterior and Interior, absorbing Panel Lighting.

Two things deliberately do not follow the aircraft. Each engine's start selector
stays beside its own fuel control, because a manual start costs a blind pilot a
panel switch where a sighted pilot only glances; and the First Officer's EFIS
panel mirrors the Captain's. Nothing changed about what any switch does.
