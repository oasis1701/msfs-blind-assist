Fly the flare and the rollout by ear. Tick **Manual landing assist (flare and rollout
tones)** when you set the destination runway and, from 50 feet above the runway to the
moment you slow to taxi speed, two tones tell you what the aeroplane is doing.

Through the flare you hear both axes at once. Pitch carries your sink rate: a high tone
means you are coming down too fast and should pull, a low tone means you are floating,
and silence means you are on the profile — so a well-flown flare is a quiet one. The
other tone pans left and right with your position on the centreline, and because it
follows how fast you are drifting as well as where you are, a properly crabbed approach
sounds centred instead of nagging at you. After touchdown the sink-rate tone stops and
the panning one stays with you down the rollout, ending with "Rollout guidance complete"
— or handing straight over to your exit if you have a landing exit planned.

Going around is spoken, never silent, because silence here means "on profile" and must
never be mistaken for the assist having given up. The box is unticked by default, so an
approach flown on the ILS sounds exactly as it does today unless you ask for this.
