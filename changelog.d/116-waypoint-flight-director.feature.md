A synthetic flight director now guides hand-flown legs between the waypoints you track
with Shift+F. You hear two tones — one for where the aircraft should be, one for where it
is — and you fly until they match: the command tone pans left and right for bank and
shifts in pitch for climb and descent. It covers the stretch between the takeoff tone and
the landing tone, so a hand-flown departure no longer has to end with engaging the
autopilot shortly after takeoff.

Ctrl+F starts and stops it. It is built from stock simulator data alone, so it works in
every aircraft, IFR or VFR, with no autopilot or real flight director needed, and it goes
quiet on its own while the autopilot is flying. A tracked waypoint can carry a crossing
altitude, and a leg can hold a course or radial through its fix instead of flying direct.
Ctrl+K adds a separate rudder cue: a tick in the ear of the rudder to press, silent once
you are coordinated.
