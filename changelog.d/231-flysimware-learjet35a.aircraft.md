The Flysimware Learjet 35A is now a supported aircraft. Every panel of the vendor's cockpit map is there — engine start and electrics, the FC-530 autopilot, both altimeters, pressurization, fuel, anti-ice, lighting, audio, the Collins radios, the GTX 345 transponder, the tablet's options, cabin and payload — plus a spoken main annunciator panel with master caution, GNS 530 and 430 windows with the bezel on the keyboard (Alt+N, Alt+P) that read the page in front of you by its structure, say which page or dialog is up, tell you what each key did, let you type an ident with Ctrl+T instead of spelling it with the knobs, and walk you through the self-test; D, Shift+D and Ctrl+W read the GNS flight plan (destination, an estimated top of descent, the next waypoint) and passing a waypoint is called out; engine readouts in jet terms: N1 and N2 on P, ITT and oil on Shift+O; and the 35A's speeds on Shift+1 to 6: takeoff, Vref and stall computed at your weight, and the Vmo, flap and gear limits.
