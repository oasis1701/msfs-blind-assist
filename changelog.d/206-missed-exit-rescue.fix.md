Roll past your planned runway exit and MSFSBA no longer gives up on the runway while there is still a mile of it ahead of you. It used to check only the exit list built for the planner dialog — a list that quietly drops every rapid-exit taxiway the scenery hasn't marked with a hold-short bar — so at Toronto 23 it announced "missed last exit" 100 ft past H2 with 5,400 ft of pavement and three more turnoffs to come, then counted you down to the end and left you turning around on an active runway. It now asks the airport's own taxi layout for the next real turnoff and retargets to it, and only says the runway has run out of exits when it actually has. Checked across 120 large airports: it changes nothing on the 533 runway directions that were already right, and rescues 25 that were not, among them Colorado Springs, Lincoln, Singapore Changi, Riyadh, Istanbul, Cairo, Cologne and Beirut. A related case is closed at the same time: after missing a high-speed turnoff, which MSFSBA lets you keep turning into for up to 500 ft, the next exit it offered could be one you had already rolled past — it now only ever offers one that is genuinely still in front of you.
