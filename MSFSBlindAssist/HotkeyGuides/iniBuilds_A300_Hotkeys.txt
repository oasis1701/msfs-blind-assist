Application Navigation:

  Ctrl+2     Focus Panels List

Tip: Press the same bracket key again to cancel a mode you activated by accident.


READ THIS FIRST - WHAT THIS VERSION COVERS

Choose iniBuilds Airbus A300-600 from the Aircraft menu. The passenger and freighter versions,
with either engine, share one cockpit, so one choice covers all four. This version covers the
A300's switch panels: the overhead, the glareshield, the main instrument panel, the pedestal
and the cockpit's back wall, both MCDUs as text (Shift+M), the FCU, autopilot and radios with
typed values, the tablet (Shift+T), and the screens: the flight mode annunciator spoken as it
changes, and status boxes for the PFD, ND, engine instruments, standby instruments and nine
ECAM system pages. It also speaks what you need while flying: the take-off call-outs (V1,
Rotate, V2), the fault and warning lights, autopilot and autothrottle disconnects, and the FCU
altitude window. Everything is read from the aircraft's own values, not from pictures.

The upper ECAM's memos are read as the aircraft shows them: the ECAM Memos status box in the
Displays section lists them ("APU running, Parking brake on, Seat belts on"). They are not
announced: when you switch a sign or light, your screen reader reads the switch, as on the other
aircraft.

Not in the status boxes: the upper ECAM's warnings, and the ND's waypoint name and bearing. The
A300 keeps these inside its own display software, not in values the simulator shares. Read them
with the AI display reads in output mode (Alt+E for the ECAM warning display, Alt+N for the ND;
see "Display reads" below), read the next waypoint on the MCDU's F-PLN page (Shift+M), and hear
warnings by the master warning and master caution call-outs.


THE PANELS

Sections (Ctrl+1): Overhead, Glareshield, Instrument, Displays, Pedestal, Cockpit, Cargo. The
panels (Ctrl+2) are sorted by system and named the way the other Airbuses in MSFS Blind Assist
name them. Where the A300 has its own panel, it keeps the A300 manual's name.

Overhead: Electrical, IRS, APU, Oxygen, Fire, Hydraulics, Fuel, Air Conditioning, Bleed,
Pressurization, Ventilation, Anti-Ice, Window and Probe Heat, Wipers, Signs, Interior Lighting,
Exterior Lighting, Flight Controls (spoilers, pitch feel, rudder travel limiters), SAS Control
(the pitch trim, yaw damper and autothrottle levers), Cockpit Door, Cargo Smoke, Recorder, Engine
Start.
Glareshield: FCU, EFIS Captain, EFIS First Officer.
Instrument: Warnings (both pilots' master warning and master caution), Gear, Autobrake, Thrust
Rating Panel, Landing Elevation, Standby Instruments, Source Switching, Clock, GPWS, Captain Side
and First Officer Side (each altimeter, the oxygen tests and the panel lights).
Pedestal: Engines (the fuel levers), Thrust Levers (TOGA and the autothrottle disconnect), Flaps
and Speed Brake, Parking Brake, Trim, ECAM Control Panel (with the normal and emergency cancel
buttons and the takeoff config test), Weather Radar, Transponder, VHF Radios, Navigation Radios,
ADF Radios, Audio Control Panel Captain and First Officer, MCDU Brightness, IDC, Pedestal
Lighting.
Cockpit: Yokes, RAT, Circuit Breakers, Cockpit. Cargo: the freighter's main deck cargo door.

Within each panel, the controls come in the order the aircraft's own checklist sets them, then
the ones it never mentions. A few panels open on a set start: Electrical on the batteries,
external power and generators, Warnings on the captain's buttons, Gear on the gear lever, and
Flaps and Speed Brake on the flaps lever. Where both
pilots' controls share a panel, each says its side first: "Captain chrono", "First officer GPWS
test".

A switch is a combo box with the aircraft's own position words. A push button is a button.
Buttons with a light say what it shows: "Pressurization system 1: On" (pressing system 2 picks
it and puts system 1 off), on the EFIS panels "Airports: On", "FPA: On", "Decision height
push: On", and on the Source Switching panel "Captain air data to system 2: On". The map filters
pick one at a time. When you press one, your screen reader reads its new label, and you hear only
the other lights it changes ("Captain waypoints light off").

The Clock panel's buttons say their state: "Captain chrono: Running" (each press goes reset,
running, stopped) and "Captain elapsed time: Running" (each press starts or stops it; it restarts
from zero). Its status display reads each clock: "Captain clock: 07:42 GMT", "Captain chrono: 1
minute 5 seconds", "Captain elapsed time: 1 hour 5 minutes". The landing elevation knob says its
value after each step: "Landing elevation 50 feet". A
dimmer or temperature knob is a slider. A knob that only turns, such as the FCU speed knob or an
ADF frequency knob, is a pair of buttons, "increase" and "decrease", each one click of the
knob. The flaps lever, the speed brake lever and the ground spoiler arm are the Flaps and Speed
Brake panel.

When you change a control, MSFS Blind Assist moves it exactly the way a click in the cockpit
would. Nothing is spoken back - your screen reader already says what you chose - unless the
control could not be moved, when you hear why: "Battery 1 unavailable" (the simulator connection
cannot carry the change), or "Switch not ready, please try again in a moment." (a switch must be
read before it may be moved; this clears within a second or two of connecting). If the aircraft
does not move a switch you chose, you hear that it did not move and the control goes back to
where the switch is: "Crossbleed did not move" (the crossbleed button works only with the
crossbleed mode in Manual).

A trim switch (aileron trim, rudder trim, the pitch trim switches on the yokes, the cabin
manual vertical speed switch) springs back: choosing a side holds it there for one second and
lets it return, the way a short press does in the cockpit, and the control goes back to Neutral.
Choose the side again for more. The Trim panel's status display reads the pitch trim ("Pitch trim:
1.1 degrees nose up"), the rudder trim as its display shows it ("Rudder trim: left 2.8") and the
rudder trim reset light, which is also the reset button's label while the trim runs back to zero.
The Flaps and Speed Brake panel's status display reads the slat and flap angles in degrees as they
move. The Thrust Levers panel's reverser unlocked and deployed lights are announced as they change.

The ECAM Control Panel's page buttons say whether their light is on. The lit one is the page the
lower ECAM shows, the one you chose or the automatic one: "Fuel page: On". Pressing the lit one
goes back to the automatic page, and the light of the page that replaces it is announced. The
Clear button says whether its light is on.

The Weather Radar panel's status display reads the antenna tilt in degrees ("Antenna tilt: 3
degrees down"); the tilt slider runs from 15 degrees down at 0 to 15 degrees up at 100. The
Transponder panel's status display reads the squawk code, and each step of a code knob says the
new code: "Squawk 7100". The large knob changes the first two digits, the small knob the last two.

The VHF and ADF panels each have two windows and a transfer switch that picks which window is in
use. It does not swap them, so after a transfer the ADF knobs called "standby" tune the frequency
in use. The ADF panels have their frequency knobs; the VHF, VOR and ILS frequencies have none here,
because each has a typed box (see Typed Values below): type the frequency and press Set. Each ADF
knob step says which window it changed: "ADF 1 standby 990" or "ADF 1 active 900". A VHF or ADF
transfer says the frequency now in use: "VHF 1 active 124.805". The status displays read each
radio's active and standby frequencies, and each ADF's bearing to its station ("45 degrees right",
or "no signal"). The Navigation Radios panel's status display reads the VOR and ILS frequencies and
courses, and each course knob step says the new course: "VOR 1 course 091".

On each Audio Control Panel, the VHF transmit keys say which one you transmit on: "VHF 1
transmit: On". Each receiver switch turns that radio on or off in the headset.

The Cargo Door panel (Cargo section) opens and closes the main cargo door. Pick how far it opens
with "Cargo door 70 degrees" or "Cargo door 145 degrees" (the picked one says "Selected"), then
choose Open or Close on the cargo door switch. MSFS Blind Assist holds the switch until the door
has had time to finish, about 35 seconds, then says where it stopped: "Cargo door open 70
degrees", "Cargo door closed and locked". Choose Neutral to stop it sooner. The status display
reads the door too.

The fire tests on the Fire panel are held as a pilot holds them: 7 seconds for an engine loop
test, 5 seconds for the APU loop test, 3 seconds for a squib test. The loop lights and the fire
handle light a loop test brings on, and the squib lights a squib test brings on, are announced (see
Background Announcements). The takeoff
config test (ECAM Control Panel), the landing gear warning test (Gear) and the smoke test (Cargo
Smoke) are held 5 seconds too: a short press never gave the takeoff config test time to warn.
Other test buttons are a short press.

Each cargo smoke agent switch, on the Cargo Smoke panel, has its guard just before it. The switch
only moves while its guard is open, exactly as in the aircraft.

The Thrust Rating Panel's buttons say whether their light is on: "TRP climb: On". In AUTO only the
AUTO button is on; the aircraft picks the limit itself, and the panel's status display says which
(see below). Pressing the flex temperature knob's increase or decrease button reads the new
temperature back once it has moved ("Flex temperature 46 degrees").

The buttons on the Autobrake panel say what their lights show: "Autobrake low: Armed",
"Decel" while the autobrake is braking, or "Off". Pressing the armed level again turns the
autobrake off, as in the aircraft. The aircraft arms it only with the gear lever down and
hydraulic pressure. A press says nothing, because the button you pressed says Armed or Off itself.
The autobrake lights you did not press are announced as they change, like the other lights: another
level going out ("Autobrake low armed light off"), a DECEL light ("Autobrake low decel light on"),
and the aircraft turning the autobrake off by itself. The Autobrake panel's status display also
reads the brake gauge: accumulator pressure and left and right brake pressure ("Brake accumulator
pressure: 3,000 psi"), and the brakes hot light. The Gear panel's
status display has each gear's down, unlocked and door open lights, spoken as they change ("3
lights off: Nose gear door open, Left gear door open, Right gear door open").

Status Display (Ctrl+3): the FCU panel shows the four FCU windows; Captain Side, First Officer
Side and Standby Instruments show the altimeter settings; Landing Elevation shows the landing
elevation; the Thrust Rating Panel shows the flex temperature and a TRP line with the thrust
rating mode, the limit AUTO chose or the flex temperature, and the N1 limit ("TRP: AUTO, climb, N1
limit 95.3 percent"; no N1 limit with PW engines, whose panel shows EPR); the Navigation Radios
panel shows the VOR and ILS courses. Warnings shows the master warning and master caution lights.
Each panel with fault or warning lights shows them too ("Engine 1 generator fault light: On"):
Fire, Electrical, Hydraulics, Flight Controls, Air Conditioning, Bleed, Pressurization, Anti-Ice,
Window and Probe Heat, APU, Engines, GPWS, Captain Side, EFIS Captain, ECAM Control Panel and
Cockpit Door.


TYPED VALUES - type, then press Set or Enter

  FCU panel: Speed or Mach (100 to 399 knots, or Mach 0.10 to 0.99; a value in the other unit
  switches SPD/MACH for you), Heading (0 to 360), Altitude (100 to 49000 feet, nearest 100),
  Vertical speed (0, or 100 to 6000 either way, nearest 100). You hear "Heading 270".
  Altitude, vertical speed and decision height take whole numbers, typed without a thousands
  separator: "-1500", not "-1,500", and "12000", not "12,000" or "12 000". A comma is read as a
  decimal point, so these are refused with "type a whole number, without a thousands separator"
  (a panel's box adds the range for "12,000" and "12 000", and any box adds it for text that is
  not a number); a vertical speed of 1 to 99, up or down, is refused too (0 is fine).
  Navigation Radios panel: VOR 1 and VOR 2 frequency (108.00 to 117.95) and course setting, ILS
  frequency (108.10 to 111.95) and course setting.
  VHF Radios panel: VHF 1 and VHF 2 standby frequency (118.000 to 136.990); the transfer button
  puts the standby frequency in use. You hear "VHF 1 standby 121.500".
  Transponder panel: Squawk, four digits each 0 to 7.
  Captain Side, First Officer Side and Standby Instruments panels: the altimeter settings, in
  inches (29.92) or hectopascals (1013). A side in STD is taken out of STD first, as Ctrl+B does.
  EFIS Captain panel: Decision height, 0, or 10 to 2500 feet (0 clears it; 1 to 9 is refused, as no
  real decision height is that low and "1,000" would reach the box as 1). It sets both pilots'
  minimums, which the PFD box shows and the aircraft's "minimums" call-out uses. You hear
  "Decision height 200 feet".

The FCU's mode buttons say whether their mode is on: "Heading select: On", "Level change: Off".
SPD/MACH says "Speed" or "Mach". The FCU panel's status display shows the four windows. Pressing
an FCU knob's increase or decrease button reads its window back once it has moved ("Heading
271", "Altitude 12,100 feet").


THE DISPLAYS

The flight mode annunciator is spoken as it changes, in the A300's own words: "Thrust mode:
Speed", "Pitch mode: Profile climb", "Roll mode: Heading select", "Glide slope armed", "Land",
"Flare", "Go around". P.CLB is Profile climb, ALT* is Altitude capture, HDG/S is Heading select,
HDG is Heading hold; level change shows as Thrust mode: Thrust with Pitch mode: Speed. It stays
quiet while the aircraft is cold and dark (the annunciator is blank until an IRS is aligned) and
when the annunciator first appears. Ctrl+M has a row for each part: Thrust mode, Pitch mode,
Roll mode, Land flare rollout and go-around, and Armed modes.

The Displays section has a status box (Ctrl+3) for each screen:

  PFD                  The annunciator (thrust, pitch, roll, armed modes and the autopilot,
                       CMD 1, CMD 2 or Dual), the FCU windows, attitude, heading, airspeed,
                       altitude, vertical speed, radio altitude, localizer and glideslope
                       deviation in dots ("1.2 dots left of localizer", "0.8 dots above
                       glideslope"), V1, VR and V2, VLS, VMAX, green dot, S and F speeds, VS and
                       minimums. V2 is the FCU speed window, shown only when it is no more than
                       30 knots above VR (with the window left at 250 it reads not set). Green
                       dot, S and F say "not shown at this flap setting" where the speed tape does
                       not draw them; the deviation reads "not shown, EFIS not on ILS" unless the
                       captain's EFIS nav selector is on ILS, as the PFD shows it.
  ND                   Mode and range, distance to the next waypoint, ground speed, true
                       airspeed, wind, VOR 1 and 2 with DME, the ILS with localizer and
                       glideslope reception, ADF 1 and 2.
  Engine Instruments   N1, EGT, N2 and fuel flow for each engine.
  ECAM pages           Engine, Electrical AC, Electrical DC, Hydraulics, Bleed, Air
                       Conditioning, Pressurization, Fuel and APU: the numbers each ECAM page
                       prints. Fuel reads in the weight unit set on the tablet.

The standby instruments (attitude, airspeed, the standby altimeter and its setting, the compass)
are read on the Instrument section's Standby Instruments panel, with its knob and cage. A system
panel's own status display also carries its ECAM page's numbers after its lights: Electrical
shows the AC and DC voltages, frequencies and currents; APU shows its speed, EGT, bleed pressure
and generator; Hydraulics shows the blue, green and yellow pressures; Fuel shows each tank and
the total, in the weight unit set on the tablet; Air Conditioning shows the compartment
temperatures; Bleed shows the bleed and pack pressures and temperatures; Pressurization shows
the cabin altitude and differential pressure; Engine Start shows each engine's start valve open
and starter armed lights, then N1, EGT, N2, fuel flow and the ECAM engine page, so a start can be
flown from it: ignition to Start A, the starter on, the fuel lever on at 20 percent N2 (Engines
panel), and "Engine 2 start valve open light off" when the start is done. Oxygen shows its six gauges: the crew and courier low pressure and the four crew
oxygen high pressure gauges, in psi ("Crew oxygen high pressure 1: 1,500 psi").

On the Oxygen panel, the crew oxygen supply button works the courier low pressure supply in this
aircraft, so the two switches always show the same position.


THE MCDU WINDOW - Press left bracket, then Shift+M

Both MCDUs, Captain and First Officer, read as text: the real screens, live. The window opens on
the screen list: the title, then each line with its label above it, numbered 1 to 6 for the
line-select keys, then the scratchpad. When the page changes you hear its title and the cursor
goes to line 1. The window switches on the aircraft's MCDU export (the tablet's External
Hardware setting) every time it opens, and again after a flight load while it stays open,
because the aircraft forgets it at each load. Shown again on the page you left, the window puts
you back on the line you were on; on any other page, and the first time, you start at the title.

How the screen reads: # is an empty box the MCDU wants filled (dashes are optional fields), the
arrows beside a title mean there are more pages (Right Arrow or Alt+Right goes to the next one),
and a degree sign is a degree sign. A screen that stays blank for a second says so: the MCDU is
probably unpowered. The MCDU blanks for a moment on every page change; that is ignored, and the
cursor stays where it was. The status box at the top shows the MCDU's lit annunciators, MSG, FAIL,
DSPY and OFST ("Captain MCDU: MSG"), and one coming on is announced while the window is open. The
Captain's MSG is announced as the fault light "MCDU message light" wherever you are, so the window
does not say it a second time.

Aligning the IRSs: with all three IRS selectors in Navigate and a FROM/TO on INIT A, INIT A offers
ALIGN IRS on right line-select key 4 (Alt+4). The IRS panel's status display then counts each
IRS down: "IRS 1 alignment: aligning, 2 minutes 27 seconds left", then "aligned". Before ALIGN
IRS it says "waiting for ALIGN IRS on the MCDU".

  Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+6   Left line-select keys 1 to 6
  Alt+1 to Alt+6     Right line-select keys 1 to 6
                     (or F1 to F6 and F7 to F12, with the alternate layout in File > FMC Settings)
  Page Up / Down     Slew up and down
  Alt+Up / Alt+Down  Slew up and down; on the MCDU unit combo box they open its list instead
  Right Arrow        Next page (on the screen list); Alt+Right from anywhere in the window
  Backspace          CLR, one character (on the screen list)
  Delete             Clear the whole scratchpad (on the screen list)
  Alt+S              Go to the input box: type, then Enter sends it to the scratchpad
  Alt+Home           Back to the screen list
  Ctrl+Shift+L / R   Captain or First Officer MCDU
  Escape             Close the window
  Page keys          Alt+I Init, Alt+F F-Plan, Alt+P Prog, Alt+D Dir To, Alt+R Ref, Alt+O Mode,
                     Alt+T Tact Mode, Alt+A T/O Appr, Alt+E Eng Out, Alt+L Sec Plan, Alt+M Menu,
                     Alt+C Clr, Alt+N Next Page, Alt+U slew up, Alt+W slew down

The scratchpad is read back once what you typed has landed. An entry holding a character the MCDU
keyboard does not have (a comma, for example) is not sent at all, and stays in the box to edit.

INIT B, the second INIT page: type the zero fuel weight and press Alt+2 (right line-select key 2).
The take-off weight on right line-select key 3 is worked out by the FMS; typing one there gets NOT
ALLOWED. The MCDU's own SimBrief request (Menu, then ACARS) loads the route, alternate, cruise
level, flight number, cost index and winds, but never the weights, so the zero fuel weight is
always yours to type.


THE TABLET - Press left bracket, then Shift+T

The aircraft's own tablet, read as a web page: headings, text, buttons, fields, choices and
sliders, in the order the tablet shows them. It starts on the lock screen: press Unlock, then pick
an app. Home is the last item on every page. The apps that matter for a flight:

  My Flight            Import from SimBrief, the route, METARs, times, checklist and flight plan
  Ground Equipment     Doors (each says open, moving or closed), slides (armed or disarmed),
                       chocks, covers, stairs, ground power, luggage, pushback, close all doors
  Weight and Balance   The cargo and fuel: see below
  Take Off / Landing   Performance: runway, conditions and configuration, then Calculate
  Settings             SimBrief (your SimBrief Pilot ID: the number on your SimBrief account
                       page, not your user name), MCDU export, IRS align time, weight units
                       and more
  Aircraft Maintenance Tires, brakes, oil, hydraulics, slides and the maintenance panels

Weight and Balance has two pages, and the window's title names the one you are on. Each page
starts with a line saying what its buttons do, then a line saying what is on board now: the
payload and the fuel, or "fueling in progress" while the tablet fills the tanks. That line is
spoken when it changes, so you hear the load arrive after Apply.

  Payload selection    The load buttons, each with its cargo and weight. A load fills in the
                       three hold weights and opens the loading page. Custom cargo opens the
                       loading page with the weights as they are; Cargo from SimBrief fills them
                       from your latest SimBrief plan. Nothing goes on board yet. Unload Cargo
                       only removes the cargo you can see in the hold: the weight stays on board
                       until you set the holds to 0 and apply. Loading page goes back to the
                       loading page once you have chosen a load.
  Loading              Total cargo, Total fuel and the forward, middle and aft holds. Update
                       from SimBrief fills in the cargo and fuel from your latest SimBrief plan.
                       Apply Load to Aircraft puts the cargo and fuel on board. The fuel goes
                       in at the speed of Maintenance Mode in Settings: Disabled loads it at
                       once, Fast at about 15 tonnes a minute, and Realistic (the default) at
                       about 2 tonnes a minute, so a full load can take half an hour. Then the
                       results: center of gravity in percent MAC, zero fuel weight, gross
                       weight, payload and block fuel, in tonnes (or thousands of pounds). Back
                       to payload selection returns to the loads.

Messages the tablet shows ("Payload Removed", a SimBrief error, "Unable to fuel aircraft when
engines are running.") are spoken as they appear.

A button whose label changes when you press it (a door, a Take Off setting) says its new label.
Password fields (Hoppie, SayIntentions) only say whether they are set. The Charts and Enroute Map
apps are pictures and read as little more than their buttons.

BACKGROUND ANNOUNCEMENTS - Ctrl+M

The master warning and master caution lights are announced when they come on ("Master
warning"). Pressing the light to acknowledge it is not spoken back.

Fault and warning lights are announced both ways: "Engine 1 generator fault light off", "Engine 1
fire handle light on". They include the fire loops and handles, generators, the battery charge
lights, hydraulic pumps, spoilers, pitch feel, rudder travel limiters, flap and slat systems, packs
and isolation valves, cabin pressure, anti-ice, window heat, the APU, the engine master switches
and oil pressure, autoland, GPWS, below glide slope, the MCDU message light, ECAM clear and the
cockpit door. The Electrical panel adds external power available, AC bus 1 and 2 off, AC
essential bus off, AC emergency bus on inverter, DC normal bus off, DC essential bus on battery,
IDG 1 and 2 disconnected, and override supply 1 and 2 flow. The IRS panel adds each IRS's align,
on battery, battery fault and fault lights. The APU panel adds APU available, APU start on, APU
starting and APU fuel shutoff valve open. More panels are being added. A
switch's own OFF or ON legend is not read as a light: the switch already says its position.
Lights that change together are read as one sentence, about a second and a half later, so an
engine start sounds like "3 lights off: Spoiler 7 fault, Spoiler 6 fault, Spoiler 5 fault". The
annunciator light test does not trigger these announcements.

A light is announced only as the cockpit shows it: lit needs its fault and its electrical power.
So switching power on reads the lights that come on, switching it off reads the lit ones going
out, and a fault that sets or clears while its light has no power says nothing. After power
changes, the lights wait about 2.5 seconds for the systems to settle, then are read once: a light
that flickers on and back off during the change is not read at all. The autobrake
lights are announced the same way. Each light has its own row in Ctrl+M; "Autobrake armed lights"
covers the three autobrake armed lights.

Take-off: "V1", "Rotate" and "V2" as the airspeed reaches them on the take-off roll. V1 and VR
come from the MCDU's TAKE OFF page. V2 is the FCU speed, which the A300 crew sets to V2 for
take-off, so set the FCU speed to V2 before you go: it is called only when it is no more than
30 knots above VR (with the window left at 250 there is no V2 call). Entering V1 or VR on the
MCDU is spoken ("V1: 148 knots"), and so is the FMS clearing them ("V1: not set"), which it does
only when it resets. V2 is not spoken as you set it: the FCU speed read-back already says it. The
V1, VR and V2 rows in Ctrl+M each turn off that speed's entry read-back and its call.

The autopilot and autothrottle: "Autopilot disconnected", "Autopilot CMD 1" (or CMD 2, Dual),
"Autothrottle disconnected" and "Autothrottle armed" when something other than MSFS Blind Assist
changes them - a yoke or hardware button, the instinctive disconnect, the aircraft itself. When
you switch them with MSFS Blind Assist, only your screen reader or the hotkey's read-back speaks.

The FCU altitude window is announced when it changes and you did not set it from MSFS Blind
Assist, for example with a hardware knob ("Altitude 12,000 feet"). The heading, speed and
vertical speed windows are not announced on their own, because the autopilot moves them itself
(heading sync, managed speed); read them with Shift+H, Shift+S and Shift+V in output mode.

"Glideslope alive" and "Glideslope lost" follow NAV 3, the A300's ILS receiver: tune the ILS
frequency on the Navigation Radios panel or with Ctrl+N. An ILS tuned on NAV 1 alone (the VOR 1
radio) gives no call-out.

The flaps lever, the ground spoiler arm, the gear lever and the parking brake are announced when
something other than MSFS Blind Assist moves them - a hardware lever, or the aircraft itself
("Flaps 15/15", "Parking brake set"). Changes you make in MSFS Blind Assist are not. The same
goes for the pitch trim and yaw damper levers on the SAS Control panel, which the aircraft drops
by itself when their system cannot hold them: "Pitch trim 1 off". Each has its own Ctrl+M row.

Each of these has its own Ctrl+M row: every light, V1 call-out, Rotate call-out, V2 call-out,
Autopilot engagement, Autothrottle engagement, Altitude window, and the flight mode annunciator's
rows.

Ctrl+M opens the monitor manager: type in the search box to narrow the list, use the Show filter
to see only the muted rows, and untick an item to stop it announcing. Your choices are
remembered between sessions.
