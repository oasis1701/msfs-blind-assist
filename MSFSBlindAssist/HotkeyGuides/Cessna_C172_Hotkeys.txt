CESSNA 172 SKYHAWK HOTKEY REFERENCE

Covers the Cessna 172 Skyhawk G1000, which flies with the Garmin GFC 700
built into the G1000. The C172 has no autothrottle, no airliner FCU and no
MCDU, so those sections of the airliner guides do not apply here.

Before the first flight of a session: MSFS can park the aircraft with
its engine, pitot and static-port covers on, and the engine will not start
with its cover in. Remove them on the Covers panel (Walkaround section).

Application Navigation:
  Ctrl+1     Focus Sections List
  Ctrl+2     Focus Panels List
  Ctrl+3     Focus Status Display field of the current panel (refreshes it if empty)

Tip: Press the same bracket key again to cancel a mode you activated by accident.

OUTPUT MODE - Press right bracket to activate

Altitude:
  Q          Read Altitude AGL
  A          Read Altitude MSL

Airspeed:
  S          Read IAS (Indicated Airspeed)
  T          Read TAS (True Airspeed)
  G          Read Ground Speed
  M          Read Mach Number

Heading:
  H          Read Magnetic Heading
  U          Read True Heading

Navigation:
  N          Read NAV 1 and NAV 2 radio info (frequency, ident, localizer, glideslope, DME)

Autopilot (the selected values, each followed by its mode when that mode is
active, for example "Heading bug 270, HDG active"):
  Shift+A    Selected altitude (and "ALT hold active")
  Shift+H    Heading bug (and "HDG active")
  Shift+S    Selected airspeed, the FLC target (and "FLC active")
  Shift+V    Selected vertical speed, signed (and "VS active")

Engine:
  P          RPM, with the arc it sits in on the G1000's tachometer (the green
             arc tops out at 2500, 2600 or 2700 rpm by altitude; red from 2700)
  E          Power: RPM, fuel flow, throttle and mixture
  Shift+P    Engine temperatures: oil, EGT and CHT
  Alt+S      The engine page in a window that updates live: RPM, fuel flow,
             oil pressure and temperature, EGT, CHT, vacuum, both tanks,
             engine hours, and the main and essential bus volts. Arrow
             through it; Escape closes it.
  Each reading says the zone the G1000 draws it in ("66 psi, green"). Where
  a reading is outside the green with a red or yellow arc further out,
  it says "below the green arc" or "above the green arc"; otherwise just
  the number (idle RPM, cold oil, a full tank).

Fuel:
  F          Read fuel per tank and total, in gallons
  Shift+F    Read fuel per tank and total, fuel flow, and endurance at the
             current flow

Weight:
  Shift+W    Read Gross Weight in kilograms and pounds, and the maximum

Flaps:
  L          Read Flap Position (Up, 10 degrees, 20 degrees, Full: 30 degrees)

Landing Gear:
  Shift+G    Says the gear is fixed

Altimeter:
  B          Read Altimeter Setting (inches, then hectopascals)

Vertical:
  V          Read Vertical Speed
  Ctrl+Shift+R  Read last landing rate (touchdown vertical speed, feet per minute)
  Ctrl+Shift+G  Read last landing peak g-force

Weather:
  I          Wind Info
  O          Read Outside Temperature (Celsius)

Time:
  Z          Read Local Time at aircraft position
  Shift+Z    Read Zulu / UTC Time

Transponder:
  X          Read Squawk Code

Location:
  C          Read Nearest City

Tools:
  Ctrl+H     Activate/deactivate Hand Fly mode
  Ctrl+V     Activate/deactivate Visual Guidance mode
  Ctrl+T     Activate/deactivate Takeoff Assistance
  Ctrl+D     Read Distance to Selected Runway
  Ctrl+I     Read ILS Guidance System Report

Taxi Guidance (turn-by-turn taxi assistance with audio steering tone):
  Y          Taxi Status (current taxiway, next turn, distance to destination)
  Ctrl+Y     Repeat Current Taxi Instruction
  Alt+Y      Where Am I (announces current taxiway, gate, or runway at nearest airport)
  Alt+L      Look Around (where you are, the apron or concourse you are in, and the nearest buildings with direction and distance)
  Ctrl+Shift+L  Surroundings window (everything nearby as a list, with the airport's fuel and frequencies)
                In its Frequencies list, Enter tunes the selected frequency into COM 1 standby
                and Shift+Enter tunes it straight into COM 1 active; COM 1 is read back to confirm.
  (Open the form with Input mode > Shift+Y)

Waypoint Tracking (use Fix Track window or EFB route viewer to add):
  1          Track Slot 1
  2          Track Slot 2
  3          Track Slot 3
  4          Track Slot 4
  5          Track Slot 5

Traffic and Weather:
  R          Announce Tracked TCAS Traffic
  Ctrl+R     Open TCAS Traffic Window
  Shift+O    Open Volanta window (Volanta users and network pilots around you,
             your Volanta friends and online ATC; also Extras > Volanta)
  Shift+R    Open Weather Radar Window
  Alt+G      Announce nearest ground traffic

Displays and Windows:
  Ctrl+M     Cessna 172 Monitor Manager - a checklist of what the C172
             announces by itself through MSFS Blind Assist (the switches, the
             RPM redline, the stall warning, the autopilot and flight director
             going off, the covers, the audio panel keys). Untick any you don't want spoken; the
             choice is saved. Readout hotkeys are unaffected. The G1000's own
             warnings and autopilot modes are not in it (see below).
  Shift+E    Route viewer, procedure planner and airport lookup
  Shift+L    Location Info
  Shift+M    Show METAR Report
  Ctrl+Shift+T  Cold Temperature Altitude Correction (offline calculator: field elevation + temperature + published altitudes -> corrected altitudes)
  Shift+B    SimBrief Briefing
  Alt+D      Describe outside scene (AI)

The G1000 windows:
  Alt+P      G1000 PFD window
  Alt+M      G1000 MFD window (Alt+N and input mode Shift+M open it too)
  The windows read the G1000's screens live and press its bezel keys, the
  same windows as the COWS DA40's. Read with the arrow keys; the window's own
  description lists its keys.
  The G1000's warnings and cautions (OIL PRESSURE, LOW VOLTS, STBY BATT,
  LOW VACUUM, LOW FUEL L and R) are spoken as they appear on the PFD and
  when they clear, window open or not, and so is the autopilot status line
  (the active and armed modes). When the master goes off nothing is
  announced; when it comes back on, every warning the G1000 shows is spoken.

Hand Fly Mode Hotkeys:
  A          Read MSL
  S          Read IAS
  D          Read Distance to Destination Runway
  H          Read Magnetic Heading
  P          Read Aircraft Pitch
  B          Read Aircraft Bank
  Q          Read AGL
  V          Read VS

Visual Guidance Mode Hotkeys (same single-letter set as Hand Fly mode, plus F):
  F          Read Target VS

Ground Services:
  Ctrl+G     Read latest GSX tooltip (no need to open the Access GSX window)

SayIntentions:
  Ctrl+S        Read last SayIntentions transmission
  Ctrl+Shift+S  Open SayIntentions flight information

VATSIM:
  Alt+V      Mute or unmute VATSIM announcements (vPilot)

INPUT MODE - Press [ to activate

Teleport:
  Shift+R    Runway Teleport
  Shift+G    Gate Teleport

Flight Planning:
  Shift+F    Fix/Waypoint Tracking Window
  Shift+D    Select Destination Runway

Autopilot:
  Shift+A    Autopilot on or off
  Shift+1    Heading mode on or off
  Shift+P    Approach mode on or off
  Shift+3    Flight level change (FLC) on or off
  Ctrl+H     Set the heading bug (0 to 359)
  Ctrl+A     Set the selected altitude (0 to 25000 feet), which FLC climbs or
             descends to; FLC is refused until an altitude has been selected.
  Ctrl+V     Set the autopilot vertical speed (-2000 to 2000 feet per minute)
  These value windows stay open after Enter, so you can type the next value
  straight away; Escape closes them.
  The mode keys press the GFC 700's own keys, so each behaves as in the
  cockpit: the first AP press engages the autopilot and flight director in
  roll and pitch hold, and pressing a mode key again turns that mode off.
  The GFC 700 panel (Autopilot section) has a button for every key:
  Autopilot, Flight Director, HDG Hold, NAV Mode, APR Mode, Back Course,
  ALT Hold, VS Mode, FLC Mode, VNAV, Nose Up and Nose Down. Nose Up and Nose
  Down change the target of the vertical mode and say the new one: the
  vertical speed in VS mode, the airspeed in FLC, and the pitch in pitch
  hold (the mode the autopilot starts in). In ALT Hold they have no effect,
  and say so. Heading Sync moves the bug to the current heading, and Course
  sets the course of whatever the CDI is on (GPS, NAV 1 or NAV 2).

Altimeter:
  Ctrl+B     Set the altimeter (28.00 to 31.00 inches)

Taxi Guidance:
  Shift+Y    Open Taxi Assist Form (enter ATC taxi clearance, start guidance)
  Y          Continue past current hold-short point
  Ctrl+Y     Stop Taxi Guidance
  Shift+X    Open Landing Exit Planner

Ground Services:
  Alt+G      Open Access GSX window (accessible GSX Ground Services Pro menu)

SayIntentions:
  Ctrl+Shift+Y Build a taxi route from the last SayIntentions taxi clearance.

General:
  Escape     Exit active hotkey mode

Automatic Announcements:
  - The G1000's warnings and cautions as they appear and clear on the PFD:
    OIL PRESSURE, LOW VOLTS, STBY BATT, LOW VACUUM, LOW FUEL L, LOW FUEL R
  - The autopilot status line (the active and armed modes) as it changes,
    and the autopilot or flight director going off
  - Engine RPM above 2700
  - Any switch moved outside MSFS Blind Assist (from hardware, the sim's own
    keys or its checklist): battery, alternator, avionics, magneto, fuel
    pump, selector and valve, lights, pitot heat, the parking brake (set and
    released), the flap lever, the standby battery, alternate static air
    and the ELT. Your own picks in the panels are not repeated.
  - Stall warning, on and off
  - A walkaround cover installed or removed
  - A circuit breaker that pops or goes back in by itself
  - An audio panel key changed outside MSFSBA (your own picks are not repeated)
  - Pressing Starter with the engine cover still installed says so
  The RPM warning speaks once and re-arms when the RPM comes back down.
  Mute any of these from the Monitor Manager (Ctrl+M), except the G1000's
  warnings and the autopilot status line.
  The trim announcement (Shift+T) stays quiet while the autopilot is flying,
  because the autopilot trims all the time; your own trim is spoken again
  once it disconnects.
