# Cessna 172 Skyhawk

> **Read this when:** working on the Cessna 172 Skyhawk G1000 (the steam-gauge Classic was removed until it is measured).

## Where it came from

Marshall Fairbrother wrote the C172 on his fork (`MarshallF03/msfs-blind-assist`); his last C172 commit was on 2026-06-01, on that fork's `build/citation-longitude` branch. It was brought over on 2026-10-16, on top of the COWS DA40 (#242), with his definitions unchanged apart from the API rename (`GetVariables()` became `BuildVariables()`) and the fixes listed under each section below. The C172 builds on #242 because its G1000 is flown through #242's G1000 windows: without them it is not flyable, so it follows #242 into `main`.

His G1000 NXi flight-plan navigator was **not** brought over: a Coherent GT client (`CoherentGTClient`, `G1000FmsClient`), the `G1000NavigatorForm` window on Shift+M, and a SimBrief route loader built on them. The shared G1000 work from the COWS DA40 (#242) replaces it.

The same day the G1000 was audited live, control by control, on MSFS 2024's stock "C172SP G1000" (below, and the rules in `docs/invariants/c172.md`). Most of the port's G1000 controls read the wrong variable or wrote an event this cockpit ignores; they were rebuilt on the measurements.

The port turned out to be written for FS2020 as well as unfinished, so on 2026-10-18 (the owner's decision) everything unmeasured came out: the steam-gauge Classic variant (`CessnaC172ClassicDefinition`, its menu item and its KAP140 panel), the K: event map it wrote every switch through, 20 definitions nothing used (the toggle and set events of every switch, two readouts nobody asked for), and the G1000's annunciator test, a Classic switch this cockpit does not have (it is not in the input-event catalog). The base class now holds the names the G1000 was measured through. The Classic is to come back built from measurements like the G1000.

## The G1000 windows

The G1000 opens the COWS DA40's `Forms/DA40/CowsDA40DisplayForm` on the same views (`AS1000_PFD`, `AS1000_MFD`): the stock Working Title G1000 is the same instrument in both aircraft (the DA40's G1000 agent also read the C172's CAS and FMA during the audit). `CessnaC172G1000Definition.HandleHotkeyAction` takes the DA40's keys: Alt+P for the PFD; Alt+M, Alt+N and input-mode Shift+M for the MFD (`CowsDA40Definition.OpensMfdWindow`). The windows are tracked (`ShowTrackedWindow`), so a second press focuses the open one and an aircraft switch closes them.

The C172 is the windows' owner (`G1000.IG1000DisplayOwner`), as the DA40 is: the PFD window takes the CAS watcher's socket while it is open and feeds it the rows it reads, so the CAS keeps speaking (below). The display units and radio-tune notes it also hands its owner are DA40 needs, and no-ops here. Until 2026-10-18 no owner was passed, and the CAS was listed in the PFD window but never announced. Marshall's navigator worked differently: it drove the flight plan through the FMS's own API with buttons of its own (insert waypoint, "Set up ILS"); this window reads the screens and presses the real bezel keys, as a sighted pilot operates the G1000.

## Transport

Over SimConnect only: no MobiFlight, no WASM. The G1000 writes cockpit **input events** (B: events) with an explicit value, the way the cockpit switch does (C172-2): `SwitchInputEvents` for the switches, `AutopilotKeys` for the GFC 700's bezel keys, `CoverInputEvents` for the covers. Four of its states are READ from the input event too, through the shared input-event subscription (SIM-24): both avionics buses, the standby battery and the magneto key, which no SimVar reports. The input-event catalog arrives a few seconds after the aircraft loads; a write before then is refused aloud.

The panels were tidied on 2026-10-18, at the owner's request. Battery and Alternator now has the standby battery first, then the master and the alternator. That is the start checklist's order (STBY BATT to ARM, then the master), and the switch sits left of the master. Fuel has the fuel pump first, then the selector and the shutoff valve. Pitot-Static (it was "Anti-Ice") has pitot heat and the alternate static source, the POH's pitot-static system and the DA40's Ice and Pitot pairing. Flaps and Brake has the flap lever as one combo; its Flaps Down and Flaps Up buttons only repeated it. Wherever the sim numbers a two-position control's resting place last, its combo lists that place first (`RestingPositionFirst`), so the preflight works down the list: the standby battery reads Off then Arm, the ELT Arm then On, and each cover Installed then Removed. The A380's chocks and cones and the PMDG 777's chocks still list Removed first.

Engine Controls has the magneto key, the Starter and the Mixture, one combo for the red push-pull knob in whole percent: Idle cutoff (pulled fully out, how the engine is shut down), then 1 to 99 percent, then Full rich (pushed fully in). The arrows move it a percent, Home and End go to the two ends, and typing a number jumps to it. Home is idle cutoff, which stops a running engine. A lever between two percents shows the nearer one. MSFSBA says nothing on a pick: the combo is the confirmation. A slider came first. The owner chose the combo, because the slider said "100" for its end instead of the knob's name, and the accessible-object override tried to fix that left NVDA silent on the row (2026-10-18). It is written with `MIXTURE1_SET` (0 to 16383) and read from `GENERAL ENG MIXTURE LEVER POSITION:1` when the panel opens. A lever, so it is never announced (C172-20). It replaced four rows: a Mixture button that did nothing, a typed field and the Full Rich and Full Lean buttons.

The GFC 700 panel has a button for each key on the G1000 bezel: Autopilot (AP), Flight Director, HDG, NAV, APR, Back Course, ALT, VS, FLC, VNAV, Nose Up and Nose Down, each pressing the real key (C172-3). Then come typed Heading Bug, Selected Altitude, Selected Vertical Speed and FLC Speed fields, as on the DA40's GFC 700 panel (where the last is "Selected Airspeed": the C172 names it for the mode, C172-21). Each refuses and writes exactly as its input-mode window does (Ctrl+H, A, V, S; `TypedAutopilotValues`), text that is not a number included. Until 2026-10-18 those four rows were the value reads, which the panel drew as buttons that did nothing; the reads stay for output Shift+H, A, V and S. A mode button shows its state. Nose Up and Nose Down say the target they moved, interrupting the last one so a run of presses reads fast: the VS target in VS, the FLC speed in FLC ("FLC speed 80 knots", never a bare "Airspeed", which a press makes easy to hear as the current one), the pitch in PIT (the mode the autopilot engages in, read from `AUTOPILOT PITCH HOLD REF`, negative for nose up); in ALT they move nothing in this sim (measured: the FMA's held altitude stayed put), and say so. VNAV, which has no state to read, says "VNAV pressed".

Input Ctrl+P opens the GFC 700 window: the panel's buttons, in its order (`AutopilotWindowRows`, so a button added to the panel joins it), without the typed values, which have their own keys. It is the shared window the PMDG 737 and 777 use for theirs (`PMDGAutopilotWindow`), with an Alt-key letter for each button (`AutopilotWindowMnemonics`). A mode button shows the panel's state, read from the cache, and nothing rather than a guessed Off before the first batch; every press goes through `HandleUIVariableSet` as the panel button's does, so what the panel says after a press, the window says too. Unlike the PMDG window it marks no UI echo, as the C172's panel marks none, so the master's and flight director's OFF stay spoken.

Two bezel knobs are there too. Heading Sync is the HDG knob's push: it moves the bug to the current heading and says where it went ("Heading bug 039"). Course is the CRS knob, typed: it sets the course of whatever the CDI is on, GPS, NAV 1 or NAV 2, read at the moment of the write (C172-11), and says "Course 090". A course outside 0 to 360, or text that is not a number, is refused aloud; 0 and 360 are both north.

The Audio panel (Radios) is the GMA 1347 between the two screens, which neither G1000 window reaches. Its keys are named as printed: Transmit Radio (the COM1 MIC and COM2 MIC keys), COM1 and COM2 (receive), NAV1, NAV2, DME and ADF (ident audio), MKR/MUTE (the marker mute), HI SENS (marker sensitivity) and SPKR, each Off or On; the PILOT and COPLT intercom keys, buttons that say what their lamp did ("PILOT: On"), with both lamps in the panel's status list; and the COM1, COM2, NAV1 and NAV2 volumes, sliders in percent. Every key is a push that toggles, so each is pushed only when the sim says it is not already where the pilot asked (C172-10). The transmitting radio is always heard, so turning its receive off says so instead. Picking COM 2 to transmit can move the receive keys too; those knock-ons are not spoken (C172-12). Left out because they moved nothing readable: COM 1/2, TEL, PA, AUX, MAN SQ, PLAY, the panel's own volume and DISPLAY BACKUP; COM3 MIC and COM3 have no third radio.

The COM and NAV panels (Radios) tune by typing, as the DA40's Radios panel does, since the PFD's knobs move a frequency one step at a time: each radio has a typed Standby field ("COM 1 standby 121.705") and a Swap button ("COM 1 swapped"), and the panel's status list shows every active and standby frequency. COM takes 118.000 to 136.990 with 8.33 kHz channels, NAV 108.00 to 117.95; anything else is refused aloud (C172-14). The Transponder panel has the Squawk field (four digits, 0 to 7, "Squawk set to 2300", C172-13), the Transponder Mode combo (Standby, On, Altitude, Ground; it reads Off when unpowered) and an Ident button, which says "Ident", or why not: in Standby the transponder cannot ident (C172-15). Its status list shows the squawk. These replaced Marshall's FS2020 panels on 2026-10-18, which could not set a frequency and set the wrong squawk.

The Covers panel lists the engine, pitot and static-port covers, Installed or Removed (C172-4).

The Oil Check panel stands in for the walkaround's dipstick check (the owner asked for it for what it teaches): no cockpit instrument shows the oil quantity, only its pressure and temperature. Its status list reads "Oil Quantity: 8 quarts", the sim's `ENG OIL QUANTITY:1` (a percentage of full, 100 on a fresh flight) as quarts of the C172S's 8-quart sump to the nearest half quart, and adds "below the 5 quart minimum" under the POH's minimum for flight. That 100 percent is a full sump is the natural reading, not a measurement: the sim's quarts unit for this variable reads nonsense (1056).

The Circuit Breakers section has the cockpit's 30 breakers, each In or Pulled, on four panels by the bus each was measured on (C172-7). What pulling each one did, engine off, with the loads switched on:

| Panel | Breaker (catalog name) | Pulled, it cut |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Essential Bus | PFD (`ESS_PFD`), ADC (`ESS_ADC`), Nav 1 (`ESS_NAV1`) | nothing alone: each is also fed through avionics bus 1; both pulled cut circuits 3, 6 and 35 |
| Essential Bus | Com 1 (`ESS_COMM1`) | circuit 37 |
| Essential Bus | Standby Indicator (`ESS_STBY_IND`) | circuit 5 |
| Essential Bus | Standby Battery (`ESS_STBY_BATT`) | the whole essential bus, with the standby on ARM and the master off |
| Avionics Bus 1 | Avionics Bus 1 (`AVN1`), PFD, ADC, Nav 1 | nothing alone: every avionics bus 1 load is also fed from the essential bus |
| Avionics Bus 1 | FIS (`AVN1_FIS`) | circuit 7 |
| Avionics Bus 2 | Avionics Bus 2 (`AVN2`) | the whole bus: circuits 4, 32, 33, 34, 36, 38, 39, 40, 41 |
| Avionics Bus 2 | MFD (`AVN2_MDF`, the catalog's spelling), Nav 2, Com 2, Audio Panel, Autopilot, Transponder (`XPNDR`) | circuits 4, 36, 38, 39, 40, 33 |
| Systems and Lights | Nav Lights, Beacon, Landing Light, Taxi Light (`RECOG`), Strobe Lights | circuits 14-16, 17, 18, 19, 20-21 |
| Systems and Lights | Lights Power (`LTS_PWR`), Warning (`WARN`), Fuel Pump | circuits 28-29, 42, 9 |
| Systems and Lights | Flaps, Pitot Heat | flagged pulled (circuits 11, 12); neither circuit was running to cut |
| Systems and Lights | Alternator Field (`ALT_FLD`), Panel Lights (`PANELS_LTS`) | nothing seen: the engine was off, and the panel lights' dimmer was down |

`RECOG` is spoken as the taxi light because the circuit it cut is the one the taxi light switch lit. A breaker that pops by itself (or goes back in) is spoken, "Fuel Pump breaker popped"; the pilot's own pulls are not (C172-7).

The Cabin Controls panel has cabin heat and cabin air in quarter steps, Closed to Open (percent in and out over SimConnect; the Coherent debugger shows the same lever as a fraction, which misled the first build, C172-9), and the pilot's and passenger's windows, Open or Closed, read from where each window is. The cabin door is not there and must never be added (C172-8).

The ELT panel has the ELT switch, Arm or On. On transmits (`ELT ACTIVATED`), and Arm is where it rests; going back to Arm stops a transmission, which is how the real ELT is reset. The ELT going off by itself, as a hard landing trips a real one, is spoken, "ELT activated", and so is its reset afterwards, "ELT stopped transmitting" (C172-26).

On the G1000 the Starter (Engine Controls) turns the key to START, which cranks for about 3 seconds and springs back to BOTH by itself; a cold engine that needs longer is pressed again. It cannot be held longer: nothing MSFSBA can send keeps the key on START past its spring-back, and a crank kept going by re-pressing cuts out every 3 seconds (C172-6). It says "Engine cover installed" first if it is.

## The G1000's warnings (CAS) and the FMA

The C172 has no annunciator lamps. Every warning and caution is a CAS message on the PFD, and the aircraft's own panel.xml (read live from the PFD's `xmlConfig` on 2026-10-18; the stock package itself is an encrypted `.fsarchive`) defines seven:

| Message | Kind | Shown when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OIL PRESSURE | warning | oil pressure at or below 20 psi, with `ELECTRICAL MASTER BATTERY` on (it reads 1 even cold and dark, so in practice whenever the screen is lit) |
| LOW VOLTS | warning | the main bus (`ELECTRICAL BUS VOLTAGE:'CROSS_FEED_BUS'_n`) at or below 25.5 V |
| HIGH VOLTS | warning | `ELECTRICAL MAIN BUS VOLTAGE:'CROSS_FEED_BUS'_n` above 32 V; that SimVar reads 0, so it never shows in this sim (C172-16) |
| STBY BATT | caution | the standby battery's master on and the main battery's off |
| LOW VACUUM | caution | suction below 3.5 inHg (it reads 4.3 at idle: the standby attitude indicator runs on vacuum) |
| LOW FUEL L, LOW FUEL R | caution | that tank below 5 gallons |

Measured on the PFD: the master on with the engine stopped shows LOW VOLTS, OIL PRESSURE and LOW VACUUM; the standby battery alone (ARM, master off) adds STBY BATT; the dark screen shows nothing. So a cold start reads: standby to ARM, "STBY BATT"; master on, "LOW VOLTS, OIL PRESSURE, LOW VACUUM"; engine running, "OIL PRESSURE cleared" and "LOW VACUUM cleared"; the alternator charging, "LOW VOLTS cleared". The same list is where the steam-gauge Classic's annunciator lamps will be checked from, when it is rebuilt.

They are spoken by the CAS watcher the COWS DA40 uses (`G1000CasWatcher`, C172-17), off the PFD, whether or not the PFD window is open: a message that appears interrupts, several in one breath, and one that clears is queued behind ("OIL PRESSURE cleared") once it has stayed gone 4 seconds, so a message flickering on its limit is said once and a real clear comes a few seconds after it (DA40G-32). The first reading after connecting and the quiet period after a flight load or a dropped connection are recorded, not spoken. That quiet period is the DA40's (`DA40LoadSettle`): it ends after five quiet seconds, or 30 seconds at most, and with the engine running the temperatures and hour meter change every second, so it always runs the full 30. A screen going dark says nothing (DA40G-31), and when it comes back every message it raises is spoken. The port's own low fuel, oil pressure and vacuum warnings, computed from SimVars, were removed: their thresholds matched the panel.xml, but the oil one was silent below 1 psi, so a cold start said nothing, and beside the CAS they would speak twice.

The watcher also speaks the FMA, the autopilot status line: "Autopilot ROL, PIT", "Autopilot HDG, ALT", "Autopilot GPS, VS, ALTS armed", "Flight director ..." when the director is up without the autopilot. That includes the ARMED modes (ALTS, GS, a NAV source), which no SimVar carries. So the GFC 700 mode rows are quiet, as on the DA40 (DA40A-22): a mode button press is confirmed by the FMA, up to one poll (1.5 s) later. The autopilot master and the flight director still say their own "Off", which the FMA does not.

## Engine, fuel and electrical readouts

Every reading is the G1000 engine page's, said with the zone its gauge draws it in, from the aircraft's own panel.xml (C172-18): "66 psi, green", "2600 rpm, above the green arc", "1.5 gallons, yellow, low". Outside the green, "below the green arc" or "above the green arc" is said only when a red or yellow arc lies further out on that side; otherwise the reading is just the number (idle RPM, cold oil, a full tank). The tachometer's green arc tops out at 2500, 2600 or 2700 rpm by altitude, as the gauge's own state machine does.

- **Keys (output mode):** P, the RPM; E, the power: RPM, fuel flow, and the throttle and mixture percent (read fresh; the mixture's ends are said by name, "Mixture full rich", "Mixture idle cutoff"); Shift+P, the oil temperature, EGT and CHT; Alt+S, the engine page in a window that updates every second (the DA40's `CowsDA40EngineGlanceForm`, fed the C172's rows): RPM, fuel flow, oil pressure and temperature, EGT, CHT, vacuum, both tanks, engine hours, the main and essential buses. It closes on Escape and on an aircraft switch. F and Shift+F read the tanks, and the flow and endurance, as before, now with a dot in every culture.
- **Panels:** Engine Controls lists RPM, fuel flow, oil pressure, oil temperature, EGT, CHT and vacuum; Fuel the two tanks; Battery and Alternator the main bus, the essential bus and the main battery's voltage.
- **Left out:** the battery amps, whose SimVar cannot tell charging from discharging (C172-16), and the G1000's fuel calculator (gallons used and remaining), which reads 0 until the pilot sets the fuel on board on the MFD.
- **Refuelling (2026-10-19, C172-24):** the Fuel panel ends with Left Tank Fuel and Right Tank Fuel, typed in gallons, and Fill Both Tanks, the transaction the DA40 has, since the filler caps are on the wings. The number typed is the USABLE fuel (the owner's choice), the number the G1000's gauge and F say back: 0 to 26.5 a side, Fill Both Tanks 26.5 each, 53 in all, the POH's figures. Each tank holds 28 gallons, 1.5 of them unusable, and the write sets the total, so MSFSBA adds the 1.5. A load outside 0 to 26.5 is refused aloud, never clamped (the sim itself takes an overfill: 30 written read 107 percent). In the air, or with the propeller turning, it refuses ("Refuelling is only possible on the ground.", "Shut the engine down before refuelling."). The confirmation is the usable figure, "Left tank 20.0 gallons."; Fill Both Tanks says what F then reads. The gross weight (Shift+W) follows at 6 pounds a gallon.
- **The center of gravity on Shift+W (2026-10-19, C172-25):** after the weights, "Center of gravity 18.7 percent MAC, within the 15 to 35 percent limits" ("forward of the 15 percent limit", "aft of the 35 percent limit" outside them), read fresh: the flight model's own `CG PERCENT` against its own `CG FWD LIMIT` and `CG AFT LIMIT`, worded as the FBW aircraft's. Not inches, though the POH works in inches: `CG FEET` reads 0, and the sim's seats, baggage areas and tanks do not sit at the POH's arms, so no conversion would be honest.
- **The port's other keys, audited 2026-10-18 (C172-19):** L (flaps), B (altimeter) and Shift+V (the autopilot's vertical speed) read a cache that never held their values, so they said "Requesting ..." and nothing, or a stale value; they and Shift+W now read fresh. B says "Altimeter 29.92 inches, 1013 hectopascals" (no longer "standard"), Shift+W kilograms and pounds with the sim's maximum (2558 pounds), each as the DA40 does, and Shift+V (with the nose wheel's read-back) the target signed, "-500", as the A320 and iFly do. The flap notches, the alternator row (it follows the switch) and the parking brake's writes were measured right.

## Background announcements

Besides the CAS and the FMA (above), each of these speaks by itself:

- engine RPM above 2700, once, re-armed when it comes back down;
- every switch moved outside MSFSBA (C172-20): battery, alternator, avionics buses, magneto, fuel pump, selector and valve, the exterior lights, pitot heat, the parking brake ("Parking Brake: Set", "Released"), the flap lever ("Flaps: 10 degrees"), the standby battery, alternate static air and the ELT; not the Starter's own turn of the magneto key, nor the levers, dimmers and windows, which sweep;
- the stall warning, on and off;
- the autopilot and the flight director going off;
- a walkaround cover installed or removed;
- a circuit breaker that pops or goes back in by itself (no Ctrl+M row);
- an audio panel key changed outside MSFSBA ("NAV1: On", "Transmit Radio: COM 2"), each with its Ctrl+M row; not the intercom lamps, which have no row of their own;
- the transponder mode changed outside MSFSBA ("Transponder Mode: Altitude");
- "Ident off" when the transponder's ident ends by itself, about 18 seconds after the press (its start is said by the Ident button);
- the ELT going off by itself, "ELT activated", and its reset afterwards, "ELT stopped transmitting"; not the pilot's own ELT switch, which the switch already says (C172-26).

The autopilot's own trimming is not spoken: the shared Shift+T announcer re-bases silently while the autopilot is engaged, as the DA40 does, so the first pilot trim after a disconnect speaks only what the pilot moved.

Oil pressure, oil temperature, EGT, CHT, fuel flow, vacuum, both fuel quantities, the two buses, the hour meter and the battery voltage are read continuously for the status displays and the hotkeys, and never spoken by themselves (their warnings are the CAS's), so they carry `ExcludeFromMonitorManager` and have no Ctrl+M row (VAR-9, pinned by `CessnaC172MonitorRowsTests`). Nor do the autopilot modes the FMA speaks.

Ctrl+M opens `Forms/C172/CessnaC172MonitorManagerForm`, with its own list (`UserSettings.C172DisabledMonitorVariables`). The RPM redline is spoken from inside `ProcessSimVarUpdate`, so the C172 is in `Services/DefAnnounceMuteSets` and an unticked row mutes it through MainForm's `announcer.Suppressed` wrap (VAR-8). The CAS and FMA have no row, as on the DA40. The window is rebuilt on every aircraft switch.

## Hotkeys

The pilot's list is `HotkeyGuides/Cessna_C172_Hotkeys.txt`. It names only keys whose handler runs on the C172: the app-wide ones in MainForm, the base class's (local and Zulu time), and the C172's own in `HandleHotkeyAction` (fuel in gallons, the engine keys P, E, Shift+P and Alt+S, flaps, fixed gear, altimeter, gross weight, the autopilot's selected altitude, heading bug, FLC speed and vertical speed on output Shift+A, H, S and V, each with its mode when active (C172-21), Ctrl+M, and the heading bug, altitude, FLC speed (input Ctrl+S, 48 to 163 knots, C172-22), vertical speed and altimeter dialogs, each parsing a typed number the same way on any PC, CORE-17). On the G1000 the autopilot hotkeys (AP, HDG, FLC, APR) press the GFC 700's keys, and the altitude dialog uses the measured selection sequence (C172-3). The heading, altitude, vertical speed and altimeter dialogs stay open after Enter, so the next value can be typed at once, as on the other aircraft (`ShowValueDialog`); Escape closes them. They closed after one value until 2026-10-17. Shift+M in input mode opens the G1000 MFD window. The speed keys, and Ctrl+W, D, Shift+D, Alt+E, Alt+I and input Ctrl+N, have their own sections below.

## Speeds, take-off and landing (2026-10-18)

- **The speeds** (`CessnaC172Speeds`, C172-23) are the flight model's own, read live from the PFD's view with `Simplane.getDesignSpeeds()`: VS0 40, VS1 48, VFE 85, VNO 129, VNE 163, Vr 55, Vx 62, Vy 74, best glide 68 knots, each the 172S POH's figure. The flight model sets no approach speed (Vapp 0), manoeuvring speed or 10-degree flap limit, so those are the POH's: approach 60 to 70 knots full flap and 65 to 75 flaps up, short field 61, the 10-degree limit 110, manoeuvring 105 at 2550 lb, 98 at 2200 and 90 at 1900. The SimVars are not the same source: `DESIGN SPEED VFE` and `DESIGN APPROACH SPEED` read 0, and `AIRSPEED BARBER POLE` reads 160 where VNE is 163 (`DESIGN SPEED VS0`, `VS1`, `VC` 124, `MIN ROTATION` 55, `CLIMB` 74 and `DESIGN TAKEOFF SPEED` 60 do read). The panel.xml has no speed section: its EFB block lists only the take-off flaps (up, 10) and the landing flaps (up, 10, 20, full).
- **Output Shift+1 to 6** carry them on the keys the airliners use for their own speeds, as the DA40 does: Shift+5 the take-off set, Shift+4 the landing set, Shift+6 the limit for the flaps set now (read fresh), Shift+3 both flap limits, Shift+2 the airspeed limits slowest first, with the manoeuvring speed for the weight read now, Shift+1 best glide.
- **The take-off roll** says "Rotate" once at 55 knots indicated (`TakeoffRollCallouts`), in place of the airliners' 80 and 100 knots, which came after a C172 is flying.
- **Visual guidance and the landing assist** fly a light single's profile (docs/visual-guidance.md). Measured parked: the reference datum 3.9 ft above the ground on the wheeled aircraft ("C172SP G1000 Passengers"; 3.8 on skis, "... Passengers Skis", whose speeds are the same), the aircraft 2.25 degrees nose up on its wheels (`PLANE PITCH DEGREES` -2.25, nose up negative). The flare height (15 ft), flare pitch (4 degrees), sink law, tone ranges and rollout end (20 kt) are estimates, a light single's, until the owner flies a recorded landing (a read-only per-frame recorder of height, pitch, sink, speed and each gear leg's ground contact, run beside MSFSBA).
- **The taxi turn lead** is 0.8 s, estimated (docs/taxi-guidance.md), to be measured from a guided taxi's turns.

## Trim and throttle (2026-10-18)

Measured on the wheeled G1000, standing on the runway with the parking brake set and the engine idling, and put back as found (docs/invariants/c172-flight-controls.md):

- **Flight Controls → Elevator Trim** (`CessnaC172G1000Definition.FlightControls.cs`): the trim typed in degrees, positive nose up, -19 to 22, the travel's ends; Trim Nose Up and Trim Nose Down, half a degree a press, saying where the trim stopped and the limit when it is there; Takeoff Trim, which sets 3.0 degrees nose up, the take-off position; the Trim Disconnect switch, Connected or Disconnected; and the trim as a status row ("1.7 degrees nose up", "neutral", "3.0 degrees nose up, takeoff position"). Each write is `ELEVATOR_TRIM_SET`, 22 degrees per 16383 both ways (C172F-1), and the trim callout is re-based on it so the position is said once. The automatic trim callout ("Trim up 4.2", shared with every aircraft) still runs for the pilot's own trimming: the pilot's hardware trim is the yoke's electric trim switch, which runs the trim at 3.6 degrees a second.
- **The trim disconnect blocks every trim** in the sim, the hardware switch included, so a trim write while it is on is refused aloud, "The trim is disconnected." (C172F-2). Whether it also drops the autopilot is not measured.
- **The throttle** is a combo on Engine Controls, the last control, right before the status display (the owner's pick): Idle, 1 to 99 percent, Full, written by `THROTTLE1_SET` (C172F-3) and silent on a pick, as the mixture is. A hardware throttle moves it again on its next movement. Output+E says "Throttle idle" and "Throttle full" at the ends, as it names the mixture's.
- **Measured and left out:** the trim wheel's and the yoke switch's input events (HANDLING_ELEVATORTRIM_WHEEL only follows the trim, in percent over SimConnect; each HANDLING_ELEVATORTRIM_YOKE write is one 0.11 degree step in no reliable direction), the stock ELEV_TRIM_UP/DN (0.029 degree a step), and the throttle's input event ENGINE_THROTTLE_1 (a 0-1 fraction in, percent out: 20 written was full power).
- **The take-off trim is 3.0 degrees nose up.** The POH's take-off position is a mark on the wheel, with no number. A flight restarted on the runway with the trim untouched had it at exactly 3.0 (and the flaps at 10 degrees), the flight model's own `ELEVATOR TRIM NEUTRAL` (2026-10-18).

## Navigation and display keys (2026-10-18)

Six keys said nothing on the C172; each now answers, and the first four with the COWS DA40's own code (#242), moved for it into the shared `Aircraft/G1000/G1000Waypoints.cs` as the CAS watcher was:

- **Ctrl+W** the waypoint flown to, **D** the destination, **Shift+D** top of descent, and the automatic **waypoint-passing call** ("Passing SOXOM. Next VEKIN, 14 miles.", DA40A-15), with a "Waypoint Passing Call" row in Ctrl+M (`C172G_WAYPOINT_PASSING`). They read the stock GPS SimVars the G1000 writes (SimConnectManager's GPS waypoint frame, every aircraft), the leg names off the flight plan over the CAS watcher's PFD socket, and the G1000's VNAV L:vars, which exist on the C172 as on the DA40 (`WTAP_VNav_TOD_Leg_Index` -1 with no descent, measured parked 2026-10-18). Distances are in nautical miles: the C172 reads none of the G1000's unit settings. The passing call needs a flight to be heard (with the flight items at the end of the list).
- **Alt+E** the CAS messages standing now, from the watcher's last scrape (`G1000CasWatcher.DescribeCurrentMessages`): "3 CAS messages. WARNING: LOW VOLTS. ...", "No CAS messages.", "The PFD is off." It was dead on the DA40 too, although its hotkey guide listed it; the same change fixed it there.
- **Alt+I** the standby instruments (`DescribeStandbyInstruments`): the airspeed and altitude (the same pitot-static SimVars as the G1000's: every index read the same), the compass (`WISKEY COMPASS INDICATION DEGREES`, 133.6 parked against a heading of 133.6), the standby attitude indicator (`ATTITUDE INDICATOR PITCH DEGREES`/`BANK DEGREES`, equal to the aircraft's parked) and the vacuum the real indicator runs on. Pitch reads nose up negative, as SimConnectManager's pitch readout takes it; the bank is taken as left positive from its bank readout (VG-7), not yet flown. Whether the sim's indicator follows a failed vacuum is not measured.
- **Input Ctrl+N** both NAV standbys in one window with two fields (the owner's choice), each filled with what its radio holds: Enter sets both and keeps the window open; a field's own Set button sets that radio alone and closes it. Each field is checked against its band and written as the Radios panel writes it (C172-14).

## The First Officer's settings on the C172 (2026-10-20)

The C172 has no First Officer window (#160): Shift+C says "No checklist for this aircraft.", as on the HS787, the owner's choice for now. Two of the First Officer's settings act on every aircraft, though, once the pilot turns them on (both are off by default), and the C172 answers them in `CessnaC172G1000Definition.AutopilotEngage.cs` and the base class:

- **Auto gear** does nothing: the gear is fixed (`HasRetractableGear` false, FO-27), so no GEAR_DOWN is sent and no "Gear down." is said.
- **Auto autopilot** presses the GFC 700's own AP key (C172-3), which toggles, so only when AUTOPILOT MASTER reads definitely off, 3 s apart, and it says "Autopilot engaged" only once AUTOPILOT MASTER reads on (FO-18). It never presses below 800 ft AGL whatever the setting (FO-17): the GFC 700's limitation outside an approach, as the Civil Air Patrol's course for its G1000 Cessnas gives it; the Cessna supplement itself was not read. Not yet flown.

## Measured live (2026-10-16, G1000)

The audit's measurements are in `docs/invariants/c172.md`, one rule per finding. In short:

- **Electrical.** The main battery is index 2, the standby index 1, and a bare `TOGGLE_MASTER_BATTERY` flips both (C172-1). The standby switch is ARM (0) or OFF (1) and the flight starts OFF. The avionics bus switches are input events that `AVIONICS_MASTER_n_SET` never touches.
- **Engine and fuel.** The magneto key reads 0 Off, 1 Right, 2 Left, 3 Both, 4 Start; START springs back to BOTH 3.0 to 3.6 s in, and one press of the Starter started the engine first time (C172-6). The fuel pump, valve and selector are the new fuel system's (C172-2, C172-5); the flight starts with the selector on LEFT. The mixture's `MIXTURE1_SET` and the flaps' `FLAPS_INCR`/`FLAPS_DECR` were already right.
- **Lights and anti-ice.** The light SimVars with ON report the lamp, not the switch; pitot heat's switch is `PITOT HEAT SWITCH:1`.
- **Covers.** The cold-and-dark flight starts with the engine, pitot and static-port covers fitted, and the engine will not start with its cover in, autostart included (C172-4).
- **GFC 700.** Not a KAP140: the owner's cockpit picture shows the AP, FD, HDG, ALT, NAV, VNV, APR, BC, VS and FLC keys and nose wheel on both bezels, and the Coherent page list holds no KAP140 gauge. Each key and the value setters measured (C172-3).
- **The two fixes made at the port, now verified.** Ctrl+B: `KOHLSMAN_SET` 16320 set 1020 hPa / 30.12 inHg (Marshall had sent inches × 16). Ctrl+V: a descent sent as the 32-bit two's complement landed as −500 fpm (Marshall had sent 65536 plus the rate).

Marshall's 2026-05-31 note that NAV is "refused while heading hold is on" was the GFC 700 arming NAV, not a fault; his turn-HDG-off-first workaround was removed with the KAP140 modelling.

## The G1000 bezel, mapped (2026-10-17)

The aircraft's 134 `AS1000_*` input events (the PFD and MFD bezels, 55 each, and the 24 of the audio panel) were set one at a time over SimConnect with MSFSBA closed, a 20 ms Coherent recorder watching the input event and the SimVars it might drive, and each function checked against what MSFSBA can press:

- **Reached by the G1000 windows (Alt+P, Alt+M), checked on this aircraft:** the DA40's agent reads the C172's PFD and MFD in full (CAS, FMA, radios, transponder, softkeys, the MFD's startup screen). The softkeys go over SimConnect (the CDI softkey stepped GPS, LOC1, LOC2 and back), MENU and CLR over the socket (the MFD's page menu opened and closed). The FMS knobs and their push, ENT, Direct-To, FPL, PROC, the range knob, the radio knobs and their push and swap, and the baro knob use the same two roads.
- **Reached by the panels and hotkeys:** the twelve GFC 700 keys (C172-3); the ALT knob through the selected-altitude dialog; the HDG knob through the heading bug; and now the HDG knob's push, the CRS knob and the whole audio panel (above).
- **Nothing readable, so not offered:** the COM squelch push, the NAV ident push, the range (joystick) push and its zoom, and the audio panel keys listed above. The CRS knob's push left every course where it was with no station tuned.
- **The MFD's own radio knobs work on this aircraft** (`AS1000_COM_SMALL_MFD` and `H:AS1000_MFD_COM_Small_INC` tuned COM standby, the MFD NAV knob NAV 1's), but the shared window says "The radios are tuned on the PFD, not the MFD." and sends nothing. The PFD's knobs do the same job, so nothing is missing; the window is #242's code, so this is for its author, not changed here.
- **Reversionary mode** is not the DISPLAY BACKUP key on this aircraft (C172-10): the PFD is reversionary, engine strip and all, while the MFD sits on its startup screen, and leaves it the moment the MFD's ENT is pressed.

## Radios, transponder, electrical and mixture, measured (2026-10-18)

Each event was sent from the Coherent view with MSFSBA running, every SimVar it might move snapshotted 1.5 s later, and everything put back (the squawk set to the 2300 the owner had typed into the old panel):

- **Radios:** the four standby events in hertz and the four swaps, each exact and each its own radio (C172-14).
- **Transponder:** `XPNDR_SET` takes BCD16 (C172-13); `TRANSPONDER STATE:1` takes every value 0 to 5 and holds it; ident lasts 17.9 s, On or Altitude only (C172-15). After a squawk is typed, its status-list row shows the new code once the list is focused.
- **Electrical:** the bus voltage SimVars tried then all read 0, so the Battery and Alternator panel shows Battery Voltage, the main battery's (C172-16). Later the same day `ELECTRICAL BUS VOLTAGE` was found to read, numbered or by the G1000's bus names (the main bus 24.37 V on the battery); the readouts are to be rebuilt on it.
- **Mixture:** `MIXTURE_RICH` moved the lever from 80 to 100 percent, and `MIXTURE_LEAN` to 0 within a frame (pulsed for 60 ms with the engine running, which kept running). Full lean is the idle cutoff, how the engine is shut down. The two buttons were replaced on 2026-10-18 by the Mixture combo, whose ends reach the same two positions through `MIXTURE1_SET`.

## Not yet confirmed

- **The Classic.** Removed on 2026-10-18 without being measured (above). When it comes back it needs its own audit: the port's reads were wrong on the G1000 (the fuel system, pitot heat, the light SimVars, the magneto, the avionics events), and its Starter sent `MAGNETO1_SET` 3 and `SET_STARTER1_HELD` 1, which the G1000 cockpit undid within one frame (C172-6).
- **The magneto key's bounce.** A set of the key has been seen to settle somewhere other than where it was sent: START from BOTH back to BOTH after 150 to 180 ms (3 of 17 times, which the Starter's backup set covers, C172-6), and once OFF onto RIGHT about 100 ms later, when the Magneto combo was arrowed Left, Right, Off with 0.2 to 0.25 s between steps. Stepped the same way at 0.6 to 0.7 s, OFF held and the engine stopped (the owner's test, 2026-10-16), so it looks tied to writes in quick succession; not measured further.
- **The standby battery's TEST position.** It exists as a switch position and does nothing else that can be measured (2026-10-16, per-frame recorder, cold and dark). `ELECTRICAL_BATTERY_STBY_2` set to 2 reads 2 for 0.24 to 0.37 s, then springs back to OFF (1) by itself, from ARM as well, like the real spring-loaded switch; −1 is ignored. The control check worked: ARM with the master off made the standby battery supply 15.5 A and switched on circuits 0, 3, 5, 6, 7, 31, 35 and 37 (the essential bus, as the real preflight's "PFD comes on"). TEST drew nothing and switched on no circuit: from ARM it dropped all eight at once, so electrically it is OFF. A TEST lamp, if the cockpit draws one, is not readable from outside. The earlier note that the event takes only 0 and 1 came from a probe too slow to see it; a first run with the engine running showed nothing either way, because the alternator carried every load (ARM drew 0 A too). The panel keeps ARM and OFF.
- **Controls measured and left out (2026-10-17, G1000 skis variant).** Each was written over SimConnect while a per-frame recorder watched it and what it might drive:
  - `INSTRUMENT_ALTIMETER_CALIBRATION_0`, the standby altimeter's knob: writes a FRACTION of its 955–1060 hPa range (−1 gave 955, 1 gave 1060, 0.554762 gave 1013.25) but SimConnect reads it back in hPa; it moves all three `KOHLSMAN SETTING` indices, exactly as Ctrl+B's `KOHLSMAN_SET` does, so Ctrl+B already sets it. Writing a pressure to it (101625, or 1013.25) slams the altimeters to 1060 hPa.
  - `INSTRUMENT_ATTITUDE_INDICATOR_BARS` (percent, `ATTITUDE BARS POSITION`) and `INSTRUMENT_AIRSPEED_CALIBRATOR` (degrees, `AIRSPEED TRUE CALIBRATE`): work, but only move a picture read by eye.
  - `LIGHTING_PEDESTRAL_1`: writing it did not hold (the pedestal light went off for one frame and came back). `LIGHTING_CABIN_1`/`_2` and `ELECTRICAL_ELECTRICAL_SWITCH_POWER12V`: the control moved, nothing measurable followed. `LIGHTING_POTENTIOMETER_5` drives `LIGHT POTENTIOMETER:5` 0–100, light not identified.
  - `LANDING_SKIS` (rests at 1): 0 and back moved no gear position, height or contact compression.
  - Not written at all, by decision: the walkaround inspection points (`UNKNOWN_LEFT_TIRE`, `_RIGHT_TIRE`, `_CENTER_TIRE`, `_LEFT_BRAKE`, `_RIGHT_BRAKE`, the three `_LANDING_*_STRUT`s, `_AILERON_LEFT`/`_RIGHT`, `_FLAP_LEFT`/`_RIGHT`, `_RUDDER`, `_TAIL_ELEVATOR`, `_INTERNAL_OIL_CAP`, `_OIL_LEVEL_CAP_DOOR`, the three `_CARGO_LEFT*` baggage doors). They are inspection animations with nothing to read back, the aileron, elevator and rudder ones follow the real control surfaces, and after the cabin door (C172-8) any of them might move the pilot. The oil check they stand for is the Oil Check panel's readout instead.
  - Cosmetic, not offered: `HANDLING_YOKE_HIDER_1`/`_2`, `UNKNOWN_BUTTONS_COLLISION_EFB_VISIBILITY_HIDE`, `UNKNOWN_MOUNT_CUP_EFB_VISIBILITY_HIDE`, `UNKNOWN_TABLET_BACKDROP`, `AIRLINER_HUD_1`/`_2` and `PROCEDURE_AUTOSTART`.
  - `LIGHTING_PANEL_1`/`_2` do work, as dimmers: 0–100 percent over SimConnect both ways, `LIGHT POTENTIOMETER:3`/`:4`, circuits 22/23, 0 is off. Built (owner's choice) as Panel Lights 1 and 2 on the Interior Lights panel, Off to Full in quarter steps; their placard names are unknown, so they keep the cockpit's numbers.
- **The ELT's third position.** `SAFETY_ELT_1` also holds 2, which stopped a transmission like ARM and did nothing from ARM. The real C172S's remote ELT switch has two positions, ON and ARM (the owner's research, 2026-10-19), so the panel offers On and Arm only and the 2 is never spoken.
