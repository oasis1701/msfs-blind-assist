# Cessna 172 Skyhawk — rules in full

Each section is the complete text of one rule. Its one-line form, under the same ID, is in `.claude/rules/c172.md`, which Claude Code loads when it reads matching code. Background: [c172.md](../c172.md).

Every measurement here was taken on 2026-10-16 on MSFS 2024's stock "C172SP G1000 Passengers Skis", over SimConnect (a scratch harness running MSFSBA's own `SimConnectManager` and `CessnaC172G1000Definition`) and the Coherent debugger (`SimVar.GetSimVarValue`, which reads B: input events too, though it cannot write them). Later measurements carry their own date. The Classic variant was never measured and was removed on 2026-10-18.

## C172-1

- **The main battery is index 2.** With both batteries on, battery 2 carried the whole load (about 20 A) and battery 1, at a higher voltage, carried none. Switching index 2 off alone killed every circuit; switching index 1 off alone changed nothing while the main battery was up. The cockpit's battery master input event, `ELECTRICAL_BATTERY_1`, follows index 2 (setting it to 0 set `ELECTRICAL MASTER BATTERY:2` to 0 and left index 1 alone). The port read index 1 as the master and index 2 as the standby, the wrong way round.
- **`TOGGLE_MASTER_BATTERY` with parameter 0 flips BOTH batteries**; with parameter n it flips only battery n. The panel's master combo sent it with no index, so a misread (the flight starts with the standby armed and the master off, which the port's index-1 read showed as "On") put both batteries the opposite way to the one chosen. The G1000's old "Standby Battery Toggle" button sent parameter 2 through MainForm's plain event-button path (which never calls `HandleUIVariableSet`) and so switched off the MAIN battery. → [c172.md](../c172.md)

## C172-2

- **Write the input event, with the value.** Setting a cockpit input event is what the cockpit switch does, so a combo set is deterministic and a misread can never move the switch the wrong way. The stock events the port used do not all reach this cockpit: `AVIONICS_MASTER_1_SET`/`_2_SET` moved only `AVIONICS MASTER SWITCH:n` and left the avionics switches and buses where they were (bus 2's nine circuits stayed dark); `FUEL_SELECTOR_SET` did nothing at all. `SwitchInputEvents` names each switch's event; a combo's value is the event's value (magneto 0 Off, 1 Right, 2 Left, 3 Both; standby battery 0 ARM, 1 OFF; fuel valve 0 closed, 1 open).
- **Read what the switch drives.** The new fuel system's `FUELSYSTEM PUMP SWITCH:1` and `FUELSYSTEM VALVE SWITCH:1`, never `GENERAL ENG FUEL PUMP SWITCH:1` (never moved) or `GENERAL ENG FUEL VALVE:1` (stayed 1 with the valve shut). `PITOT HEAT SWITCH:1`, never `PITOT HEAT` (stayed 0). The lights' `LIGHT NAV` (and BEACON, STROBE, LANDING, TAXI), never `LIGHT NAV ON`, which is 1 only while the lamp is lit: with the battery off a switched-on light read Off.
- **Three switches drive nothing a SimVar reports**, so they are read from the input event itself (`SimVarType.InputEvent`, SIM-24): both avionics buses (bus 1's loads are all dual-fed from the essential bus, so no circuit changes when it moves) and the standby battery (`ELECTRICAL MASTER BATTERY:1` stays 1 in both positions; only the battery's load changes). The magneto key is read the same way, because no SimVar holds its position (`RECIP ENG LEFT MAGNETO:1` is one magneto, a Bool).
- Before the input-event catalog arrives (a few seconds after the aircraft loads) no write can land, and the pilot hears a refusal rather than a silent fallback to the events measured not to work. → [c172.md](../c172.md)

## C172-3

- **The GFC 700's keys are pressed.** Each `AS1000_AUTOPILOT_<KEY>_PFD` set to 1 is one press of the bezel key; 0 does nothing. Measured key by key: AP engaged AP and FD in ROL and PIT and a second press disengaged it; FD was refused while the autopilot flew; HDG, ALT (holding the current altitude) and VS engaged; VERTICALSPEED_UP/DOWN stepped the VS target 100 fpm in VS, the pitch reference 0.5 degrees in PIT (`AUTOPILOT PITCH HOLD REF`, negative for nose up: -3.7 after engaging near 2.7 nose up and two Nose Up presses) and nothing at all in ALT (two presses left the FMA's "ALT, target 760 FT" and every altitude SimVar unchanged); FLC was refused until an altitude was selected, then engaged with ALTS armed; NAV, VNV, APR and BC did nothing on the ground with no flight plan or signal, as the real unit does. Marshall's "NAV refused while HDG is on" was this autopilot arming, not a fault, and his turn-HDG-off-first workaround is gone.
- **The selected altitude.** `AP_ALT_VAR_SET_ENGLISH` ignores its value on this G1000: the first one set the current altitude rounded up to the next 100 ft and each later one added 1,000 ft (as DA40A-2 found on the DA40). `ShowSelectedAltitudeDialog` sends one `AP_ALT_VAR_INC` (which marks the altitude SELECTED; a direct write alone leaves FLC refused) and half a second later writes the exact value to `AUTOPILOT ALTITUDE LOCK VAR` with a data-definition write. Both are SimConnect, so no MobiFlight is needed; measured 6000 and 3500 exact, and FLC engaged after it on a fresh flight. `HEADING_BUG_SET`, `AP_VS_VAR_SET_ENGLISH` (a descent as 32-bit two's complement) and `KOHLSMAN_SET` (millibars × 16) land exactly as sent. → [c172.md](../c172.md)

## C172-4

- **MSFS 2024 can park the aeroplane with its walkaround covers fitted, and an engine with its cover in does not run.** On a fresh cold-and-dark flight `COVER ON:1`, `:2` and `:3` were all 1; the sim's own autostart cranked for ten seconds with no fuel flow and gave up, and MSFSBA's Starter did the same. With the covers off, one press of the Starter started the engine at once.
- The input events `UNKNOWN_COVER_ENGINE`, `UNKNOWN_COVER_PITOT` and `UNKNOWN_COVER_STATIC_PORT` each TOGGLE their cover (the B: value stays 0); one press at a time showed engine = `COVER ON:1`, pitot = 2, static port = 3. A toggle pressed against a stale state puts the cover the wrong way, so `SetCoverAsync` presses only after a fresh read says the cover differs from what the pilot chose (a second "Installed" leaves it installed, measured). The covers are announced, so the Starter can say "Engine cover installed" from the cache before it cranks. → [c172.md](../c172.md)

## C172-5

- **Measured by which tank drained**, with the engine at 1,500 rpm for 40 s a setting: on junction setting 1 only the left tank dropped (0.048 gal), on 2 both dropped equally (0.024 each), on 3 only the right. The selector's input value 0, 1, 2 sets the junction to 1, 2, 3. The cold-and-dark flight starts on setting 1, LEFT; the sim's autostart moves it to BOTH. The provisional labels written before the measurement (1 Both, 2 Left, 3 Right) were wrong, which is why a label is never taken from an assumed order. → [c172.md](../c172.md)

## C172-6

Measured on 2026-10-16 with the engine cover fitted (the starter cranks, the engine cannot catch), a recorder in the cockpit's VLogic view sampling `B:ENGINE_MAGNETO_1`, `GENERAL ENG STARTER:1` and the RPM about 70 times a second, and the probe sending the writes.

- **The starter follows the key, every frame.** `SET_STARTER1_HELD` 1 with the key on BOTH engaged the starter for one frame (25 ms) before the cockpit switched it off again. The only way to crank is the key on START.
- **The key springs back 3.0 to 3.6 s after it reaches START, whatever is sent meanwhile.** `ENGINE_MAGNETO_1` set to 4 again every 150 ms (the owner asked for a Push/Release combo that held it, which did this) changed nothing: a set to the value the key already has is ignored, 4.01 reads as 4, and 3 then 4 in the same instant is seen as no change. The key left START at 3.1 and 3.3 s, and the next set brought it back 44 to 130 ms later. One `MAGNETO1_START` cranked for 3.0 s; repeated every 100 ms it toggled the key on and off, and the engine never turned. The earlier note that a 150 ms repeat holds the key came from a probe that read too slowly to see the drops, and its "about 2 s" was wrong too.
- **Every gap restarts the crank.** Cranking RPM climbs 3, 47, 59 over three seconds; after even a 44 ms drop it fell to 29, and after the longer ones to 0, so a crank kept going by re-pressing hiccups every 3 s (the owner heard it). The cockpit's own mouse hold is in the model behaviour, out of reach of SimConnect and the calculator (`B:ENGINE_MAGNETO_1_Set` through the calculator moves the key; nothing found holds it).
- **So the Starter is a button: one START, a crank of about 3 s, as the sim gives a key turned and let go.** A single set from BOTH sometimes bounced back after 150 to 180 ms (3 of 17 sets that moved the key to START; it could not be provoked afterwards), so `StarterBackupMs` (250 ms) later the button sets START again: that puts back a bounced key, which then cranked the full 3.4 s, and on a key still at START it changes nothing. Six presses with the backup, from OFF and from BOTH, each cranked 3.07 to 3.40 s without a gap. A cold engine that needs longer is pressed again. → [c172.md](../c172.md)

## C172-7

Measured on 2026-10-16, engine off, master and both avionics buses on, the lights, pitot heat and fuel pump switched on so their circuits were live, with the per-frame recorder watching every `CIRCUIT ON:n` and `CIRCUIT BREAKER PULLED:n` and the probe writing the breakers.

- **A direct set.** Every one of the 30 `ELECTRICAL_BREAKER_BTN_BREAKER_*` input events went to the value written: 1 pulled the breaker, a second 1 left it pulled, 0 pushed it back in. All 30 read 0 on a fresh flight. Unlike a cover (C172-4) there is no toggle, so a stale reading cannot pull the wrong way.
- **Read from the input event** (`BreakerInputEvents`, SIM-24). `CIRCUIT BREAKER PULLED:n` also flags most of them (and index 0 is 1 while any is pulled), but the bus feeds and a few others set no flag, and a flag gives no key back to the breaker.
- **Some breakers cut nothing alone, by design.** ADC, Nav 1 and PFD each have one breaker on the essential bus and one on avionics bus 1; pulling either alone cut nothing, pulling both cut the circuit (PFD circuit 3, ADC 6, Nav 1 35). Avionics bus 1's own feed cut nothing for the same reason (all its loads are dual-fed, as C172-2's audit found). Before calling one of these broken, pull its partner.
- **A breaker that moves by itself is spoken** (the owner's request), from `ProcessSimVarUpdate`: "Fuel Pump breaker popped", "... breaker in", with the bus named for the three names on two buses ("Essential Bus PFD breaker popped"). The first reading after a load, a flight reset or a switch back to the aircraft is a baseline, never a change. The pilot's own pull from the panel is silent: MainForm suppresses the announcer around the call while a combo set echoes back (CORE-8). The method returns false so the generic path still refreshes the combo; true would leave a popped breaker's combo saying "In". The breakers have no Ctrl+M row, since they are not continuous announced variables. → [c172.md](../c172.md)

## C172-8

- **The cabin door and its handle take the pilot out.** On 2026-10-16 the probe wrote `UNKNOWN_DOOR_HANDLE_1` = 1 (it read nothing back) and, six seconds later, `INSTRUMENT_DOOR_LEFT` = 1: the door (`INTERACTIVE POINT OPEN:0`) opened, then snapped shut in a single frame 1.7 s later, and from then on ignored every write. The owner found the pilot standing outside the aircraft, as after the walkaround key, and had to restart the flight. Neither is a cockpit switch in MSFS 2024: never write either, and the door is not offered (pinned by a test).
- The two windows are ordinary direct sets: `AIRCRAFT_DOOR_WINDOW_PILOT` 1 opens the pilot's (`INTERACTIVE POINT OPEN:4`) in about a second, 0 closes it; `AIRCRAFT_DOOR_WINDOW` the other one (`:5`, which side it is was not measured). → [c172.md](../c172.md)

## C172-9

- **Two readers, two scales.** The cabin heat and cabin air levers (`PNEUMATICS_IE_PNEUMATICS_LEVER_VALVE_HOTAIRVALVE`, `..._COCKPITVALVE`) were first measured through the Coherent debugger: written 100 they read 1, written 0.5 they read 0.005, so the lever looked like percent in and a fraction out. The panel was built on that, a combo keyed 0 to 1 that wrote its step times 100. In the owner's first test (2026-10-16) every write landed, but SimConnect delivered the value in PERCENT (debug.log: `SetInputEvent ... = 50`, then `Input event ... (C172G_CABIN_AIR) = 50`). The combo's classifier read 50 as past fully open and snapped to Open after every pick, so arrow keys seemed dead or jumped.
- **So the scale of any input event is taken from SimConnect**, the path MSFSBA reads, never from `SimVar.GetSimVarValue('B:...')` in a Coherent view. The integer switches measured this way (magneto, breakers, ELT, standby battery) read the same on both; a continuous input may not. The levers are keyed 0, 25, 50, 75, 100 and written as their step (`Lever`, `QuarterStep`); the panel light dimmers too (`Dimmer`), checked over SimConnect before they were built (50 written, 50 read). → [c172.md](../c172.md)

## C172-10

- **Every GMA 1347 key is a momentary push that toggles.** Measured on 2026-10-17 (MSFSBA closed, the scratch harness writing each `AS1000_*_MIDCOM` input event to 1 over SimConnect, a 20 ms Coherent recorder watching the input event and the SimVars): the input event read 1 for about 150 ms and dropped back to 0 by itself, and each push flipped the key. NAV1 pushed once set `NAV SOUND:1` to 1, pushed again to 0; NAV2, ADF and DME the same on `NAV SOUND:2`, `ADF SOUND:1` and `DME SOUND`; MKR/MUTE flips `MARKER BEACON TEST MUTE`, HI SENS `MARKER BEACON SENSITIVITY HIGH`, SPKR `SPEAKER ACTIVE`. Writing 0 is not a press: it changed nothing.
- **So a key is pushed only after a fresh read says it differs** (`SetAudioKeyAsync`, `SetTransmitRadioAsync`), the shape the covers already use (C172-4). A push against a stale state would put the key the other way.
- **This is not the DA40's GMA 1347.** The COWS DA40's keys are `AS1000_MID_*` and take a value, 1 on and 0 off, a second 1 doing nothing (DA40F-12). Same instrument, different cockpit binding: never copy one aircraft's write onto the other.
- **The transmitting radio's own key does nothing.** COM1 pushed while COM 1 transmitted left `COM RECEIVE:1` at 1; COM2 pushed while COM 2 transmitted the same. The other radio's key flips its receive and `COM RECEIVE ALL` together. Turning the transmitting radio's receive off is therefore refused aloud ("COM1 is the transmitting radio and is always heard."), as the DA40 does, and the combo is re-read so it shows On again.
- **The intercom keys have no SimVar of their own.** PILOT and COPLT, pushed in every order, moved two SimVars: `INTERCOM SYSTEM ACTIVE` 0 means neither lamp is lit; with it 1, `INTERCOM MODE` 0 is PILOT alone, 1 both, 2 COPLT alone, and the mode stays where it was when both go out. So they are buttons that read the lamp back, and the audio panel's status list composes both lamps (`IntercomLamps`).
- **Measured and not offered:** COM 1/2, TEL, PA, AUX, MAN SQ and PLAY moved no SimVar the recorder watched; COM3 MIC and COM3 moved `COM TRANSMIT:3` and `COM RECEIVE:3`, but the aircraft has no third radio; `AS1000_AUDIOPANEL_PASS_COPILOT_MIDCOM` behaved as a level (a 1 stayed, read 0.01 in Coherent) with no visible effect; `AS1000_AUDIOPANELVOLUME_MIDCOM` at 50 moved nothing. `AS1000_DISPLAYBACKUP_MIDCOM` registered each press but put neither display in reversionary: the PFD's reversionary mode came only from the MFD's startup screen (`WTG1000_PFD.checkIsReversionary` sets it while `isMfdPoweredOn` is false, which it is until the MFD's database page is acknowledged with ENT). → [c172.md](../c172.md)

## C172-11

- **The CRS knob sets the course of whatever the CDI is on**, as on the DA40 (DA40A-20): with the CDI on GPS, `AS1000_COURSE_PFD` and `H:AS1000_PFD_CRS_INC` moved `GPS OBS VALUE`; on LOC1, `NAV OBS:1`; on LOC2, `NAV OBS:2`. The CDI source reads as `GPS DRIVES NAV1` (1 on GPS) and, off GPS, `AUTOPILOT NAV SELECTED` (stepping the CDI softkey GPS, LOC1, LOC2: 1/1, 0/1, 0/2; back on GPS the NAV radio stays 2).
- **The stock events land exactly:** `GPS_OBS_SET` 90 set `GPS OBS VALUE` 90, `VOR1_SET` 90 set `NAV OBS:1` 90, `VOR2_SET` 90 set `NAV OBS:2` 90. The typed Course (`SetCourseAsync`) reads the source fresh and sends the event for it (`CourseEventFor`), over SimConnect: the C172 uses no calculator path, so the DA40's RPN `CourseWrite` is not an option.
- **Never the knob's own input event as a setter.** `AS1000_COURSE_PFD` written 1 raised NAV 1's course by one degree, and the same 1 a few seconds later lowered NAV 2's by one; its value is not a direction that can be relied on.
- **Heading sync** is the HDG knob's push, `AS1000_HEADING_SYNC_PFD`: one press moved `AUTOPILOT HEADING LOCK DIR` from 0 to 039 with the aircraft on 039. The button reads the bug back and speaks it. → [c172.md](../c172.md)

## C172-12

- **The three COM audio rows move each other.** COM2 MIC set `COM TRANSMIT:2` and also turned COM 2's receive on, with `COM RECEIVE ALL`, in the first run, and in a second run turned COM 1's receive off instead; COM1 MIC put them back. The knock-on varies, so the rows always show what the sim reads back.
- **A knock-on of the pilot's own pick is not spoken.** The screen reader already said the pick (CORE-7); hearing "COM2: On" after choosing COM 2 to transmit reads as somebody else's change. After MSFSBA sets one COM audio row, the others' changes stay quiet for `ComAudioGraceMs` (2.5 s, past the 1 s batch that delivers them, DA40S-2; `IsComAudioSideEffect`); a change with no write of ours behind it speaks.
- **Spoken by the definition, never swallowed from the generic path.** MainForm's generic announcer keeps a baseline per variable (`SimVarMonitor`) and speaks only a value that differs from it. A definition that returns true for a knock-on leaves that baseline at the old value, so a later change BACK to it, from the cockpit or hardware, is silent. The DA40's `IsComAudioSideEffect` has that shape. So the C172 speaks every audio row itself (`NoteAudioUpdate`, returning true, with `RefreshControlWhenDefHandled` so the combos follow) and moves its own baseline on every delivery, quiet or not; the pilot's own pick is hidden by MainForm's echo wrap (CORE-8) and still moves it, and a Ctrl+M mute works through `DefAnnounceMuteSets` (VAR-8).
- **Fast-sampled, not batched.** On the 1 s batch every pick waited for the next batch before its fresh read answered: in the owner's first test (2026-10-17) every push landed at the same 0.7 s past the second, and COM1, refused and then put back to On by a forced read that waited for the following batch, felt laggy. The audio rows and the intercom SimVars have their own SIM_FRAME subscriptions (`ExcludeFromBatch`, `HighFrequency`), delivered on change: the fresh read answers from a cache at most a frame old, and the forced read goes out within a frame. COM1's is held 1.5 s after "COM1 is the transmitting radio and is always heard." so the combo's own "On" does not speak over it. → [c172.md](../c172.md)

## C172-13

- **The squawk is BCD16.** Measured 2026-10-18 through the Coherent view (`K:XPNDR_SET`): 0x4567 read back `TRANSPONDER CODE:1` 4567 and held; a plain 1234 read back 452. Marshall's "Set Transponder" sent the typed code as a plain number through the generic event path, so the owner's 2300 became squawk 0074.
- **MainForm already writes squawks correctly** for a field whose key is `TRANSPONDER_CODE_SET` (padding to four digits, the octal check, `ConvertSquawkToBCD`, "Squawk set to 2300"), as the FBW A320 and A380, the Fenix and the HS787 use it. The C172 uses that key and its `HandleUIVariableSet` returns false for it before its generic event path, which would otherwise send the plain number again. → [c172.md](../c172.md)

## C172-14

- **The typed standby goes in hertz.** `COM_STBY_RADIO_SET_HZ` 121705000 set COM 1's standby to 121.705 and `COM2_STBY_RADIO_SET_HZ` 118185000 set COM 2's to 118.185, both 8.33 kHz channels exact; `NAV1_STBY_SET_HZ` 113300000 and `NAV2_STBY_SET_HZ` 109500000 set 113.30 and 109.50 (2026-10-18, as on the DA40, DA40A-6). The hertz go through whole kilohertz (`StandbyHz`), so a typed 121.705 is exactly 121705000.
- **Each radio swaps through its own event:** `COM_STBY_RADIO_SWAP` (COM 1), `COM2_RADIO_SWAP`, `NAV1_RADIO_SWAP`, `NAV2_RADIO_SWAP`, each moving only its radio.
- **A frequency outside the band is refused aloud, never clamped** (`StandbyRefusal`: COM 118.000 to 136.990, NAV 108.00 to 117.95), and text that is not a number is NaN, never 0 (`UnparseableTextAsNaN`). → [c172.md](../c172.md)

## C172-15

- **The mode is `TRANSPONDER STATE:1`, written directly.** 4, 3, 1, 5, 0 and 2 written each set and held (1.5 s later unchanged). The Transponder Mode combo is continuous, so it follows a mode changed elsewhere, such as the PFD's XPDR softkeys (that they move this SimVar is likely, the G1000 showing the sim's transponder, but not measured). Offered: the G1000's own modes, 1 Standby, 3 On, 4 Altitude and 5 Ground. 0 is listed as Off because a combo keeps its last pick for a value it does not list, so an unpowered transponder would otherwise still read "Altitude"; picking Off is refused aloud and the combo re-read, since the G1000 has no Off key. 2 (Test) is no G1000 mode and is not listed; `ProcessSimVarUpdate` swallows it (`IsUnlistedTransponderMode`) so it is never spoken as "Transponder Mode: 2", which costs the combo nothing, since it keeps its pick for an unlisted value either way. The measurement wrote it from a Coherent view; MSFSBA writes it with `SetSimVar`, the data-definition write, still to be confirmed in the sim.
- **Never `OnlyAnnounceValueDescriptionMatches` on the mode.** The first build set it to keep the 2 quiet, and `PanelRowRules.IsReadOnlyStatusRow` treats that flag (with more than one description) as a status-only row: the panel built a read-only text box instead of the combo, and the owner could not change the mode at all (2026-10-18). `The_transponder_mode_is_a_combo_of_the_g1000_modes_and_off_that_follows_the_sim` pins it.
- **Ident works only On or Altitude.** `XPNDR_IDENT_ON` with the transponder in Altitude set `TRANSPONDER IDENT:1` for 17.9 s and cleared it by itself; in Standby it did nothing. So the Ident button refuses aloud in Standby ("The transponder is in standby. Ident needs On or Altitude.") and otherwise reads `TRANSPONDER IDENT:1` back ("Ident"). → [c172.md](../c172.md)

## C172-16

- **The old bus voltage names read 0.** With the engine running (2026-10-18) `ELECTRICAL MAIN BUS VOLTAGE` (with and without an index 1-4), `BUS VOLTAGE:1`-`:6`, `ELECTRICAL BATTERY BUS VOLTAGE`, `ELECTRICAL GENALT BUS VOLTAGE:1`, `ELECTRICAL AVIONICS BUS VOLTAGE` and `CIRCUIT VOLTAGE:3` all read 0, as they did engine off on 2026-10-16. `ELECTRICAL BATTERY VOLTAGE:1` and `:2` read 24.79 and 24.42 V, so the Battery and Alternator panel shows the main battery's (index 2, C172-1) as Battery Voltage. `ELECTRICAL BATTERY LOAD:2` read 28.95 A at 623 rpm with the alternator carrying nothing.
- **`ELECTRICAL BUS VOLTAGE` reads.** The G1000's own panel.xml (read live from the PFD's `xmlConfig`, 2026-10-18) draws the engine page's M and E bus volts from `ELECTRICAL BUS VOLTAGE:'CROSS_FEED_BUS'_n` and `:'ESSENTIAL_BUS'_n`, and its battery amps as 0 minus `ELECTRICAL BATTERY LOAD:'Main_Battery'_n` / `:'STBY_Battery'_n` (so a discharge, a positive load, shows as negative amps). Over SimConnect, cold and dark with the master on: `ELECTRICAL BUS VOLTAGE:n` read 24.37 V on indices 0-6 and 8-16 (7 and 17-24 read 0, the avionics switches off), the named forms read the same 24.37 V, and the main battery's load read 30.47 A. With the standby battery alone (ARM, master off) the essential bus read 24.77 V, the main bus 0, and the standby load 15.48 A. `ELECTRICAL MAIN BUS VOLTAGE:'CROSS_FEED_BUS'_n`, which the G1000's HIGH VOLTS message tests, read 0, so that message cannot show in this sim. With the engine running and the alternator on, both buses read 28.0 V. The Battery and Alternator panel shows the main and essential buses by these names (`C172_MAIN_BUS_VOLTS`, `C172_ESS_BUS_VOLTS`, batched like every readout), and the main battery's voltage beside them.
- **No battery amps.** `ELECTRICAL BATTERY LOAD:'Main_Battery'_n` (the same as `:2`) read +28.58 A with the alternator charging the battery (its estimated capacity rose from 88.27 to 88.31 percent over a few seconds, its voltage 24.098 to 24.107), the same sign as the +30.47 A it read discharging with the engine off. The load is a magnitude in this sim, so the engine page's "0 minus load" shows a negative current either way, and a reading built on it cannot tell charge from discharge. The bus volts can: 28 on the alternator, about 24.4 on the battery alone. `ELECTRICAL GENALT LOAD:1`, `GENALT BUS AMPS:1` and `GENALT BUS VOLTAGE:1` read 0; `ELECTRICAL TOTAL LOAD AMPS` read 49.6. → [c172.md](../c172.md)

## C172-17

- **The warnings are the G1000's CAS.** The C172 has no annunciator lamps: its panel.xml defines seven CAS messages, read live from the PFD's `xmlConfig` (2026-10-18): OIL PRESSURE (warning, oil pressure at or below 20 psi with `ELECTRICAL MASTER BATTERY`, which reads 1 even cold and dark), LOW VOLTS (warning, the main bus at or below 25.5 V), HIGH VOLTS (warning, above 32 V, on a SimVar that reads 0, C172-16), STBY BATT (caution, the standby battery's master on and the main battery's off), LOW VACUUM (caution, below 3.5 inHg), LOW FUEL L and LOW FUEL R (cautions, below 5 gallons). Measured with the DA40's agent on the C172's PFD: the master on with the engine stopped showed LOW VOLTS, OIL PRESSURE and LOW VACUUM; the standby battery alone added STBY BATT; dark, the list is empty. The port spoke four of these from SimVars with its own thresholds (they matched the panel.xml), its oil warning silent below 1 psi so a cold start said nothing; those warnings are gone, and the CAS watcher the DA40 uses (`G1000CasWatcher`) speaks the screen. A warning recomputed from a SimVar beside it would be said twice.
- **The FMA speaks the autopilot.** As on the DA40 (DA40A-22), the watcher speaks the FMA ("Autopilot HDG, ALTS armed"), so the GFC 700 mode rows are handled silently and refreshed in an open panel (`RefreshControlWhenDefHandled`); the master and the flight director keep their OFF, which the FMA does not say. Each mode stays `IsAnnounced`, which keeps it in the cache the nose wheel's read-back reads. A press is now confirmed by the FMA, up to one poll (1.5 s) later. → [c172.md](../c172.md)

## C172-18

- **The zones are the aircraft's own.** The G1000's engine page draws each gauge's colour zones from the aircraft's panel.xml (`EngineDisplay > EnginePage`, read live from the PFD's `xmlConfig`, 2026-10-18): tachometer (`PROP RPM:1`, measured equal to `GENERAL ENG RPM:1`) green from 2100, red 2700 to 3000; fuel flow green 0 to 12 gph; oil pressure red 0 to 20, green 50 to 90, red 115 to 120; oil temperature green 100 to 245 F, red 245 to 250; EGT no zones; vacuum green 4.5 to 5.5 inHg; each tank red 0 to 1.5, yellow 1.5 to 5, green 5 to 24 gallons. - **The words.** The DA40's ("green", "red, below minimum", "yellow, high") so both G1000 aircraft sound alike. Oil pressure 20 is red, matching the CAS's OIL PRESSURE at or below 20. A reading in an uncoloured stretch says "below the green arc" or "above the green arc" only when a red or yellow zone lies further out on that side, and is otherwise just the number: idle RPM (622 measured), cold oil (76 F), vacuum at idle (4.3 inHg), a full tank and a high fuel flow are not cautions.

## C172-20
