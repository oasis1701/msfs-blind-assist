# Route start: on a runway, and away from the first cleared taxiway

Full text of the rules in `.claude/rules/route-runway-start.md`. Background: [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md).

## RTE-37

A plain taxi route loaded while the aircraft rolls or stands along a runway starts on a node of that runway's own pavement (`RouteRunwayStart.Candidates`, tried in order by route loading), because runway pavement is not a routable corridor and the nearest graph node can be a parallel taxiway across the infield.

RJAA 34L, 2026-11-06: stopped between A3 and A2, a SayIntentions clearance (A1, A6, ...) the router could not join, the lead-in rejected, and a route starting on taxiway A 200 m to the side. The tone aimed 482 m straight over the grass.

The order is the first cleared taxiway's node ahead, the other nodes ahead by distance (30 m or more), then the nodes behind, one node per exit. Nodes of one exit's curve collapse to its nearest; the first cleared taxiway's own exit is offered once. Exits on opposite banks at about the same distance are two exits, not one curve (a node's bank is the side its taxiways leave the runway by, shared along the nodes linked to it on the pavement; a crossing leads to both and stands for an exit beside it): collapsing them by distance alone could drop the near bank's exit, while the far bank's route comes back over the runway and is skipped. An exit on the other bank beside one already offered does not take one of the six places (`MaxCandidates`), so the reach along the runway stays what one bank's exits give: when the two halves of a crossing took two places, KBNA 02L (fs2020) lost the sixth exit, the only one whose route stayed off the runway.

A heading more than about 45° off the runway has no "ahead" and keeps the older start. So does a first cleared taxiway within the lead-in range (75 m) that is on this runway or at its edge (half-width + 15 m, `ClearedEdgeMarginMeters`: an exit drawn from the edge), which the usual pre-snap starts on (RTE-23). A cleared parallel across the grass strip of a small runway, within 75 m of the aircraft, does not count: the pre-snap would aim straight over the grass at it. Runway destinations and landing-exit routes have their own start logic and stay out (`RouteRunwayStart.Applies`). A closed runway (both ends with closed markings in the runway table, `RunwayCenterline.IsClosed`) is not one the aircraft rolls along to an exit: taxiways are drawn along closed runways, and an aircraft there is taxiing. The fs2020 navdata keeps start rows for 1,226 closed runway ends at 678 airports with taxi paths (LKPR 12/30, KABQ 17/35 among them); fs2024 marks none. A closed runway still drops a node whose roll would cross it, like any runway, and every other runway question still sees it.

A node whose straight roll would touch a runway the aircraft is not already on is dropped, because no stop can be placed before the route's first node. An exit whose route leaves the runway and comes back onto it is skipped (`RouteReturnsToRunway`): at KFLL 10L Q the far bank's node made the hold pass stop the pilot short of the runway just left. The exits ahead are tried before those behind (`RouteRunwayStart.Choose`), since one behind turns the aircraft round on the runway. Among the exits one way, a start whose route follows the clearance wins over one where the router fell back to a shortest path, even an earlier one; with none following it, the first fallback. Preferring the clearance across both ways was measured and rejected: at KLSV 21L every exit ahead fell back, and an exit behind that followed the clearance sent the pilot round on the runway and 99 m off the pavement. With no exit left, the older start stands, since a stop on the pavement is never acceptable (HLD-4). The chosen start's route is the one loaded, not built again.

Until the aircraft reaches it, the tone aims at the start node itself (`AimsAtStartNode`): the look-ahead walk reads along the first segment and, where that leaves at an angle, lands up the exit, aiming the whole roll beside the runway. The spoken "Join taxiway X in N" counts the roll to the start node (`_routeStartGapMeters`), and the far-start warning is not spoken for it.

A first-row Turn pick works from that start (`TurnPickContext.KeepRouteStart`, as off the destination's network): the node nearest the aircraft, which the applier otherwise starts a first-row pick from, can be the parallel across the infield. Should a pass still rebuild the route from another node, the runway roll goes with it (`_routeStartGapMeters` back to 0): the far-start warning and the tone's ordinary aim return for a start that is no longer on the runway.

A recalculation while the aircraft is still on the runway (`TryRecalculateRoute`, plain routes only) chooses its start the same way, from the remaining clearance (or none, RTE-20). Each candidate's route is built as the recalculation builds it, with the committed Turn-pick blocks and a kept tail for a pick still ahead (TURN-3), so the "comes back onto the runway" test judges the route that is adopted, and the chosen route is not built again. A probe built without them judged a different route from the one adopted whenever a Turn pick was in play. The recalculated route is not joined until the aircraft reaches that node, so the tone aims at it and the never-joined escape watches the roll (RTE-7). Before, a pilot who rolled past the start exit, or kept rolling away from a start behind, was re-routed from the parallel taxiway the start had avoided.

Only nodes on the pavement are candidates. An exit drawn to end just beyond the edge, and never reaching the pavement, is not offered; its runway then falls back to the older start or another exit. Counted on the 100 busiest airports of each navdata (2026-11-20): 6 such exit chains in fs2024 (295 runways), 27 in fs2020 (300 runways), most of them at EDDF, against about 5,800 exit nodes on the pavement in each. Widening the candidates by the edge margin was not measured with VirtualPilot and was left alone.

Measure any change with `VirtualPilot rwystop` ([virtual-pilot.md](../virtual-pilot.md)).

## RTE-38

The lead-in ends where the route meets the first cleared taxiway (`TaxiLeadIn.Extract`): at its first segment of that name, or, when no segment carries the name, at its first node on that taxiway.

The router may join the first cleared taxiway exactly where the next one leaves it (`FindConstrainedPathCore` step 1, entry node equal to the first target). The route then has no segment of that name. `Extract` used to walk to the first such segment, found none, and measured the whole route as lead-in. The length guard (`IsAcceptable`, gap × 2.5 + 300 m) then rejected it, and the route started on the cleared taxiway's node however far away that was.

VTBS 02R, 2026-11-18: cleared E7, E8, D6, but the aircraft vacated one exit early at E10 (the rollout's own switch to an earlier exit, at 20 kt and 190 m short of E7) and stopped where E10 and E9 meet taxiway E, with the E7/E8 junction 550 m away. A 620 m lead-in was measured as the whole 2,213 m route and rejected, and the tone aimed 550 m in a straight line that left taxiway E by 55 m, over grass and water. VHHH 07L the same evening: cleared C8 with the C8/C junction 600 m ahead on the parallel, the whole route of nearly 5 km measured as lead-in, and the tone led off the runway edge across the infield.

A route that never touches the taxiway at all is still measured whole, so a router fallback keeps failing the guard.

## RTE-39

When the route from the aircraft's position never drives the first cleared taxiway and turns straight back at the junction where it meets it (`TaxiLeadIn.DoublesBackAtUndrivenStart`), that taxiway is left out of the clearance and the route is built again (`UndrivenClearanceStart.Count`, applied by `LoadRoute`). The pilot hears "Taxiway E7 is not on the way from here and was left out." ahead of the summary.

"Straight back" is 150° or more between the two edges that meet at that node, however short they are: a route that comes back over the edge it arrived by turns 180° whatever the edge's length. Reading the turn over 30 m of route either side instead was measured and rejected (2026-11-19): at KDEN 07, cleared B4 and vacated at B3, the route reaches B4 only by a 3.3 m spur from WA out to the B4 node and back, which the smoothed reading took for a 119° turn; B4 stayed, and the route started 450 m away in a straight line. The real 122° turn from VHHH C8 onto C stays a turn. A route that starts on the taxiway has met it there, as `Extract` reads it, so it never doubles back at it: a later node carrying the same name is not where it meets it (navdata repeats names on separate pieces, and merged path ends carry them onto junctions).

With RTE-38 alone the VTBS lead-in was accepted, and the route ran up E, out along E8 to the E7 junction beside the runway hold line, and back along E8: a reversal no aircraft can make on a taxiway. Without E7 the route goes up E and turns onto D6.

It stops at the first taxiway the route can keep:

- A taxiway the route only passes through at a junction stays. Leaving out every undriven taxiway was tried first, and at VTBS it also dropped E8 and D6, after which the router was free to take D7 instead of the cleared D6.
- The last cleared taxiway stays: it decides where a runway route ends (RTE-17).
- A taxiway a hold-short or Turn pick is keyed to stays (`PicksAllow`), so the pilot's own stop is never lost. So does one followed by a Turn pick on the first taxiway that would stay (`TurnPicksAllow`): a Turn pick names the turn onto its row's taxiway from the row before, and from wherever the route arrives once that row is gone, the same turn can be the other way round. The picks that remain move down with the clearance (`ClearanceRows.Without`), in the stored copies too, because a recalculation reads them against `_originalTaxiwaySequence`. A runway used as a taxi leg, skipped earlier in `LoadRoute`, re-keys every pick kind through the same helper; before, only the Turn picks moved, and a hold-short pick after the skipped leg sat on the next taxiway, which `PicksAllow` then read. A hold short set on the skipped leg's own row goes with it and is said with the hold-short notes (`ClearanceRows.DroppedHoldShortsNote`, in the summary, `LastRouteHoldShortWarning` and `LastRouteStartWarning`): "The hold short set on a skipped runway leg was dropped."
- A clearance the router cannot honour once the taxiway is gone keeps it.

A first cleared taxiway that stays although the route still turns straight back at it (a pick keyed to it, the only cleared taxiway, a clearance the router cannot honour without it, a runway start whose shorter route comes back onto the runway) is said too: "Taxiway E7 is not on the way from here. The route goes out to it and turns back the way it came." (`UndrivenClearanceStart.TurnBackNote`, in the summary and `LastRouteStartWarning`). Not for a route the router built ignoring the clearance: it never goes out to the taxiway, and its fallback reason is said already. The route is kept, on the pavement. Since RTE-38 the length guard accepts that lead-in; before, it rejected it and the route started on the taxiway's node with the tone aimed straight at it, over the grass at VTBS. Rejecting it again would bring that back, and keeping it without a word left the pilot to find a reversal at a junction.

It is judged from the node the route starts at when the decision is made: the runway start (RTE-37), else the lead-in's entry node. When the taxiway that is first after the leave-out lies within 75 m, the route then pre-snaps onto it as any clearance's does (RTE-23) and is built from that node with the shorter clearance. On a runway the route kept must still not come back onto that runway (`RouteReturnsToRunway`). A replay on VTBS 20L, loaded 150 m past E7 while still on the runway, started at E13 and ran the same out-and-back along E8 until the runway start was included. A first cleared taxiway within 75 m keeps its pre-snap (RTE-23).

The sentence is published in `LastRouteStartWarning` as well as the summary. `StartGuidance`'s first callout cuts the queued summary off, and the form's standstill utterance is the one place a route's own warnings are reliably heard (HLD-17).

`Count` starts from the route a runway start already built and hands back the route it settled on, and `LoadRoute` adopts it, so no route is built twice, except from a lead-in when a pick keeps the taxiway `Count` would leave out: the full clearance's route is then built once more. `taxi_router.log` still shows one build for each runway-start candidate tried and for each clearance `Count` tries, two for a clearance with a repeated taxiway (plain and honoured): the number of builds says nothing about a fallback, its `FALLBACK:` line does.

A lead-in still starts at the nearest node whatever the heading, so some routes now open with "Taxiway X is behind you" where they used to open with a far-start warning and a straight line.

Measure any change with `VirtualPilot missedexit`, on both navdata sets (`--sim fs2020` and `--sim fs2024`).
