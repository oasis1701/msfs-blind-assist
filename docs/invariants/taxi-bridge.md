# Taxi router: bridges between cleared taxiways that never meet

Full text of the rules in `.claude/rules/taxi-bridge.md`. Background: [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md).

## RTE-42

Two consecutive cleared taxiways that share no node are linked by the router itself: `FindRunwayBridge` picks an exit on the one and an entry on the next, and a free A* between them is the bridge leg. Built for a runway crossing between two hold lines ("K14, hold short 30L, M17" at OMDB), it serves just as often an unnamed link between two taxiways, or a clearance taken from a route's own names where two of them meet only through a short piece of a third. Its score ([RTE-24]) takes the STRAIGHT-LINE gap and the cost on to the destination by ANY path, neither of which the route then drives, and the leg was used whole:

- KEWR, 2026-11-26, "A11, A12, A, AA" to 04L. A11 and A12 meet only through an unnamed link. A12 runs straight on to the A junction and also has a curved branch whose dead end (node 693) has an unnamed link of its own towards the runway; that end won the score (384 m straight from A11 against a real leg of 497 m, and 821 m on by the unnamed link against 947 m along A12). The leg drove A12, turned onto the cross-link F2 (never cleared, "Sharp right now, taxiway F2") and ran out along the branch, and the A12 leg came straight back the same way: 95 m out and back that guidance skipped over.
- At the other end, an exit beyond the turn-off: KBIF "D, E, B, A" drove D 440 m on past its link and back.

Each bridge leg is refined as it is joined to the route (`FindConstrainedPathCore(refineBridgeLegs)`), and the route as scored is then the yardstick (`BuildConstrainedPath`):

1. **The leg joins the next cleared taxiway where it first reaches it** (`JoinWhereTheBridgeFirstReaches`), and the route drives that taxiway on from there. Only where the taxiway itself leads from that node to the scored one, by `AStarSearchStrict` (its own edges and the short hops onto them). Gap chaining was tried first and refused: it "bridged" a 25 m gap in HECA's A1 through 20 nodes of A and back, and 153 routes of the sweep below gained an out-and-back with it, 113 with the strict search. A node of the name that does not lead on is passed over and the next is tried.
2. **No out-and-back or loop at a seam a bridge leg makes** (`AppendBridgeLeg`), for the bridge itself and for the leg after it. While the leg goes straight back over the edge the route arrived by, both drop that edge: an early join on a short taxiway left the next bridge leaving from the join straight back (RJTT "R, E3, E, E2, C2"), and an exit beyond the turn-off drove on and came back (KBIF "D, E, B, A"). Then a leg that comes back to a node the route reached since the start of the leg the bridge leaves from cuts that loop out: an exit with a turning loop at its tip came back over the same nodes by another edge (OIII "B, D": 268 m east along B to a loop by runway 11R and back west; before this change, onto 11R and back). Neither reaches below that start, so the legs of earlier cleared taxiways, and the approach to the first, stay as they were. Unwound without that floor, a route that went out to its first cleared taxiway and straight back no longer touched it, the start check that leaves such a taxiway out ([RTE-39]) no longer saw it, and the lead-in was refused: ZJHK "S2, B, B10" from Gate 12 began on S2, 430 m away in a straight line, where it had been "Taxiway S2 is not on the way from here and was left out" and a lead-in via S4 and B8. The route ends where it did and only gets shorter.
3. **The route as scored stands where it needs neither** (`KeepAsScored`). Whenever a leg was refined the route is built again with none refined. A refined route that fell back gives way to one as scored that does not (an earlier join moves every junction picked after it, and where a later taxiway is drawn in pieces the new junction can have none along it: KNZY "B2, A3, A", with 9 such routes in the sweep). Otherwise the route as scored is kept when it passes no node twice, follows the clearance and drives every cleared taxiway the refined one drives; then, if it drives more of them, within the limit a repeated taxiway is honoured within (25 % + 100 m longer, [RTE-33]), even if it ends short ([RTE-17]: EBBR "E4, T" to 01, where the refined route never drove T and took 1.7 km of other taxiways to the runway); if it drives the same ones, only if it reaches the destination whenever the refined one does, with no more metres of taxiways the clearance never named and no more metres in all. Without the yardstick an early join could leave a short taxiway between two bridges at its join and go round by another (KABQ "R, Q, P, M, B": Q not driven, 35 m longer, over E), and with the reach test first, a refined route that dropped the last cleared taxiway beat the one that honoured it.

With refining off, `FindConstrainedPathCore` is the old router exactly (the sweep's 64,297 routes, identical). A named taxiway the bridge takes between two cleared ones that never meet is said in the route summary, worded as the lead-in is: "From A11, taxi via F2 to reach A12." (`RouteTaxiwaySequence.LinkClause`, `TaxiGraph.TaxiwaysMeet`); an unnamed link says nothing, and two cleared taxiways that DO meet say nothing about what the route crosses between them. KEWR itself now runs A11, the unnamed link, A12 straight on past F2 to A, and AA: 1,343 m to 1441 where it was 1,487, with no F2 and no turn back, so its summary gains no sentence.

Measured (2026-11-27, the 100 busiest fs2024 airports, plain navdata, the router alone): 64,297 constrained routes where two consecutive cleared taxiways share no node. "Controller" clearances are a stand-to-runway shortest route's own names (4,858 routes, 995 changed at 54 airports): exact out-and-backs 817 → 92 (82 with no floor under the out-and-back, which 2 explains), routes passing a node twice 825 → 100, reaching the runway 4,481 → 4,840 (none lost), fallbacks to the shortest path 727 → 720 and none new. Of the 636 changed routes whose reach did not change, 624 are shorter (median 137 m) and 12 longer: 7 KSLC "… A9, A, A11" to 16R (+169 to 204 m), which fell back to the shortest path before and now follow the clearance with a gap-chained spur where A9 meets A, not at a bridge (the same spur is in the old routes, "A8, A9"), and KABQ twice (+490 m: no out-and-back now, 480 to 640 m more of the cleared taxiways driven). 14 routes no longer drive one cleared taxiway, each of which they reached only by going out and back; 38 now drive one they did not. The 359 that now reach the runway stopped short before because the bridge's entry, near the destination by its score, read as the last taxiway's node nearest it and set off the "branches off the destination" terminus ([RTE-17]) from the wrong end (RJTT "T, U" to 22: out to U's dead end, back, and stopped at U's other end). Two synthetic families (every pair of named taxiways within 400 m that share no node, alone and with a third that meets the second): out-and-backs 3,990 → 161 and 13,860 → 1,989, fallbacks 601 → 571 and 1,474 → 1,464 with none new; 500 now end on their last cleared taxiway instead of reaching the runway, each a last taxiway leading away from it that the old route drove out along and back (EBBR "A1, B1" to 19: 1.8 km out to a dead end of B1 and back); 19 drove a cleared taxiway the new route does not, 18 of them out to the last one's end and stopping short there, where the new one reaches the runway: the 25 % bound refuses the far longer route (KDFW "JS, L" to 35L: 5.3 km against 1.3); the controller clearances have none of these.
