# iniBuilds A300-600 displays, MCDUs, tablet and autoflight — rules in full

Each section is the complete text of one rule. Its one-line form, under the same ID, is in `.claude/rules/a300-displays.md`, which Claude Code loads when it reads matching code. Background: [a300.md](../a300.md).
Split from `a300.md` on 2026-10-10, when the rule-file cap fell to 8,000 characters (CCT-4); the text is unchanged. A cross-reference to an A300 rule may point at either file.

## A300-4

- The A300 MCDU screens come from the client data areas `iniAirbusMCDU_1`/`_2` (1008 bytes), published only while `L:INI_MCDU_OPTION` is 1 — which does NOT survive a flight load, so `IniA300Definition.EnsureMcduExport` sends it on EVERY window open and whenever it is not known to be on (next bullet). `A300McduDataManager` keeps the MD-11 manager's rules: one per connection (reused, never re-registered, on the same handle), ON_SET + CHANGED subscription PLUS a one-time snapshot on a DIFFERENT request id. Read characters through `A300McduText.Glyph` — the MCDU fonts draw `!` as an entry box, `^` as a degree sign and `a`/`b`/`l`/`r`/`{`/`}`/`~` as arrows and a delta (decoded from the glyph outlines), never as the letters they are. MCDU keys are `INI_MCDU1_`/`INI_MCDU2_` L:var pulses through the definition's ONE queue: a press and release in ONE calculator string does not register. Never press CLR on an empty scratchpad (the A300 puts "CLR" into it): the clear-all first lets the keys already in the queue land and gives the last of them the settle margin (`SettleMarginMs`, 0.4 s) to show on that unit's screen (an entry just sent, a Backspace; a change of the scratchpad is its only sign that a CLR landed, so a queued key would read as one and each such change queued one more CLR), then presses ONE CLR and waits for the scratchpad to change (`ClearConfirmMs`, 1.5 s from the CLR's release), then re-reads. The margin counts from the key's ACTUAL release: the definition raises `McduKeyReleased` from the key pump and the window stamps it per unit (`A300McduForm._lastRelease`), because the pump runs slower than its nominal 160 ms a key (two real timers, `Task.Delay(80)` measured about 93 ms: about 187 ms a key, PR #300 review, 2026-10-14), so a margin counted from the nominal pace is about 494 − 27·P ms for a P-key entry and gone at about nineteen keys. It is kept PER UNIT, not in the scratchpad read-back's single hold, which belongs to the unit shown and stays `pending × 160 + 400`: after a unit switch (Ctrl+Shift+R) the clear goes on with its own unit and must still wait out that unit's margin, since the empty queue alone leaves the last key's effect off the screen. It is skipped when no key but the clear's own CLR has gone to the unit since that CLR (`_keysSent`), or each character would cost over half a second. The wait for the queue is bounded by PROGRESS, not by the queue's size when it began: it fails only when the queue has not shrunk for `ClearConfirmMs`, since a slow pump, or an entry typed on the other unit meanwhile, is not stuck. Not covered: a key pressed in the cockpit itself (it is not in the queue) and an aircraft slower than the margin (a margin, not a measurement); either can still be taken for a CLR's effect. → [a300.md](../a300.md)
- The definition keeps the export on; one write per window open is not enough (PR #300 review, 2026-10-13). The window's only write was `EnableMcduExport`, called when the window went from hidden to shown, with its result ignored: a window opened before the sim connected (the write cannot land and says so), or kept open across a flight load, never got a fresh screen. `EnsureMcduExport(force)` remembers whether the option is on for THIS connection and flight: off at construction, cleared by `OnSimContextReset` (a disconnect or a flight load), by `OnLoadSettled` (below) and by a send that could not land, and it sends `1 (>L:INI_MCDU_OPTION)` when the export is off or the caller forces it. The window forces it on EVERY open (Shift+M on an open window too), because what is remembered is only what MSFSBA last sent and the tablet's setting can be switched off by hand, and the window also asks on every poll, so a window kept open across a flight load, or opened before the sim connected, gets the screens without being closed and opened. A plain ask while the export is on sends nothing and does not consult the write path, so a caller may ask as often as it likes (a poll); a send that cannot land returns false and leaves the export off, so the next ask tries again, and a forced send that cannot land forgets an export that was on. A disposed definition, or one with no sim attached, returns false and sends nothing. A live read on 2026-10-13 (A300 cold and dark, nothing written yet that session) gave null: the export is off until MSFSBA sends it.
- `OnLoadSettled` clears the flag once more when the post-load seed gate releases (the load has settled, or its ceiling passed: `Md11SeedTrigger.Quiet` or `Ceiling`; `OnContinuousBatchDelivered`, where `SeedFromCache` runs; PR #300 review, 2026-10-13). The flag is set at SEND time, and after a flight load the first send can land before the aircraft's own start-up, which may switch the option off again; so once the gate releases, the next poll sends the option once more (one extra write per load while the window is open). The race itself is not measured: a flight load with the window open, listening for the screens to come back, is the live check. `IniA300McduKeyTests` and `A300McduFormTests` pin the contract.

## A300-5

- A300 typed values go through `A300TypedValues` only. NAV frequencies are written as their `_MHZ`/`_KHZ` L:var parts (the combined `INI_VOR1_FREQUENCY` and the stock NAV radio are rebuilt from them, so writing either is silently undone). A typed speed in the other unit sends SPD/MACH, WAITS `ModeSwitchSettleMs`, then writes — never in one string: the aircraft applies the switch later and converts whatever the variable holds then. Whether the flip is needed is decided from the mode MSFSBA last commanded (`SpeedMode`, which `IsMach` reads: a typed speed's flip or a press of the SPD/MACH button, held 2.5 s by `A300CommandedState`), else the cache. The cache lags a second, and the flip is a TOGGLE: a second entry (the value box stays open after Enter) or a speed typed right after the button, planned from the old mode, flipped it back and wrote the number in the wrong unit. Typed speeds also go out one after another (`SendTypedAsync` queues each behind the last), so a write never lands inside an earlier entry's wait (PR #300 review, 2026-10-13). Altimeters are `index value (>K:2:KOHLSMAN_SET)`, index FIRST. → [a300.md](../a300.md)
- A typed altitude, vertical speed or decision height is a WHOLE number (PR #300 review, 2026-10-13). Both ways of typing a value (the panel's box in `MainForm.PanelBuilder.cs` and `Parse` in the value boxes) turn a comma into a point, so "-1,500", the thousands separator the box's own hint showed, arrived as -1.5, rounded to 0 and was written and confirmed as "Vertical speed 0 feet per minute", and "1,200" became a decision height of 1 foot. `A300TypedValues` refuses a fraction (`WholeNumberError`) instead of rounding it, and a non-zero vertical speed under 100 either way (`VerticalSpeedError`), as the MD-11 and the FBW A320 do; 0 stays valid (level off). The hints and errors write 49000, 6000 and 2500 with no separator, and the value box speaks the plan's own refusal (`ValidateBoxInput`) so "-1,500" is never answered with a range it is inside. A typed altimeter setting is confirmed as the value WRITTEN, `Math.Round(mb * 16) / 16`: the aircraft stores sixteenths and every read-back says that figure, while the confirmation worked out from the unrounded entry was a hectopascal out for about 3% of the inch entries (29.90 inches is 1012.53, stored as 1012.5, read back as 1012).

## A300-6

- The A300 tablet reader (`coherent-a300-efb-agent.js`) presses with ONE click event (pointer and mouse down/up, then click; a bare `el.click()` is ignored and two clicks would run a toggle twice), never requires a non-zero size on a CONTAINER (the page container is 2048 x 0), never `focus()`es a field (the on-screen keyboard covers the page), and never emits or writes back a password field's value. The box is emitted EMPTY and its name says only whether a code is stored ("Hoppie, code set"); `A.setValue` writes nothing, and returns false, for an empty or blank entry, so Enter on the untouched box cannot overwrite the stored code (PR #300 review, 2026-10-13: the box used to carry the placeholder "set", which Enter wrote over the Hoppie code). Clearing a stored code from MSFSBA is not offered. The heading above the box ("Hoppie") is dropped as an echo of its name, with or without a stored code (`A.dropEchoHeadings` compares the name without the `A.CODE_SET` suffix, through `A.echoName`). The press goes where a finger on the control's center would land (`A.pressTarget`: the topmost element there when it is inside the control, else the first inner element with its own onclick), NEVER on the stamped control alone: the tablet hangs the Unlock and gear handlers on the picture INSIDE the button, so a press on the button did nothing and the tablet could not be unlocked (2026-10-04). An overlay (control box, its confirmation, powered off, pause, My Flight's viewers) is read ALONE under its own page name, and found with `A.shown` (every ancestor displayed), never `A.visible`, which looks at one element and lets a hidden page's viewer pass. → [a300.md](../a300.md)
- `A.setValue` writes a number box (`type="number"`) the number the pilot's text means, clears it for an empty entry, or writes nothing (PR #300 review, 2026-10-13). The browser blanks a number box on any text that is not a plain number, so "20,000", "20 000" and "12,5" emptied the field. `A.typedNumber` reads a comma or a space (plain, no-break, thin or narrow) as a thousands separator only in a well-formed grouping: one to three leading digits, the first not 0, then groups of exactly three ("20,000" is 20000, "1,234,567", "20 000", "12 345,5"; a space groups the whole number only). Any other single comma is the decimal point ("12,5" is 12.5; "0,500" is 0.5 and "1234,567" is 1234.567, since neither is a well-formed grouping). It then writes `String(n)`. An empty entry (or spaces alone) clears the box and returns true, committed like any typed value (input, change, keyup, blur): a number box is emitted with its real value, so Enter on an emptied box is a deliberate deletion, where a password box is emitted empty and is never cleared (above). Any other text that is still not a finite number ("abc", "15 kg", "1e3" and any exponent, "+5" and any plus sign, "1.234,5", "1,234 567", "1234 567", "0 500") is refused with false, the field is left as it was and no event fires. A text box keeps exactly what the pilot typed.
- The jsdom harness (`tools/a300-efb-test/run.js`) answers what jsdom lacks, so the reader's two page reads are really exercised (PR #300 review, 2026-10-13): `document.elementFromPoint` comes from the fixtures' `data-rect` boxes (the LAST displayed element in document order whose box holds the point, so the deepest; null outside the view) unless a test loads with `noHitTest`, and `getComputedStyle` reports an element's inline `background-color`. Before, the lock test's hit-test variant ran the same fallback as its twin and a slide's color read as empty, so neither check could fail. After a change to `A.pressTarget` or `A.slideState`, mutate it (`return el` in place of the hit; `r < g` in place of `r > g`): the lock, header and slide tests must fail.

## A300-8

- The A300 display readers read ONLY aircraft variables and stock SimVars: never the simulator's memory, a picture or AI (design rule, 2026-10-04). So the upper ECAM's warnings and the ND's waypoint name and bearing are NOT read: they live inside the aircraft's display software, not in any variable. Do not "complete" them with a memory read or an AI capture without a new design decision. The memos are read since 2026-10-10 by TRANSCRIPTION, not by reading the display: their conditions are the aircraft's own code over its variables ([A300-27]).
- On-demand AI reads (design decision, 2026-10-10). After a data-versus-AI review (data covers almost all of the PFD, the ND but the waypoint names, the memos, every number on the system pages and all of the standby instruments; only AI reaches the warnings with their cleared state, the ND's waypoint names and picture, the PFD's flight director bars and flags, and the STATUS page), the fleet's AI display reads were adopted for four keys, as the Fenix has them: Alt+P (captain's PFD), Alt+N (captain's ND), Alt+E (ECAM warning display, the left screen) and Alt+S (ECAM system display, the right screen), the `A300DisplayReads` table and the `PFDA300`, `NDA300`, `WarningDisplayA300` and `SystemDisplayA300` prompts. Alt+I stays unbound ("for now"): the standby instruments are all data. The boxes, the FMA and memo call-outs and every other announcement stay data-only, and an AI answer never feeds one. With a route loaded the A300 FMS fills none of the stock GPS waypoint variables (measured 2026-10-10), which is why the ND needs AI for its waypoint names.
- The views were measured live on 2026-10-10 (MSFS 2024 1.8.16.0, Freighter GE, 1.0.11): index 3 ("CPT ALTIMETER") frames the captain's PFD and ND whole and largest, index 17 ("GAUGES") is the only view holding the whole warning display (with the engine gauges and the system display), index 4 ("ECAM") frames the system display large. The aircraft lists 42 instrument views, as many as its cameras.cfg, and these four matched file order; re-measure after an iniBuilds update, never read an index off the file's titles. The PFD prompt says the A300 PFD has no altitude tape: the frame holds the round altimeter beside it. → [a300.md](../a300.md)

## A300-9

- The FMA (`A300Fma`) transcribes the A300's own PFD::drawFMA (v1.0.11) word table, characterised by running that routine on chosen mode numbers; change a word only from a new characterisation, never by analogy with the A320. It is composed once a sample at the end of the batch that carries its sources (`DeferredFlushWatchVariable` = the pitch mode key, all 27 sources in ONE batch, pinned by `IniA300FmaBehaviourTests`; the batch boundary is at the 300th name in ordinal order, so ANY new batch-covered A300 variable can push the last sources, `INI_pitch_trim1/2`, into batch 2 — the 58 fault lights did, which is why they have their own subscriptions), and spoken there, OUTSIDE MainForm's mute wrap, so it checks `A300DisabledMonitorVariables` itself (`IsMuted`). Six sources are shared rows and lights (the autothrottle and SPD/MACH lights, both A/THR master switches, both autopilot switches, both pitch trim switches) and must never be registered a second time. The thrust column is blanked together with the guidance columns while the PFD would draw none (an IRS aligned, essential bus 2 powered, pitch trim 2 or pitch trim 1 with the captain's FD source): a DELIBERATE divergence, because the mode numbers keep old values cold and dark. Baseline-first; the guidance columns appearing and a settling flight load (`Md11SeedGate` armed) are silent. → [a300.md](../a300.md)

## A300-10

- The A300 display boxes read: green dot, S and F only at the flap lever positions the speed tape draws them (0; 1; 2 and 3 — `PFD::drawSpeedTape`); VMAX is `INI_max_speed` (the tape's top, 270 with the gear down), not `INI_VMAX_SPEED` (VMO); the standby altimeter is `INDICATED ALTITUDE:3` (it follows baro 3, measured); the ILS receiver is NAV 3; the fuel tank weights are KILOGRAMS (they sum to the stock total) and fuel reads in the tablet's unit (`L:INI_IS_METRIC`). An ECAM page lists only what its own drawing routine PRINTS as a number; a needle with no printed number or a value whose unit the page does not establish is left out, never guessed. → [a300.md](../a300.md)

## A300-11

- The A300 take-off call-outs (`A300TakeoffCallouts`) take V1 and VR from `L:INI_V1_FMGS`/`L:INI_VR_FMGS` (the MCDU take-off page) and V2 from `L:INI_V2_FMGS`, which FOLLOWS THE FCU SPEED WINDOW (250 with V2 typed as 150, measured 2026-10-04), because the A300 crew sets V2 on the FCU for take-off. So V2 is used only when `PlausibleV2` accepts it: at or above VR and at most 30 knots above. Never read `INI_V2_FMGS` as the MCDU's V2, and never announce its changes (every FCU speed change moves it). The arm is dropped on every context reset AND reconnect, never the speeds (the shared machine's rule). → [a300.md](../a300.md)
- V1 and VR ARE spoken as they are entered (2026-10-11, the FBW A380's "V1: 148 knots" and "not set" on a clear), by the definition (`SpeakEntry`), never the monitor: the speeds stay registered in "number", because a batched L:var is read in its registered unit and "knots" would convert the FMS's plain knots (`IniA300TakeoffCalloutTests` pins it). The PFD box shows V2 only through `PlausibleV2`, as the call-out judges it.

## A300-14

- Of the A300 FCU windows, ONLY the altitude window is announced on its own (`A300FcuWindows`): `INI_Altitude_Dial` is written by the knob handlers alone, while the aircraft's own autopilot logic also writes the heading, speed and vertical speed windows (heading sync, managed speed, `SyncVerticalSpeed`), so announcing those would speak changes nobody made. A panel knob step on any of the four is read back once it lands; MSFSBA's own typed altitude or altitude step suppresses the altitude call-out for 2.5 s. EVERY way of typing an altitude arms that mute, inside the one typed send (`SendTypedAsync`, on `A300TypedValues.AltitudeKey`, just before the write) and not in each caller: the Ctrl+A value box sent without arming it and spoke "Altitude 12,000 feet" twice, its confirmation and then the window's call-out about a second later (PR #300 review, 2026-10-13). A new way to type an altitude goes through that send. → [a300.md](../a300.md)

## A300-15

- The A300 decision height is `L:INI_MINIMUMS_PILOT` (and `_FO`): the stored value, written directly by the typed Decision height box. The DH knob changes it only while `L:INI_DH_SELECTED` is 1 and steps the flight path angle otherwise, so a knob-based setter would silently move the FPA instead. → [a300.md](../a300.md)

## A300-16

- Measured live 2026-10-05 at the gate (external power and batteries on, package 1.0.11, through the Coherent debugger on VCockpit01 with the same B: events the app fires). Pulling the captain's or first officer's altimeter knob is STD and pushing is QNH, as iniBuilds' tooltips say ("SET STD PRESSURE", "SET QNH PRESSURE"); the push event writes a variable named `INI_<side>_ALTIMETER_STD_COMMAND`, so never trust the name. `L:XMLVAR_Baro1_Mode` / `Baro2_Mode` read 1 in STD and 0 in QNH and drive the altimeter's STD flag; the standby (Baro3) has no push or pull. A pull saves the setting × 16 in `L:INI_BARO1_PRESSURE` / `INI_BARO2_PRESSURE`. But STD is a FLAG ONLY: the Kohlsman setting does not change, the drums keep reading against it (500 feet at 1024.94 in STD against a pressure altitude of 197), the knob still turns in STD, a push does not restore the saved value, and `A:KOHLSMAN SETTING STD` stays 0. So Ctrl+B's STD pulls only the sides in QNH (pulling a side already in STD would save 1013 over its QNH) and then writes 1013.25 to all three altimeters; QNH pushes the sides in STD and restores each one's saved setting, the standby taking the captain's. A side already in QNH is never restored (what it saved may be older than what it shows), and a saved setting outside 955 to 1060 hectopascals is reported, never used. Each press is confirmed by reading the mode back before anything is written. The mode variables stream on their own subscriptions, never the batch ([A300-9]). → [a300.md](../a300.md)

## A300-17

- `BaseAircraftDefinition.ShowTrackedWindow` keys its registry on the window's TYPE, and all five A300 value boxes (Ctrl+S, H, A, V, B) are `ValueInputForm`, which stays open after Set. Without a check, a second hotkey found the open box under the same type and re-showed it: with the speed box open, Ctrl+H brought back the speed box. `ShowValueDialog` therefore closes an open box whose title differs before asking for the new one (`A300AutoflightWindows.ReplacesOpenBox`); closing removes it from the registry, so the factory builds the requested box. → [a300.md](../a300.md)

## A300-18

- The input-mode windows name a control exactly as its panel row does (`A300PanelLayout`), so one control has one name across the app, and add what a knob push or pull does in iniBuilds' own tooltip words, which the generator copies into the map's `action` field ("Heading knob push, aircraft heading"). Words that only repeat the name are dropped ("SET SPEED KNOB" on the speed knob pull). Each control sits in one window only: the value boxes carry their axis's knob push and pull and mode buttons, and Ctrl+P carries the engage cluster, the approach modes (VOR LOC, Land), the disconnects, TOGA, the bank limit and the flight directors, as on the PMDG. Every press goes through the panel row's write path, so the refusals ("unavailable", "Switch not ready") and the commanded-state hold match the panels. In the value boxes a lamp button is read back fresh and a knob push or pull says nothing; Ctrl+P says nothing on a press. → [a300.md](../a300.md)

## A300-22

- Ctrl+B (design decision, 2026-10-09). The box has ONE mode button, labelled from the modes the aircraft REPORTS (`XMLVAR_Baro1/2_Mode`, [A300-16]), never the one just asked for: "Altimeter mode: QNH" or "STD" while both sides agree, "captain STD, first officer QNH" while they differ, and "Altimeter mode" alone while neither has been read (`A300Baro.ModeState`), as the FBW A320 window's Mode control reads its mode. A press runs the STD sequence only when both sides read QNH, and the QNH sequence otherwise, so sides that differ both end in QNH (the side already in QNH is left alone; the FBW A320's control reads one side only, so it has no rule of its own for this: a design decision); a side unread is refused aloud (`A300Baro.ToggleFor`). Each sequence speaks its own settings read-back, so the box's echo is off. A TYPED value with a side in STD first pushes every side in STD (the standby has none) and confirms each reads QNH by reading its mode back (`PressKnobsAsync`); a side that stays in STD is named ("First officer altimeter did not switch to QNH") and nothing is written, because in flight (2026-10-06, descending through FL180) a value typed with both sides in STD reached only the standby, the captain and first officer staying at 29.92 with no word said. Then the value goes to all three altimeters and is confirmed as before. An unread mode is refused aloud, and so is a typed value while a sequence is running (its decision would come from the 1 Hz cache, which still shows the old modes). The PMDG 737's typed altimeter takes STD off first the same way (`DisengageStdThenRotateAsync`). The Captain Side and First Officer Side panels' "Altimeter setting" boxes run the same sequence for their ONE side (`SetSideAltimeterAsync`; PR #300 review, 2026-10-13): they wrote `KOHLSMAN_SET` straight away and spoke the confirmation even with the side in STD, where the value never landed. Now a side that reads STD is pushed to QNH and confirmed (the other side is left alone), and only then is the value written to that side and confirmed ("Captain altimeter 1018 hectopascals, 30.06 inches"); that side's unread mode, or a running sequence (another box's push too), is refused aloud. The standby altimeter's box has no STD and writes directly. The value field is named with both units (`A300Baro.FieldName`: "altimeter setting in inches or hectopascals"), through the field name the A300 passes the shared `ValueInputForm`. → [a300.md](../a300.md)

## A300-27

- The E/WD memo list is `EWD::UpdateMemo` in inibuilds-A300.wasm (v1.0.11, read from its disassembly 2026-10-10): `EWD::prepareMemoList` builds `Message_Memo1` to `Message_Memo20` in that order, and each sample walks them: a memo whose `is_active()` holds starts a timer, and is shown once it has held for more than 2.5 s; a memo that stops holding drops out and its timer resets. The shown memos keep the list's order. Each `is_active()` compares aircraft variables (`Variables::GetInt` rounds, so the transcription rounds an L:var before comparing), three of them stock (`AircraftVariables` table at 574528: 17 TOTAL AIR TEMPERATURE Celsius, 85 SPOILERS HANDLE POSITION Percent, 279 EXTERNAL POWER ON:1 Bool). `INI_FUEL_FEED_MAN_CTL` is written by `UpdateMemo` itself from the tank pumps, and read as the aircraft leaves it.
- MSFSBA reads the inputs on their own subscriptions, consumed silently, and composes the list once a sample in `OnDeferredFlushBatchDelivered`, where the FMA is composed, and only once a flight load has settled (`Md11SeedGate`). The first complete reading after a reset is the baseline (a load's memos are not changes). The ECAM Memos box in the Displays section lists them in the E/WD's order. → [a300.md](../a300.md)
- Memos are NEVER spoken (design decision, 2026-10-11). They were spoken as they appeared from 2026-10-10, as the FBW Airbuses speak theirs ("Memo: No smoking on", about 4 s after the switch), but most follow a switch the pilot just moved (signs, parking brake, landing lights, anti-ice, TCAS), so the memo repeated the screen reader's own read-back of that switch ([CORE-7]). A sign or light now gives its switch read-back only, as on the Fenix, the PMDGs, the iFly and the MD-11. With nothing spoken, the memo line carries no Ctrl+M row ([VAR-9]).
