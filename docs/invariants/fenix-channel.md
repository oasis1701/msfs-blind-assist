# Fenix display channel rules — rules in full

Each section is the complete text of one rule. Its one-line form, under the same ID, is in `.claude/rules/fenix-channel.md`, which Claude Code loads when it reads the channel's code: the `FenixChannel*` services, the Fenix definition's display channel partials and their tests. The rules for each display's own reader are in [fenix-channel-displays.md](fenix-channel-displays.md). The data-service rules are in [fenix-data.md](fenix-data.md), which loads on this code too; its [FNX-3] records the decision to read this channel. Background: [a32nx.md](../a32nx.md).

## FNX-21

- The display channel is the Fenix's own local gRPC service on 127.0.0.1:18083, the feed its displays are drawn from (HTTP/2 without TLS; the contract is protobuf-net "code first", so there is no .proto file). `FenixChannelClient` opens only the four calls `FenixChannelCall` has: `DisplayData` (`/ProsimRemoting.GrpcAircraftDataSource/Subscribe`, a stream of display pushes, about 17 a second), `EcamEntries` (`/ProsimRemoting.GrpcSdk/SubscribeEcamEntries`, a stream sent when the ECAM list changes), and `MapDataVersion` and `MapData` (`GetMapDataVersion` and `GetMapData`, unary). `FenixChannelFields.PathOf` gives the path of each and throws for any other value, and no API of the client takes a path, so a fifth call is a decision, never an edit: it needs a new enum member, and `FenixChannelReadOnlyTests` fails at that first. The address is the numeric 127.0.0.1, never `localhost`, as [A320-40] has it for 8083.
- `FenixChannelClient.CallOnceAsync` makes only the two map calls (one request, one reply message and the trailers; an `ArgumentException` for a stream call), and `Start` refuses them. `FenixChannelMonitor.RequestOnceAsync` makes one through the monitor's own client, 5 s at most, and returns at once after the channel is refused or disposed. Only the ND window's poller calls them, while the window is open (`FenixChannelNdMapPoller`).
- The service has other calls that write a value, run an action or hand out a credential, and the client never makes one: WriteDataRef, SetSetting, SetDefault, SaveManualFailure, StartRefueling, StopRefueling, StartGsxServices, StopGsxServices, DisconnectNavigraph, GetNavigraphAccessToken and DoRpc. The rule is the four paths, not this list, which is only the calls the design names. The channel client makes none of these gRPC calls, and the names are plain words here and in the rule line because the scan below looks for a call path, never a bare name. Two of the names, SetSetting and SetDefault, exist in the app only as the 8083 data service's GraphQL settings operations (`FenixOptionEdits.SetSettingDocument` and `SetDefaultDocument`, which the Fenix options window sends; the documents window's pin write reuses the first), outside this channel. The 8083 gateway's two navigraph operations are never sent either ([FNX-3]).
- Fenix's own protections are never touched: its license and startup checks, its anti-debugging and its entitlement pipe. The channel answers any program on the same PC without a key (measured 2026-11-01), which is why it can be read at all; the decision to read it is [FNX-3]'s, and it covers the read calls and nothing around them. The app never works around, imitates or probes one of those protections, and never tries to get past a refusal.
- A refusal ends the channel for the session: every call, whichever of them was refused, a map read included. grpc-status 7 (permission denied) or 16 (unauthenticated), or HTTP 401 or 403 (which gRPC maps to 16 and 7), whether it comes in the headers of an answer that carries no message or in the trailers behind the messages, makes `FenixChannelClient` report `Refused` once and stop that call's loop for good: no retry, no other path, no other way in. `FenixChannelMonitor` then ends the rest: it marks the channel refused, so every later callback of the client is ignored; it tells the gate the channel is not answering, with the refusal as the reason, so every display reads "channel not answering: <the refusal>" and keeps its AI read ([FNX-22]); it writes "<call>: refused (<reason>); stopped for this session" once; and it disposes the client, so the other call stops too and neither asks again. A key Fenix adds is Fenix's decision, and the app does not go around it. Other answers are not refusals: status 12 (the call is not offered, as after an update that changed the channel) logs one line and asks again every 30 s; any other status, a broken stream and a refused connection (the Fenix not running) reconnect after 3, 6 and 12 s and then every 30 s, quietly.
- The default handler (`FenixChannelClient.CreateDefaultHandler`) sets `AllowAutoRedirect = false`. A followed redirect could open a path `PathOf` never gave, so a 3xx answer is a failure like any other status that is not 200: the client reports "HTTP 307" and asks its own path again.
- Pinned by `FenixChannelReadOnlyTests` (`PathOf_gives_the_four_allowed_paths`; `There_are_exactly_four_calls_and_no_path_for_any_other_value`; `The_channel_is_addressed_by_its_numeric_address`; `Only_the_four_allowed_call_paths_exist_in_the_app`, a scan of every .cs file under `MSFSBlindAssist/` for a `/ProsimRemoting.<service>/<method>` path, in code or in a comment, which also requires `FenixChannelFields.cs` to be the only file that names the service), `FenixChannelClientTests` (`Status_16_stops_the_channel`; `An_http_refusal_stops_the_channel` for 401 and 403; `A_refusal_after_the_messages_ends_the_call_too` for 16 and 7 in the trailers; `The_default_handler_follows_no_redirect`; `A_redirect_is_not_followed_and_is_reported_as_an_http_status`) and `FenixChannelMonitorTests` (`A_refusal_of_the_data_call_stops_the_ecam_entries_call_too`; `A_refusal_of_the_ecam_entries_call_stops_the_data_call_too`; `After_a_refusal_every_callback_of_the_client_is_ignored`) and `FenixChannelNdMapCallTests` (`A_unary_call_returns_its_one_message`; `A_failed_unary_call_says_why`; `A_refused_map_call_ends_the_channel`; `A_stream_call_is_not_a_unary_call`; `Nothing_is_called_after_a_refusal`). A path assembled from pieces escapes the scan: `PathOf` throwing for any value but the four is the guard there. → [a32nx.md](../a32nx.md)

## FNX-22

- `FenixChannelTrust` is the gate: a pure state machine (the time passed in, no I/O) that answers, display by display (PFD, E/WD, SD, ND, ISIS, DCDU), trusted or not with a reason. Its purpose is that a channel that has gone wrong, or a Fenix build no developer has checked, never reaches a pilot, so in doubt a display is not trusted and its key falls back to the AI read ([FNX-32] has the one exception, a dark display). A display is trusted only when every channel-wide condition and every condition of its own holds, and `VerdictFor` gives the first one that fails, in this order, with this text:
  - not connected: "channel not answering", or "channel not answering: <the client's reason>";
  - no data message yet since the connection opened: "no data yet";
  - the stream stalled, no data message for more than `StreamStaleMs` (2,000 ms; the client itself drops a data call that is silent for 10 s, or sends no answer within that time, and reconnects; it drops an ECAM entries call after 15 s, three of its 5 s heartbeats missed): "stream stalled";
  - the build unknown, the build query not yet answered on this connection: "build unknown";
  - the build not in `FenixChannelFields.VerifiedBuilds`, an exact match ([FNX-23]): "build <build> not checked";
  - a shape latch, below: "malformed message", "field <path> has the wrong shape", "malformed ECAM entries message" or "ECAM entries field <path> has the wrong shape";
  - the first of the display's own conditions: no powered display unit shows the display (Display live, below): "display off";
  - the PFD only: the latest good frame has no FMA slot at all (Shapes, below): "no FMA";
  - an unknown text code in a line the display covers ([FNX-24]): "unknown code '<letter>' in the FMA", "… in the upper ECAM" or "… in the FOB line"; otherwise the reason is "trusted".
- What each display has. Each display's first condition of its own is that it is live. Then the PFD's are that its frame has an FMA, and then the FMA's 16 texts; the E/WD's are the upper ECAM's left and right lines and the FOB line; the SD, the ND, the ISIS and the DCDU have no other, so being live is their one condition here, and their text is each feature's decoder's to judge.
- Display live. RemoteData field 12 (`FenixChannelFields.Remote.DuData`) is DUData { Power (1) repeated bool, Content (2) repeated DisplayTypeEnum, Brightness (3) repeated double }, one entry per display unit. 3.0.0.7852 added member 4, a switch-on delay per unit, which the app does not read and the decoder skips with the other unknown sub-fields. A power-up from cold and dark on 3.0.0.7852 (kn4iee, 2026-11-13, A320 CFM at a gate) kept each window on "display off" after power came on until it filled in by itself, and `fenix_channel.log` shows the displays turning live one by one rather than all at once: ISIS 19:23:11, DCDU 19:23:38, PFD 19:23:42, E/WD and ND 19:23:43, SD 19:23:45. DisplayTypeEnum, in the contract's order: PFDCapt 0, PFDFO 1, NDCapt 2, NDFO 3, ECAMUpper 4, ECAMLower 5, DiagonalLine 6, SelfTest 7, EcamOnND 8, DCDUCapt 9, DCDUFO 10, DCDUWaitingData 11, SelfTestDCDU 12, DraimsCapt 13, DraimsFo 14, DraimsOvhd 15. A display is live while a powered unit shows it: the PFD on a unit showing PFDCapt, the ND on NDCapt, the E/WD on ECAMUpper, the SD on ECAMLower, the DCDU on DCDUCapt (`FenixChannelFrame.PfdLive`, `NdLive`, `EwdLive`, `SdLive`, `DcduLive`); the ISIS is on no unit and is live while the `Powered` flag of its own message 182 is set (`IsisPowered`); the gate keeps each from the latest frame with its shapes. A display shown only on another unit type (the PFD as PFDFO, an ECAM page as EcamOnND) counts as off, which the owner accepted on 2026-11-03 when a dark display came to open its window on the status row ([FNX-32]). The channel's DCDU unit reads trusted on an airframe with no DCDU (measured 2026-11-03), so the DCDU feature checks `FenixVariant` first. Measured on 3.0.0.7613 at a gate at EDDF on 2026-11-02: the Fenix sends Power and Content unpacked, one varint per element; cold and dark, Power was false on all 11 units and Content DiagonalLine (6) on the six main units, then 9, 10, 13, 14, 15; powered, Power was true on all 11 and Content 0, 2, 3, 1, 4, 5, then 9, 10, 13, 14, 15. The decoder accepts protobuf's packed form of the two lists too, since it encodes the same contract; any other wire type is a wrong shape at "12.1" or "12.2", and field 12 itself not length-delimited is one at "12"; Brightness and unknown sub-fields are skipped. A string, bytes or message field shares wire type 2 with a packed list, so a Power or Content field reshaped into one of them can read as packed varints: "12.1" and "12.2" catch only a fixed32 or fixed64 reshape, the build check ([FNX-23]) covers the rest, and such a misread can only make a display read live, never weaken another check. Power[i] pairs with Content[i] up to the shorter list, and any Power but 0 is on. No field 12, or no unit powered, leaves every display off, and is never a wrong shape. Why the condition exists: cold and dark the channel still sends its altitude and heading, so with every display dark a display would read trusted and a read would speak what no display shows; and a RemoteData holding only fields the app does not read decodes to a frame of defaults, which would read trusted too. "display off" comes first among a display's own conditions.
- Shapes. A malformed message, or a field in the wrong shape, in a data message or an ECAM entries message latches the whole channel until the next connection, and the first cause stays. On a checked build a wrong shape means the check itself cannot be trusted, so it takes down every display, the SD included, not only the one the field belongs to. An FMA of 1 to 15 or of 17 or more slots is such a wrong shape ("field 111 has the wrong shape"); an FMA with no slot at all is not: protobuf-net leaves out an empty or null array, so an unpowered PFD can send none, and a latch would cost every display for the session. The PFD alone reads "no FMA" instead, from the latest good frame, until a frame brings its 16 slots again; it never latches, and the E/WD and the SD keep their verdicts. The entries stream has texts of its own, so a fault in it is never taken for one in the display data. A frame that fails its shape check gives the gate no values: the latch holds every display already. That stream sends only when its list changes, so after each reconnect the monitor gives the gate the last entries message again: a wrong-shaped one that came before the reconnect is still the last thing the stream said, and the gate's fresh start would otherwise erase the latch. A push with no display data (`FenixChannelFrame.NoData`) is not a message to the gate: no shape check and no sign of life, and the monitor keeps the frame before it as the latest, so five seconds of them leave a trusted display "stream stalled" and never latched (the log still takes such a push as the call working).
- No comparison with SimConnect: never judge a display by comparing a value of the channel with SimConnect's. The gate did until 2026-11-04 (the PFD's flight director flags with the FD lights, its true altitude with PLANE ALTITUDE, the PFD's and the ND's heading with the magnetic heading and the E/WD's FOB line with the total fuel, with tolerances, a 3 s hold that latched until reconnect, time alignment, a light test exception and a UI-thread stall guard). Robin had it removed that day: test flights showed that a display's value can differ from SimConnect's for no fault of the channel, which left healthy displays untrusted, and it cost four continuous keys on the 1 Hz batch, a UI-thread heartbeat and a log line every 30 s. What went with it is a net against a field that moved to another number of the same shape among the values it compared: the re-check of [FNX-23] is what finds such a field now. The last commit with the comparisons is 1dc45789b.
- Measured on 3.0.0.7613 at a gate at EDDF on 2026-11-02 (MSFS 2024):
  - The altitude field (197), which the app no longer reads, is the true altitude and never moves with the baro: [FNX-25] has the figures.
  - The fuel: in a five-minute idle run (both engines and the APU running) the FOB line moved in 20 kg steps, from 2,980 to 2,900 kg, and stayed within 12 kg of the sim's total; the line rounds (the sim read 2,998.0 kg while it read 3,000). An instant load of 2,100 kg through the EFB moved both sides within the same second.
  - The heading: cold and dark, 155.5 against the sim's 155.8 degrees. Field 194.1 tracks the sim's magnetic heading from cold and dark on, and nothing in the channel moved through the ADIRS alignment.
  - The flight directors: the channel's FD flags follow the pushbuttons, also with no mode engaged (at power-up the FCU turned both on with every bar NaN, and the sim's lights followed within about a second), and FdInhibit and FdFlash never appeared. In the annunciator light test both FD lamps read exactly 1 with both flight directors off while the flags stayed off.
  - Cold and dark: the FMA is 16 empty slots (not zero slots), the FD bars are NaN and the FD flags absent (off), the FOB amount is amber crosses ("aXXX"), and fwcPhase 6 and fwcPage 12 are stale while the FWCs are unpowered. The altitude and the heading still track the sim's, which is why a display must be live (above).
- The monitor. `FenixChannelMonitor` owns one session of the channel: it runs both calls, decodes each message before taking its one lock and feeds the gate under it, keeps the latest frame (swapped in whole; cleared on each connection of the data call, and replaced only by a frame with data and every shape right, since the gate takes no values from the others), and asks the Fenix for its build through `FenixDataService.GetSoftwareVersionAsync` on each connection of the data call, again every 30 s until it answers. A build answer belongs to the connection that asked: after a newer connection, a disconnect or Dispose it is dropped and never reaches the gate. A reader takes `Snapshot`: a display's verdict, the latest frame, the RemoteData message it was decoded from, the ECAM entries, the clock and the connection number, read under the one lock at the same instant. Nothing in it throws at a caller, speaks or touches a control, and a disposed monitor trusts nothing ("channel stopped").
- Nothing is spoken from an untrusted display. A display key reaches the channel through `TryChannelDisplayRead`, which hands it to the seam of the feature that owns it; a window, a key readout and an announcer each read through one snapshot and act only on its verdict, and a seam that throws leaves the key to its AI read. The verdict and the data come from one snapshot (`FenixChannelMonitor.Snapshot`), taken at one locked instant, so the frame a feature reads is the very frame the gate judged in giving that verdict: never a frame of an earlier connection, never a damaged one, never an older frame beside a verdict that turned trusted on a later one (an unknown code that went away), and never a newer frame beside an older verdict. A fallback to the AI read always beats a possibly wrong exact value.
- The log is `fenix_channel.log` (`Log.Channel("fenix_channel")`, in `%APPDATA%\MSFSBlindAssist\logs`, [CORE-14], [CORE-15]). It holds: each call's connection changes ("display data: connected" is written with a connection's first message, not with the response headers, since a call that opens and says nothing is dropped and asked again; "ECAM entries: connected" with its headers; "not answering (<reason>)", written only when it differs from the last failure written since a message arrived; and "refused (<reason>); stopped for this session", written once, when a refusal of either call ends the channel, [FNX-21]); the build and whether it is checked ("build <build>: checked", or "build <build>: not checked; every display uses the AI read"); each change of a display's verdict with its reason ("PFD trusted", "E/WD not trusted: <reason>"; a change is the trusted flag or the headline of the reason, the text before its first colon, so a failure whose text changes while the channel stays down is one line, and the first tick writes all six displays); "channel stopped" when the monitor is disposed (an aircraft switch or quit), always the last line; and each distinct problem once (a failing build query, a failing tick or build loop). Neither channel call carries a credential. The build query does pass one by: its answer is the 8083 data service's full settings answer, which holds the pilot's credentials ([FNX-19]), but `GetSoftwareVersionAsync` hands the monitor only the build string from it, and that string is all the monitor logs of it, so nothing in the log needs masking.
- Pinned by `FenixChannelTrustTests` (every reason text and the order of the channel-wide conditions, the shape latches and their texts, the stall rule, the unknown-code reasons, "no FMA" for the PFD alone; "display off" for each display cold and dark, before the display's other conditions and after the channel-wide ones (`A_dark_display_is_not_trusted`, `Display_off_comes_before_the_displays_other_conditions`, `A_frame_of_defaults_trusts_no_display`); that no value in a frame untrusts a display (`No_value_in_a_frame_untrusts_a_display`)), `FenixChannelDecoderTests` (`An_fma_without_sixteen_slots_is_a_wrong_shape`, `An_fma_with_no_slots_is_not_a_wrong_shape`; the display units as measured cold and powered, unpacked and packed, a wrong wire type, lists of two lengths and no field 12), `FenixChannelWireTests` (`IsAtEnd`, the end of a packed list) and `FenixChannelMonitorTests` (frames fed to the gate, the latest frame cleared on each connection and never a damaged one, a snapshot's verdict and frame taken at one instant, the build asked on each connection and a stale answer dropped, the ECAM entries latch surviving a reconnect, a push with the displays dark, each log line above); `FenixChannelDefinitionTests` pins that the definition registers no key to compare with (`The_channel_registers_no_simconnect_key_to_compare_with`). What the live channel gives the gate is not pinned: it is read from `fenix_channel.log` in the sim ([CORE-5]). → [a32nx.md](../a32nx.md)

## FNX-23

- Fenix ships its display program and its systems program together, so any Fenix update can renumber the channel's fields without breaking anything of Fenix's: the Fenix goes on working, and the app's reader would go on decoding numbers that now mean something else, and speak them. The shape check catches a changed wire shape, not a field that moved to a number the app reads for something else. So the app trusts a display only on a build whose numbers a developer has compared with that build itself. `FenixChannelFields.VerifiedBuilds` is the list of those builds, spelled as `FenixDataService.GetSoftwareVersionAsync` gives them (for example "3.0.0.7613") and matched exactly, ordinal: "3.0.0.7613 " and "3.0.0.76130" are other builds.
- `VerifiedBuilds` holds two builds, 3.0.0.7613 and 3.0.0.7852 (below). On them the gate goes on to judge each display; on any other build it says "build <build> not checked", the log says "build <build>: not checked; every display uses the AI read", every display key keeps its AI read, no announcement speaks and the session notice tells the pilot so, once per Fenix load (`FenixChannelNotice`).
- First checked build: 3.0.0.7613, confirmed by Robin (the owner) on 2026-11-02, after the in-sim run of that day. Asked "Do you confirm the channel's field numbers were checked against Fenix 3.0.0.7613?", he answered "Yes, confirmed". The field numbers in `FenixChannelFields` came from the 2026-11-01 findings made on 3.0.0.7613, and the run of 2026-11-02 found every field in its expected shape. The change that added the build replaced the test that pinned the empty set with `FenixChannelReadOnlyTests.The_checked_builds_are_the_ones_Robin_confirmed` (named `The_first_checked_build_is_the_one_Robin_confirmed` until the second build joined). A later build joins the set the same way: the re-check below, the owner's confirmation recorded here, and that test changed to name it.
- Second checked build: 3.0.0.7852 (Fenix's update of 2026-11-13), confirmed by Robin on 2026-11-14. Asked "Do you confirm the channel's field numbers were checked against Fenix 3.0.0.7852?", he answered "Yes, confirmed". The re-check of 2026-11-13: the whole contract printed from the installed build and compared with 3.0.0.7613's showed no field number, type or enum value moved or gone, only additions the app does not read (RemoteData 207 to 212, the terrain alert's state; DUData 4, a switch-on delay per unit; three new types; four window blind entities). On an A320 IAE at the gate, live pushes read through the app's decoder, gate and window composers gave all six displays and all twelve SD pages with no wrong shape and no unknown letter, and the app's log read "build 3.0.0.7852: checked", then the six displays "trusted", nothing latched. kn4iee's run of this change's build on 2026-11-13, at a gate, logged the same, and its display windows read; the same day a power-up from cold and dark passed: each window stayed on "display off" until its screen was up (DUData 4, [FNX-22] has the times). The flight check followed on 2026-11-14: Robin flew an A320 CFM from EGPF to LOWW, cold and dark to parked, ending in a dual-autopilot ILS autoland, with the team's recorders running (every FMA change, the ECAM entries, and a whole push every 500 ms, 24,493 of them). The FMA's slot layout was the one measured on 2026-11-04 ([FNX-42]): 16 slots, every mode from SRS to LAND, FLARE and ROLL OUT in the slot it had on 3.0.0.7613, field 112 at 1 from LAND until after the rollout, and the texts the reference flight had not shown (RWY, A FLOOR, MAN PITCH TRIM ONLY, ALT with G/S armed) in the slots their columns give them. Every recorded push, read afterwards through the app's decoder, gate and window composers, gave no exception, no wrong shape, no unknown letter and no FMA slot without a place; each display went from "display off" to "trusted" at power-up and stayed there. The ND read its messages, wind, speeds, ILS and each waypoint of the route's top right block, and the DCDU read real CPDLC messages (two logons, a descent clearance, a frequency change, WILCO with SENDING and RECEIVD BY ATC). Both streams stayed connected throughout, and the app's log held only notes it also wrote on 3.0.0.7613. Still not covered: the ND's map points and traffic, which come from a call the recorders do not capture (the app's log of the flight shows no fault with them, which is not a check), and the terrain alert's members (207 to 212), which never left their defaults because no alert fired. The app does not read them at all, a known gap noted in the ND section of [a32nx.md](../a32nx.md).
- The re-check, after a Fenix update that changes the build, in this order:
  - Compare every call path, field number and wire shape in `FenixChannelFields` and its feature partials (`FenixChannelFields.Pfd.cs` and the others) against the installed build (the four call paths, each field a decoder reads with the shape it expects, and the FMA's 16 slots), using the team's local channel tools, and fix any number that moved there alone: no other file names a call path or a field number. Record the FMA's slot layout in flight, as it was on 2026-11-04 ([FNX-42]), since `FenixChannelFmaLayout` rests on it. The tools read Fenix's own program files and the live channel, so they and everything they produce (the decoded contract, captures, the field map) stay outside this repository and are never committed.
  - Add the build to `VerifiedBuilds` in the working copy only, build the app and run it in the sim. The in-sim check cannot be made before this: on a build outside the set the gate answers "build <build> not checked" for every display before it judges anything else, so the log shows no "trusted" and no latch for it.
  - Read `fenix_channel.log`: "build <build>: checked", the six displays "trusted" once they are powered ("display off" while dark), and no shape latch.
  - Commit the addition only when all of that holds and the owner confirms. Otherwise take it out of the working copy again: the build stays on the AI read.
- Never commit a build on a version bump alone, and never loosen a check to make a build pass. A build that fails a check is the check doing its work. The nets are separate, and neither is complete alone: the build check keeps every display of a build no developer has re-checked on the AI read; and the shape check latches the channel when a field arrives in another wire shape. A field that moved to another number of the same shape passes the shape check, and only the re-check finds it, so committing a build without the re-check would let the app speak what a stale field number reads. A failing build stays on the AI read until its numbers are re-checked and fixed in `FenixChannelFields`.
- Pinned by `FenixChannelReadOnlyTests.The_checked_builds_are_the_ones_Robin_confirmed` (the set is exactly 3.0.0.7613 and 3.0.0.7852), `FenixChannelTrustTests` (`An_unchecked_build_is_not_trusted`, `A_build_must_match_a_checked_one_exactly`, `An_empty_checked_list_trusts_nothing`, and `The_channel_wide_reasons_come_in_the_order_of_the_table`, in which an unchecked build outranks the shape latch) and `FenixChannelMonitorTests.A_build_the_app_has_not_checked_is_logged_as_such`. That a developer really re-checked the numbers cannot be pinned: it is the owner's confirmation. → [a32nx.md](../a32nx.md)

## FNX-24

- The channel's text carries a lowercase letter before a run as a format code, and `FenixChannelText.Parse` turns one line into runs, each with its text, color, underline, box, flashing and size. The parser knows fifteen letters and one code. Seven letters are colors: `w` white, `g` green, `c` cyan, `a` amber, `m` magenta, `y` yellow and `r` red (all but red are the letters `FenixMcduFormat` also handles for the Fenix MCDU markup). `u`, `b` and `f` toggle underline, a box and flashing, and `s` and `l` set small and large. `p`, `i` and `d` are known and never spoken, and `x1`, the letter x with exactly the digit 1, is one known code that draws nothing. A box and flashing are attributes like underline, never colors: the color in force does not change. Display text is uppercase, so an uppercase letter is never a code, and a lowercase letter outside a to z is text. U+2800, the blank braille cell the display uses as a spacer, becomes a space. A run before any color code has no color. Each code holds until another changes it, and a code that restates the line's current color or size does not split a run (`u`, `b` and `f` always do, since they toggle); no run is empty. Where each letter has been seen in fields the app reads, on 3.0.0.7613 by 2026-11-02:
  - `w`, `g` and `c` everywhere in the FMA and the upper ECAM;
  - `a` in the amber cautions, and in the cold-and-dark FOB amount ("aXXX");
  - `r` in the TO CONFIG test ("rFLAPS NOT IN T.O CONFIG", below) and in an engine fire ("urENG 1 FIREu ");
  - `m` in the magenta "mT.O INHIBIT" in the upper ECAM's right column, at the take-off roll (18:31:40 on 2026-11-02);

- Why the codes are data. On the displays the color is part of what a line says: red, amber, cyan, magenta, green and white tell a warning from a caution, a memo or an action. Stripped to plain text, a line would sound the same whatever it means. So the codes are parsed, never stripped for speech: `FenixTextLine.JoinedText` (the runs' text without color, underline, box, flashing or size) is only for parsing a number out of a line (the FOB), for a key readout's plain value and for the log. The reads of this repository speak each run's color through `FenixChannelSpeech` (`Line(parts)` for text drawn in pieces): stretches merge by color alone, a part's leading colorless run is spoken white when the part holds a color letter (checked: every line starts in the color of `w`), and a part with none stays colorless. How they word the colors was settled by ear. A key readout (Shift+1 to Shift+6, Ctrl+W, D) speaks plain values parsed out of channel text, never colors, and a row the app builds from values or flags speaks no color. The ND and the DCDU speak through their own rules, below.
- The ND's exception (the owner's decision, 2026-11-04). The ND window and its announcer (`FenixChannelNdSpeech`, `FenixNdLookEntry.ColorTellsApart`) speak a color only where it tells something: amber and red always; another color only when it is not one of its line's usual colors, or when it tells map items apart. Why: the ND's text lines keep fixed colors. Measured over about 8,000 pushes of two flights, the colors never changed within a line except the messages' amber and the failure flags' red, so speaking them was noise to a blind pilot. The codes are still parsed (never stripped), and an unknown code still makes the whole ND not available.
- The DCDU's exception (the owner's ruling, 2026-11-03). The DCDU window and its announcement speak no color names, as the MCDU window does, since the DCDU mostly carries text (`FenixChannelDcdu.Plain`). Unknown letters still make the display not available.
- A letter the parser does not know is dropped from the text and listed on the parsed line (`FenixTextLine.UnknownCodes`, once each, in the order first used). The gate does not trust a display while any line it covers holds one: the FMA's 16 slots for the PFD; the upper ECAM left and right lines and the FOB line for the E/WD ("unknown code 'x' in the upper ECAM"). A letter the app does not know means the line is drawn in a way the app cannot tell, perhaps in a color that matters. It does not latch ([FNX-22]): it is an exact condition, not a noisy one, and trust returns when the line goes away.
- Red's letter is `r`, measured on 3.0.0.7613 on 2026-11-02 with the capture this rule waited for: the TO CONFIG test with the flaps up, which lights a red warning ([FNX-4] measured the warning lighting), sent "urCONFIGu ", "rSLATS NOT IN T.O CONFIG", "urCONFIGu " and "rFLAPS NOT IN T.O CONFIG" above the amber gear lines (the format codes are plain lowercase letters, and `u` toggles the underline). The same holds for any letter: one joins the list only after a capture shows what it draws, never by analogy with the MCDU markup or another display.
- The box and flash letters were seen in flight on 3.0.0.7613 with an A321 on 2026-11-02. At the take-off roll the FMA's slot 0 read "wbMAN" (slot 5 below it "FLX c+56"). After the thrust reduction slot 10 read "wfLVR CLB" (captured at 18:32:34, with "wbMAN" still in slot 0), and after an engine failure "wfLVR MCT". What `b` and `f` mean is inferred from what the real A320 shows at those moments (MAN FLX boxed, LVR CLB and LVR MCT flashing), not confirmed from what the Fenix draws. That is the one exception to the rule above that a letter joins the list only after a capture shows what it draws (ruled 2026-11-02): `b` and `f` are known, so a display that shows them stays trusted, since a letter seen in a field the app reads is never left "unknown"; but what they draw is not confirmed on the Fenix, so no read may speak "boxed" or "flashing" until a capture shows it.
