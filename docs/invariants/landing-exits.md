# Landing exits: measurement, planner and re-plan — rules in full

Each section is the complete text of one rule. Its one-line form, under the same ID, is in `.claude/rules/landing-exits.md`, which Claude Code loads when it reads matching code. Background: [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md).
The text is verbatim from CLAUDE.md as of `1f37801a`; a trailing "→ doc" pointer is the original's. Cross-references such as "the bullet below", "above" or "under Core" point at CLAUDE.md's old single list, whose rules now live in several files: search `docs/invariants/` for the rule's key name to find it.

## EXIT-1

- Landing Exit Planner's `SIM_ON_GROUND` handler must always use `RequestAircraftPositionAsync`, never trust `LastKnownPosition` — that cache can be stale from a prior mode and silently fails the GS≥40kt "real landing" gate. → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md)

## EXIT-2

- `SetExit(..., currentlyAirborne)` must be armed from the actual air/ground state, never unconditionally true — unconditional true would false-trigger the exit plan during a high-speed taxi or rejected takeoff. → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md)

## EXIT-3

- The landing-exit plan's runway must be checked against the runway actually landed on BEFORE it becomes the rollout's measurement frame (issue #234: a 12L plan landed on 30L at OMDB handed off to Taxiing on the rollout's first frame, with no callout at all). `Navigation/LandingRunwayMatch` identifies the runway by ALIGNMENT (heading within 45°; the best-aligned wins, and among ends within 3° of it the smallest cross-track wins — near-parallel ends differ by hundredths of a degree) among the runway ends that are LANDABLE (`IsLandable` — never a closed record, never a water runway; the pilot's own runway is always judged whatever the database says) and whose pavement contains the aircraft (laterally by `RolloutExitGate.IsLaterallyClearOfRunway`, the SAME test the rollout uses one frame later — a separate spelling let the app believe the aircraft was on the runway and clear of it at once) — never by pavement alone, because at an intersection the aircraft is on both (KDCA 04/01, KPHL 17/27R) — and reports `ReciprocalEnd` only for the planned runway's own twin (`IsTwin`), never a crossing runway. On another runway or the other end the planner RE-PLANS on the runway actually landed on (`LandingExitReplan`, two passes: usable = a turn of at most 90° and comfortably reachable — `RolloutExitGate.ComfortableExitLeadFeet`, 2 s then 2.0 m/s² down to the exit's turn-off speed — first, and `RolloutExitGate.ExitLeadFeet` only in the fallback pass after the rescue scan, because that floor was tuned below 50 kt and asks 4-6 m/s² at touchdown speed; the pilot's own exit counts on the OTHER END only, as the same node or the same name within 1,400 ft and only on the same physical side, since `GetLandingExits` usually keeps a different node per end and 16% of same-name exits leave the other way) and never reuses exit geometry measured from the other threshold; the runway-end countdown is only the fallback with no usable exit, and its entry (`BeginRunwayEndCountdownRollout`) must hand over the taxi graph, data provider and ICAO and START the steering tone, or the backtrack that follows is silent. `Unknown` must never hand the planned frame to the rollout, and must NOT consume the plan (`LandingExitActivationPolicy`): it rests on ONE position sample at the ground bit, so latching it made a bounce, a touch-and-go or a go-around fly the next approach with no exit guidance and no second word — the message is latched once per plan instead, and no longer claims the plan was cancelled. The countdown ends by POSITION (`RunwayEndCountdownGate`), never on any stop or turn — that rule told a pilot vacating mid-runway to turn around — and NEVER on a turn near the end either: between 15° and `TurnedAroundMinDeg` a turn-off at the runway end and the start of a turnaround are indistinguishable, and `BacktrackingOnRunway` latches so the `Vacated` arm can never take a wrong call back, so only a STOP within `RolloutExitGate.NearRunwayEndFeet` commits. That window is a guidance constant of its own, never `DistanceMilestones`, which is built from the pilot's feet/metres setting. Distance-to-end comes from `RunwayFrame.DistanceToEnd` so a `length`-0 row falls back to threshold-to-threshold — those rows are exactly the ones that reach the countdown, since the exit finders skip them, and reading the column raw gave a NEGATIVE distance (`Stop.` at touchdown speed, no countdown at all, first stop anywhere called the end). The backtrack heading is spoken MAGNETIC. The correction is ONE utterance with the touchdown sentence, through `AnnounceInstruction`, and every milestone reached within its MEASURED 9 s lead (7.51 s at SAPI Rate 0, plus about a fifth) is retired and folded into it (`TouchdownCallout`) — the lead converts to a distance through `RolloutCalloutSupersession.ReachFeet`, which BRAKES toward `RolloutExitGate.TaxiGroundSpeedKts` rather than holding touchdown speed flat (flat overstated the reach by hundreds of feet and wrote off all three approach calls on a 2,600 ft exit) and stops there rather than at a standstill (the crossing-decline sentence is spoken from 0 to 90 kt, where assuming heavy braking understates it), never left to `AnnounceImmediate` over it — except turn-now, which is retired only when the aircraft is already inside it and so can still interrupt the sentence. The manual landing assist switches its tones to the actual runway for one engagement (`LandingAssistRunwaySwitch`) and restores the armed runway after, but at TOUCHDOWN it must not SPEAK the correction while a plan is pending (`exitPlanWillSayIt`) — both features notice the change and speak hundredths of a second apart, the planner interrupting, so the assist's sentence was cut mid-word and under a silent flare that was the only telling the pilot would have had. The re-plan screens candidates through `LandingExitVacateScreen` so it PREFERS an exit mapped clear of the runway, as the planner dialog does (a flagged exit is still offered when it is all there is). A database switch must clear the plan AND disarm the assist (`RefreshDatabaseProvider`): both captured a runway list the new database can name or place differently. Only the two rollout entries reached without a `Taxiing` transition raise `PositionStreamRequired` — never restart the position stream on every `LandingRollout` entry. → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md)

## EXIT-4

- The overshoot handler must NOT answer "any exit left?" from `_rolloutAllExits` alone — `GetLandingExits` is built for the planner DIALOG and is lossy on purpose: one entry per taxiway name, and `hasHoldShortOnRunway` switches the geometric fallback off for the WHOLE runway the moment ONE corridor node carries a hold-short marker, so every RET the scenery left unmarked (they are one-way, so they usually are) stops existing. When it comes back empty, ask the graph via `TaxiGraph.FindDownfieldExits` BEFORE announcing "Missed last exit" — that verdict's only remedy is a 180 on active pavement (CYYZ 23: six exits ending at the missed H2, with 5,400 ft of runway and H4/J2/H6 ahead). Measured: the rescue fires on 25 of 558 swept runway directions and is silent on the other 533, so a well-mapped runway is untouched. Rescue findings must go through `RolloutExitGate.MergeRescueExits`, never straight into `_rolloutAllExits` — that is what drops a rediscovered node of the arc just missed (five of them on the CYYZ H2 arc, nearest 526 ft on) and keeps the list nearest-first for the fall-forward/undershoot/early-vacate readers. In that merge a known TURNAROUND never covers a rescued FORWARD exit of its name — every picker skips the turnaround, so dropping the forward exit beside it left the pilot with neither (measured 2026-09-26: 4 runway directions gain an exit, none loses one). The rescue never offers a node beyond the pavement an aircraft cannot reach on paved ground (`IsRescueCandidateSite`): every edge within `TaxiGraph.ParallelTaxiwayMaxDeg` (5°) of the axis (S36 15's parallel, called a 0.2° "high-speed exit" with turn-now pointing across the grass), or a taxiway that does not lead onto the runway (`ExitBranch.LeadsOntoRunway`: its own pavement stops more than a 2 m seam short of the runway's, and walked inward by steps angled over 5° it stops on a line along the runway — NC12 26's A, bending 6.7°, and SC41 33's B where an apron loop leaves it at 48°). Never judge a site by its steepest edge alone — a parallel's bend and an apron loop are steep too — nor by the walk alone: a parallel whose pavement meets the runway's is reachable (4AK6 19's CC). A taxiway stopped short of the runway edge, a fork, an edge crossing the runway or a rapid exit's arc leads onto it and stays. Both overshoot sites — the LandingRollout detector and the post-handoff monitor — pick through the one `PickOvershootRetarget` (the aircraft-relative cutoff, the rescue, the verdict logged with its list). → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md)

## EXIT-5

- The exit LIST must be logged, not just its count, at `BeginLandingRollout` and at the no-exit verdict (`DescribeExits`) — the CYYZ verdict was reached inside a loop that recorded nothing about what it considered, and no amount of log reading could settle whether the list or the scan was at fault. → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md)

## EXIT-6

- **Exits are measured by their whole BRANCH (`Navigation/ExitBranch`) in every producer** (`GetLandingExits`' main and fallback passes and `FindDownfieldExits`, all through `TaxiGraph.RefineExitByBranch`), never by the first named edge. This SUPERSEDES the old rule that the angle is the first named edge's (the bullet that stood here said the shallow-angle override must leave `ExitAngleDegrees` alone): that rule called a curved 73° fillet "High-speed 14°" (KMEM 36L M5) and folded a 128° hairpin to 52°, offering the 18R arm of a Y-shaped exit to a 36L landing (KMEM M6, 2026-09-26: "Turn right now" at 49 kt, the tone steered to 127° true, about 13 s in the grass). The branch: an inward walk into the 5 m centreline band, then back toward the threshold along a SIMPLE line only (it starts only at a node with ≤ 2 walkable neighbours, continues only through degree-2 nodes, ends at — and includes — the first node where the line meets other pavement or ends, and never follows more than `BandWalkMaxMetres`, 150 m) — that end is the junction; the measured path runs from it to the first node 10 m past the runway edge (`RolloutExitGate.RunwayClearMarginM`), and `ExitAngleDegrees` = the sharpest turn from the landing heading along it, read over strokes of at least `ExitBranch.MinStrokeMetres` (5 m, `StrokeAt`) so a 2–4 m navdata jog never sets a whole branch's angle, capped at 90°. Once off the pavement a branch never walks back over the runway (each path carries the side it left on — GMMN 17L A measured the far side), its corridor node must descend from its clear node (KACK 24 A took another arm's), a node already off the pavement is never read as leaving backward, a crossing seeded on its backward half is measured on its FORWARD half (`ExitBranch.ForwardHalf`), and every producer seeds with the one `BestExitEdge` rule. How steeply the exit leaves its OWN node (`LandingExit.DivergenceAngleDegrees`) is what the overshoot margin and the exit-alignment test read (`OvershootMarginFor`, `IsAlignedWithExit`), never the branch's sharpest turn, which shrank a rapid exit's overshoot margin to 100 ft (EDDB 24L M3: 292 ft). A TURNAROUND is judged by how the branch LEAVES the pavement (`LandingExitBranch.TurnToLeaveDeg`, up to the first node beyond the half-width), never by what the taxiways do past the edge (1,394 real 90° exits that hook back beyond it read as turnarounds that way); above 110° (`TurnaroundAboveDeg`) it is recorded as 130° "End" (`TurnaroundExitAngleDeg`), which every "> 90°" filter skips. The inward walk AND the outward search stay on the exit's own taxiway (unnamed or same-name edges, compared by `ExitBranch.SameTaxiwayName`: letters and digits, case-insensitively, invariant culture — an online spelling of the exit's own name is its own taxiway), or exits are measured along ANOTHER exit's arm (368 were; KMIA 08R M7 took M6's). NO RELOCATION: an exit keeps its producer node, position and distances; the refinement changes only `ExitAngleDegrees`, `ExitType` (`ClassifyExit` at the exit's own along-track position) and `ExitBearingTrue`/`ExitSide` (`BranchExitBearing`, at the exit's own place on its branch, `PlaceOf`: the edge from it on the pavement, the edge into it once off the pavement; an exit whose own stretch runs along the runway steers toward where the branch clears, KSFB 36 C) — moving exits to their junctions or lead-in starts put "turn now" hundreds of feet early (up to 150 m at KMIA 08R Z, 777 ft for a sibling's lead-in start) and then a false "missed exit", and dropped about 1,300 distinct exits through the same-name coverage window. When the swap finds nothing, the branch is read again from the exit's OWN node (`ExitBranch.FromExitNode`: the strokes from that node to the edge, or the stroke crossing it) and an exit leaving forward that way is kept forward at its node with that angle, flagged `LandingExit.ForwardOnlyFromItsNode` — the junction the inward walk reached can lie past the lead-in's own start, so the branch runs backward over pavement never driven (SBGL 15 F: 178° back from its junction, 47° off the runway from its node; 299 exits across the database). The sibling is always tried FIRST (RJTT 34L A8 keeps its High-speed 23° sibling), a flagged exit never replaces or covers an unflagged forward exit of its name in any dedup (`ReplacesInDedupWindow`; KLIT 22R's D RET was displaced 700 ft late), and the hold-short gate keeps the JUNCTION reading (EGAA 35 5 exits → 1 otherwise) — never let the node reading reach the gate or the sibling decision. The ONE move is a sibling swap: a turnaround is replaced by its forward sibling (`ExitBranch.FindForwardSibling`: same side, never across the runway, junction within `SiblingJunctionMaxMetres`, 300 m, along the runway, unnamed or same-name edges only, not itself a turnaround), landing on the sibling arm's DIVERGENCE node — the last node inside the 5 m band — never its lead-in start. Nothing is silently dropped: an UNMEASURED branch (its own taxiway never reaches or never clears the runway within reach) changes nothing; a turnaround whose sibling is missing or fails the distance rules stays 130° / "End" at its own node (only the rescue scan drops it); a recorded turnaround never hides or covers a forward exit of its own name in the same-name dedup and coverage fill. The refinement never changes WHICH exits exist: the hold-short gate (`hasHoldShortOnRunway`) counts a node forward only when its own branch is not a turnaround and must NEVER consult siblings (38 directions lost their unmarked exits, CYVR 26L 12 → 1), and `hsOnlyEnds` reads the PRODUCER's unrefined types (KPWK 34 7 → 1) — over the refined list, deliberately: judged on the producer's own list, 16 directions go back to hold-short mode and lose 54 exits (KDTW 09L 11 → 2) while none gains one. The producers' shallow-angle overrides still adjust only `ExitBearingTrue`, and a measured forward exit replaces that bearing with its branch's. Validated with `tools/LandingExitSweep` (standalone, not in the solution) before/after over the whole database — usable exits 42,470 on main → 40,823 (2026-09-26, after the PR #252 review fixes), 0 directions losing every exit; re-run it before trusting any change here. Known residuals: EDDK 24 (an unnamed arm of another exit can still be measured), same-name coverage drops where every connector carries one name, KMCI 01L (the per-name High-speed dedup), and the turnaround test reading a hook or zig-zag on the runway surface as leaving backward (SBGL 15 F: a 44 m back-and-forth walk makes a 47–72° exit a 130° turnaround; 502 turnarounds cross the pavement edge at 110° or less, and 159 directions left with no usable exit hold one). → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md)

## EXIT-7

- The Landing Exit planner never pre-selects a turnaround while a forward exit exists (`Navigation.LandingExitDefault`: the first exit that gets clear of the runway with an angle ≤ 90°, else the first with an angle ≤ 90°, else the first that gets clear, else the first — the touchdown re-plan never guides to a turnaround either) — KMEM 36L's list opens with M3, an 18R high-speed exit that is a 130° turnaround for 36L at 1,400 ft. The online taxiway-name refresh restores the pilot's pick by node, else the nearest same-named exit of the same kind (`LandingExitDefault.RestoreIndex`), never the first entry of the name, which moved a pick of C@3281 onto the C@2231 turnaround with nothing said. → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md)
