# Taxi Turn picks and the off-chart leg: rules in full

Each section is the complete text of one rule. Its one-line form, under the same ID, is in `.claude/rules/taxi-turn-picks.md`, which Claude Code loads when it reads matching code. Background: [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md).

## TURN-1

- **Turn picks are applied after the default route, never inside it.** `TurnPickApplier.Apply` takes the route `FindConstrainedPathCore` built exactly as before and rewrites it row by row, so a clearance with every Turn on Automatic is byte-identical to the route without the feature. Threading a pick into the constrained search would change every route (the search's tie-breaks and its fallback to shortest path are measured, RTE-33). `TaxiRouter.BlockedNodes` is how a pick keeps the rest of the route from coming back through the junction; its searches skip those nodes, and it is empty by default, so the check costs nothing without a pick. What a block does to gap chaining is TURN-5. Never add a pick parameter to the router's own entry points. → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md)

## TURN-2

- **Every gate of `OffChartLeg.Judge` is load-bearing.** A leg is 5 to 150 m, leaves at most 25 degrees to the other side of the incoming heading and 100 degrees to the picked side, turns 20 to 120 degrees onto the taxiway, keeps the two turns under 170 degrees, lies on mapped pavement at both ends and every 5 m, and touches no runway it does not start on. Measured at KBOS 2026-11-14 ("H, left on C"): the 126 m diagonal across the corner passes every shape gate and is refused ONLY by the pavement gate; the legs that would re-enter 04R from the connector start are refused ONLY by the runway gate. Loosening either puts a tone on grass or on an active runway. The runway gate is "any runway the leg does not start on", not just the one being crossed. → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md)

## TURN-3

- **A recalculation never judges a Turn pick again from where the aircraft is.** A pick is resolved once, at load. `TryRecalculateRoute` reads its phase from the loaded route: ahead, it rebuilds only the part up to the turn and keeps the turn and everything after it as loaded (`TurnPickCarryOver.TailFrom`), and a pick that is not kept is always said ("could not be kept"), never dropped silently; in the turn (between the junction and the taxiway), it counts as missed and "Route changed" says so; made, its junction stays blocked for every later recalculation so none routes back through it. `TurnPickCarryOver.BlocksToKeep` returns the running set union the blocks of every pick made now and removes nothing: once a made pick has left the route, that set is the only place its junction survives, so removing the start node there (the first cut did) unblocked the junction for good after one recalculation that started at it. A recalculation exempts its start node on its own router only, and its "no route with the blocks" retry runs without blocks for that recalculation only; the committed set stays whole in both cases. A recalculation in which a pick was missed, or in which a pick still ahead is not kept, replaces a route the pilot has left at the turn, one whose taxiway names usually match the charted rebuild: the no-op guard (RTE-22) never discards it, and for a missed pick the sanity gate's backwards-bearing indicator (RTE-18) does not reject it, while its length blow-up indicator still does. Keeping the old route there steers back toward an off-chart leg that is gated only from its start. With no pick in play both run exactly as before. The blocked set changes only when a route is adopted, and a refused `LoadRoute` restores it (RTE-29). Re-judging from the aircraft would let the aircraft's drift reroute the pilot's named turn, or undo a turn already made.

  Three refinements, from the code review of 2026-11-17. **A corner cut is a turn made, not missed:** a pick the index has not yet counted as made while the aircraft is already on its taxiway (`TurnPickCarryOver.MadeByCut`: within the off-route tolerance of the taxiway's first 80 m from the join, heading within 45 degrees of it) is neither missed nor ahead. It says nothing, keeps its junction closed like a made pick, and leaves the no-op guard and the sanity gate as they are; otherwise a corner cut onto the picked taxiway, the usual trigger of RTE-22, said "was missed" with a spurious "Route changed". This decides whether the turn happened; it never picks a turn again. **A kept tail never takes the aircraft round again:** a rebuilt prefix that runs through the loaded tail it joins (the aircraft cut onto the picked taxiway before the index reached the junction) would take it back to the junction to turn again, so a kept-tail route that uses a node twice is not kept (`VisitsANodeTwice`, as at load, TURN-4). **A turn at the route's very first node is not made by loading it:** a pick whose `FirstTaxiwaySegment` is 0 reads as made from the first frame, so `BlocksToKeep` commits its junction only once the index has left segment 0; a pilot who turns the wrong way there can still be routed back through it. → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md)

## TURN-4

- **A pick is honored only by a route that itself makes the turn.** The junction block alone lets the router "honor" a turn the destination does not lie on by turning the picked way and then finding its way back to the charted side: `FindConstrainedPathCore` tries up to ten entries onto the row's taxiway, each reached by a free search, so the remainder leaves the taxiway and re-enters it beyond the block. VirtualPilot's `turnpicks` mode (2026-11-15, 10,008 picks on the 100 busiest MSFS 2024 airports) flew these shapes, and `TurnPickApplier.Complete` refuses each as `DoesNotLead` by looking at the STITCHED route, not at the option it started from:
  - **Doubling back.** LTFM "right onto N1": 65 degrees right onto one branch of a fork, then 153 degrees the other way 55 m on. `BuiltRouteMakesTheTurn`: the route must be a clean turn to the picked side along the row's own taxiway (`TurnShape.IsCleanTurn`; for an off-chart leg, from the leg onto the taxiway), and no heading change within 80 m of the join may exceed 120 degrees, whatever the next edge is named.
  - **Out and straight back.** ZBAD "right onto L2": 116 m west along the next taxiway to the node the router had picked to leave it by, and back east over the same five nodes. Each leg the router builds is a simple path; joined, they are not. A stitched route that uses a node twice is refused (`VisitsANodeTwice`); 487 of the picks still honored at that point did this somewhere.
  - **Back past its own junction.** A turn, and straight back beside it on other nodes. A route that, 60 m or more beyond the join, passes within 30 m of the junction it turned at is refused (`ComesBackPastTheJunction`).

  Step 1 ("already so") walks the same row-only path (`LegsThroughRow`): walking 80 m along the route let a turn onto the NEXT taxiway count as this row's, so a straight-on join followed by a left turn onto the next row read as "left, already made" and the pick vanished without a word. → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md)

## TURN-5

- **A blocked node never switches gap chaining off for its whole taxiway.** `AStarSearchWithGapChaining` used to return null whenever any node of the required taxiway was blocked, on the reasoning that a hop to "the next section" would step over the block. A pick's own join node is always on its row's taxiway, so that rule switched chaining off for EVERY remainder, and the remainder failed wherever the taxiway had a navdata gap anywhere ahead. Flown by VirtualPilot on the whole airport, the KBOS incident route ("H, left on C" to runway 33L) was refused "does not lead to Runway 33L": the real taxiway C has 400 m with no name 900 m east of the junction (nodes 339 to 348). The unit-test replica of the junction had no such gap and passed. Now a gap is chained as always, and only a hop that would step over a block is refused (`HopStepsOverABlock`): onto a blocked node, into a section that runs on behind a block (`SectionsBehindBlocks`), or to a node on the far side of a blocked node of the taxiway (seen from the block, the hop's two ends lie more than 90 degrees apart, and the block lies within `HopBlockCorridorMinMeters`, 50 m, or a quarter of the hop if that is more, of the hop's line), which also covers a section behind the block that a gap of its own detaches. The corridor was added in the code review of 2026-11-17: a pick's whole other arm is blocked, and on a curving taxiway some of it falls inside the circle on the hop as diameter while lying well off to the side; the 90-degree test alone then refused a hop nothing stood in the way of. All of it is behind `BlockedNodes.Count > 0`. Measured: 30 of 396 "does not lead to" refusals in the sweep were this defect. `KbosHcJunctionFixture.Build(eastGap: true)` now carries the gap, with twelve nodes before it, because the router's ten nearest entry candidates must all lie short of it as they do at the airport. → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md)

## TURN-6

- **The rest of a picked route is searched with more than the junction closed.** `Complete` closes, for that one search:
  - **the nodes the route has already been through** (the prefix). WMKK "left onto G": left, 50 m of G, back up another arm of the previous taxiway to a node the route had passed, and down the charted side;
  - **the charted way the pick declined**, the charted route's nodes up to and including its own join (`chartedBeforeJoin`). ZJHK "right onto C": an off-chart leg beside the stand's charted lead-in, 37 m of C, then up that lead-in to the taxiway before and onto C the charted way, a loop guidance itself then skipped, steering onto the charted way with the pick still reading as made;
  - **the taxiway's other arm** (`OtherArm`): every node reached along the taxiway's own edges from the join the way the pick did NOT turn, and not also reached the picked way (a taxiway that is a ring has none). This one is the owner's ruling (Robin, 2026-11-15), and it replaces the first design's "long way round": a pick was honored when the destination could be reached by turning the picked way, leaving the taxiway and coming back onto its other arm, inside the length bound. VirtualPilot showed what that is in practice: 379 of the 1,013 picks still honored travelled along the other arm and 169 more crossed it, and KCLE "left onto G7" at a runway exit went back across the runway just vacated and round to G7's other arm, where a recalculation on that lap began the lap again (the made junction is closed, and the recalculated route takes its start on the near arm). The destination itself stays open, so a destination that stands on the other arm is still reached from another taxiway when there is a way (`At_a_crossing_node_left_takes_the_east_arm_the_long_way_round`).

  A later pick's search has the earlier picks' other arms closed as well (`Apply` adds them to its running set). None of the three is in a pick's `BlockedNodeIds`, which stay the departure node, the connection and the join: that is what a recalculation keeps closed (TURN-3), and it must not grow. A pick counts as made once the segment index passes its join, about 25 m before a crossing junction, so a pilot who then takes the other arm after all is "made" by the index; with that arm closed, the recalculation could not route along the taxiway the aircraft is on. Closing the arm also lets the router find the clean way round where there is one: it otherwise takes the taxiway's node nearest the destination as its target, which is often on the other arm. What the rule costs: an explicit clearance whose later taxiways cross the picked taxiway's other arm at a junction gets "does not lead to" for that pick. Not seen in the sweep's clearances, which are shortest routes; it is the price of the ruling. → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md)

## TURN-7

- **A pick never adds a stretch along a runway.** A crossing puts 45 to 60 m of route on a runway's pavement; where the scenery draws taxi lines along a runway, the router uses them like any other edge, and the hold passes have no good stop for a route that is driven along a runway (the known class behind VirtualPilot's `HOLD_STOP_ON_RUNWAY_PAVEMENT` and `RUNWAY_ENTERED_UNHELD`, with or without picks). OMDB "left onto N" was honored by a route that went onto runway 12L/30R at N8 with "Passing the runway 30R at N8 hold line", ran 370 m along it, and only then met a hold: unheld, and with no warning in the summary. `Complete` refuses a stitched route whose longest stretch on one runway's pavement (`LongestRunAlongARunwayMeters`, both ends of each segment on it) is over 100 m and more than 50 m longer than the charted route's own. A runway the destination stands on is not counted: the route ends there by design. The charted route may already drive along a runway (KLAX H8/H9, EGKK 08L used as a taxiway); that is not this rule's business, only making it worse is. With it the same pick gets a 70 m off-chart leg and a route that flies clean. → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md)
