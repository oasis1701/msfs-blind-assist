# Taxi Turn picks and the off-chart leg: rules in full

Each section is the complete text of one rule. Its one-line form, under the same ID, is in `.claude/rules/taxi-turn-picks.md`, which Claude Code loads when it reads matching code. Background: [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md).

## TURN-1

- **Turn picks are applied after the default route, never inside it.** `TurnPickApplier.Apply` takes the route `FindConstrainedPathCore` built exactly as before and rewrites it row by row, so a clearance with every Turn on Automatic is byte-identical to the route without the feature. Threading a pick into the constrained search would change every route (the search's tie-breaks and its fallback to shortest path are measured, RTE-33). `TaxiRouter.BlockedNodes` is how a pick keeps the rest of the route from coming back through the junction; its searches skip those nodes, and it is empty by default, so the check costs nothing without a pick. What a block does to gap chaining is TURN-5. Never add a pick parameter to the router's own entry points. → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md)

## TURN-2

- **Every gate of `OffChartLeg.Judge` is load-bearing.** A leg is 5 to 150 m, leaves at most 25 degrees to the other side of the incoming heading and 100 degrees to the picked side, turns 20 to 120 degrees onto the taxiway, keeps the two turns under 170 degrees, lies on mapped pavement at both ends and every 5 m, and touches no runway it does not start on. Measured at KBOS 2026-11-14 ("H, left on C"): the 126 m diagonal across the corner passes every shape gate and is refused ONLY by the pavement gate; the legs that would re-enter 04R from the connector start are refused ONLY by the runway gate. Loosening either puts a tone on grass or on an active runway. The runway gate is "any runway the leg does not start on", not just the one being crossed. → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md)

## TURN-3

- **A recalculation never judges a Turn pick again from where the aircraft is.** A pick is resolved once, at load. `TryRecalculateRoute` reads its phase from the loaded route: ahead, it rebuilds only the part up to the turn and keeps the turn and everything after it as loaded (`TurnPickCarryOver.TailFrom`), and a pick that is not kept is always said ("could not be kept"), never dropped silently; in the turn (between the junction and the taxiway), it counts as missed and "Route changed" says so; made, its junction stays blocked for every later recalculation so none routes back through it. `TurnPickCarryOver.BlocksToKeep` returns the running set union the blocks of every pick made now and removes nothing: once a made pick has left the route, that set is the only place its junction survives, so removing the start node there (the first cut did) unblocked the junction for good after one recalculation that started at it. A recalculation exempts its start node on its own router only, and its "no route with the blocks" retry runs without blocks for that recalculation only; the committed set stays whole in both cases. A recalculation in which a pick was missed, or in which a pick still ahead is not kept, replaces a route the pilot has left at the turn, one whose taxiway names usually match the charted rebuild: the no-op guard (RTE-22) never discards it, and for a missed pick the sanity gate's backwards-bearing indicator (RTE-18) does not reject it, while its length blow-up indicator still does. Keeping the old route there steers back toward an off-chart leg that is gated only from its start. With no pick in play both run exactly as before. The blocked set changes only when a route is adopted, and a refused `LoadRoute` restores it (RTE-29). Re-judging from the aircraft would let the aircraft's drift reroute the pilot's named turn, or undo a turn already made. → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md)

## TURN-4

- **A pick is honored only by a route that itself makes the turn.** The junction block alone lets the router "honor" a turn the destination does not lie on by turning the picked way and then finding its way back to the charted side: `FindConstrainedPathCore` tries up to ten entries onto the row's taxiway, each reached by a free search, so the remainder leaves the taxiway and re-enters it beyond the block. VirtualPilot's `turnpicks` mode (2026-11-15, 10,008 picks on the 100 busiest MSFS 2024 airports) flew five shapes of this, and `TurnPickApplier.Complete` now refuses each as `DoesNotLead`:
  - **Doubling back.** LTFM "right onto N1": 65 degrees right onto one branch of a fork, then 153 degrees the other way 55 m on. `BuiltRouteMakesTheTurn` judges the STITCHED route, not the option's straightest run along the taxiway: it must be a clean turn to the picked side along the row's own taxiway (`TurnShape.IsCleanTurn`; for an off-chart leg, from the leg onto the taxiway), and no heading change within 80 m of the join may exceed 120 degrees, whatever the next edge is named.
  - **Back through where the route has been.** WMKK "left onto G": left, 50 m of G, back up another arm of the previous taxiway to a node the route had already passed, and down the charted side. The remainder is searched with the prefix's nodes closed.
  - **Back up the charted way it declined.** ZJHK "right onto C": an off-chart leg beside the stand's charted lead-in, 37 m of C, then up that lead-in to the taxiway before and onto C the charted way. The remainder is searched with the charted route's nodes up to its own join closed (`chartedBeforeJoin`).
  - **Out and straight back.** ZBAD "right onto L2": 116 m west along the next taxiway to the node the router had picked to leave it by, and back east over the same five nodes. Each leg the router builds is a simple path; joined, they are not. A stitched route that uses a node twice is refused (`VisitsANodeTwice`); 487 of the picks still honored at that point did this somewhere.
  - **Back past its own junction.** A turn, and straight back beside it on other nodes. A route that, 60 m or more beyond the join, passes within 30 m of the junction it turned at is refused (`ComesBackPastTheJunction`).
  
  The two extra node sets are closed for that one search only; a pick's `BlockedNodeIds` (what a recalculation keeps closed, TURN-3) stay the departure node, the connection and the join. The long way round stays allowed: a remainder that keeps clear of all of that, and of the length bound, is honored even when it comes back onto the taxiway's other arm far from the junction (`A_way_round_clear_of_where_the_route_has_been_is_still_taken`). Step 1 ("already so") walks the same row-only path (`LegsThroughRow`): walking 80 m along the route let a turn onto the NEXT taxiway count as this row's, so a straight-on join followed by a left turn onto the next row read as "left, already made" and the pick vanished without a word. The cost of these gates: of the 2,515 picks that sweep honored before them, 1,502 are now refused with a note (the sweep tries both sides of every row, so most of its picks point away from the destination). The long way round that remains is still long (an honored charted pick adds a median 325 m), and one shape of it is not closed: KCLE "left onto G7" at a runway exit goes back across the runway just vacated and round to G7's other arm, and a recalculation during that lap began the lap again. Refusing every route that returns to the same taxiway's other arm would close it, at the price of the designed long way round; that is the owner's decision, not a gate to add in passing. → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md)

## TURN-5

- **A blocked node never switches gap chaining off for its whole taxiway.** `AStarSearchWithGapChaining` used to return null whenever any node of the required taxiway was blocked, on the reasoning that a hop to "the next section" would step over the block. A pick's own join node is always on its row's taxiway, so that rule switched chaining off for EVERY remainder, and the remainder failed wherever the taxiway had a navdata gap anywhere ahead. Flown by VirtualPilot on the whole airport, the KBOS incident route ("H, left on C" to runway 33L) was refused "does not lead to Runway 33L": the real taxiway C has 400 m with no name 900 m east of the junction (nodes 339 to 348). The unit-test replica of the junction had no such gap and passed. Now a gap is chained as always, and only a hop that would step over a block is refused (`HopStepsOverABlock`): onto a blocked node, into a section that runs on behind a block (`SectionsBehindBlocks`), or to a node on the far side of a blocked node of the taxiway (seen from the block, the hop's two ends lie more than 90 degrees apart), which also covers a section behind the block that a gap of its own detaches. All of it is behind `BlockedNodes.Count > 0`. Measured: 30 of 396 "does not lead to" refusals in the sweep were this defect. `KbosHcJunctionFixture.Build(eastGap: true)` now carries the gap, with twelve nodes before it, because the router's ten nearest entry candidates must all lie short of it as they do at the airport. → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md)
