# Ground traffic and the runway watch — rules in full

Each section is the complete text of one rule. Its one-line form, under the same ID, is in `.claude/rules/ground-traffic.md`, which Claude Code loads when it reads matching code. Background: [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md).
The text is verbatim from CLAUDE.md as of `1f37801a`; a trailing "→ doc" pointer is the original's. Cross-references such as "the bullet below", "above" or "under Core" point at CLAUDE.md's old single list, whose rules now live in several files: search `docs/invariants/` for the rule's key name to find it.

## TRF-1

- `GroundTrafficMonitor.SuppressCheck` must silence Caution/Warning callouts during the takeoff roll, when no taxi route is active, and during a landing rollout that is STILL ROLLING — but the forward-arc filter (120°) must never gate Awareness pings, and the Alt+G manual summary hotkey must stay ungated. On the landing exit at or above taxi speed (`RolloutExitGate.TaxiGroundSpeedKts`, 30 kt; unknown counts as fast) the monitor is NOT muted: it keeps evaluating and the runway watch keeps watching, and only what is SPOKEN is filtered (`GroundTrafficSuppression.LandingExitWarningsOnly` → the monitor's `LandingExitWarningsOnlyCheck`, `TrafficSpeechPolicy.SpeaksOnFastLandingExit`: Warning, RunwayCritical and RunwayInfo only) — KMEM 36L's two "Slow down" cautions at 44–47 kt wait, a genuine "Stop" still interrupts (at 40 kt an aircraft 250 ft ahead is under 4 s off), and a held-back line is not latched, so it speaks at taxi speed if it is still true. Never go back to muting the monitor there: that silenced the "Stop" and suspended the runway watch for the parallel an exit crosses. The rule lives in `Services/GroundTrafficSuppression` (pure, pinned), NOT as a lambda in MainForm, and the rolling qualifier (`> RolloutExitGate.NoExitStoppedGroundSpeedKts`) is load-bearing: the two stated reasons for the rollout arm — hands on the brakes, and the exit/runway-end callouts must not be talked over — both die when the aircraft stops, and since `RunwayEndCountdownGate` learned to sit through a mid-runway hold rather than calling it a backtrack, a pilot held on the runway by ATC stays in `LandingRollout` indefinitely and used to have every traffic callout dropped for the whole of it. An unknown ground speed counts as rolling. The runway watch has its own gate, `GroundTrafficSuppression.SuppressRunwayWatch` (see the runway-watch bullet), which outside takeoff assist is exactly `Suppress`'s rule. → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md)

## TRF-2

- **The departure-queue position counts the CONTIGUOUS line the pilot is in** (`GroundTrafficLogic.QueueAheadOf`, `QueueLinkMaxGapM` = 250 m) — the 1,500 m scan says how far to look, not where one queue ends — and **the wording is `GroundTrafficLogic.ReadQueue`'s alone**: "departure queue" ONLY on a route whose destination is the takeoff runway AND when the head of the pilot's OWN line (`QueueCluster.HeadAheadM`, never the farthest aircraft on the route) is within `QueueAtRunwayHoldM` (150 m) of the route end, BOTH measured from the aircraft. `GroundTrafficRouteContext.RouteEndMetres` is measured from the START of the current segment, so the monitor subtracts its own route position — compared unconverted, the real runway hold said "queue" and an intermediate one said "departure queue" (PR #247 review). A queued aircraft is on the route (≤ 30 m), ≤ 6 kt and POINTING ALONG it (±35°, by its EFFECTIVE direction — the track from a few seconds of position history when it is moving backwards, so a 2–4 kt pushback reads by its track even at the 1 s cadence) — pushbacks, crossing traffic, head-on creepers and parked aircraft never count — and on a queue route the intake keeps ground traffic out to `QueueScanM`, not only while a runway is watched. Under-counting a line with a wide gap in it is the safe failure against merging two lines. Pinned by `DepartureQueueClusterTests`, `QueueReadingTests` and `TrafficMotionModelTests`, and the route-end conversion end to end by `GroundTrafficMonitorHeadlessTests` (the pilot partway down a segment). → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md)

## TRF-3

- The ground-traffic **runway watch** never says a runway is "clear" — only "no traffic seen". Its scope is `RunwayWatchScopes.Resolve`: the held runway from `Navigation.HeldRunwayLabel` (the ONE derivation the status readout also uses — never a mirrored field: PR #247's `_heldRunwayLabel` was never assigned and no ordinary hold-short was ever watched), a Progressive hold (never an INTERMEDIATE holding point — `ProgressiveHoldRunwayResolver`), the backtrack, the lineup, the takeoff wait (takeoff assist's runway) and any runway under the aircraft — keyed on the RUNWAY (`RunwayKey`, both ends) and, when the watch has a REASON (takeoff wait, lineup, backtrack, hold, Progressive hold), on the runways that reason names ONLY (`RunwayWatch.IdentityKey`): another runway's pavement crossed or stood on widens the scan (`Runways`) but never changes the key, so hold → backtrack → lineup → takeoff wait is ONE watch that never restarts, even through an intersection; a position-only watch keeps the join of all its runways. The key the monitor compares is `RunwayWatch.Key`, which is that `IdentityKey` when a reason set one and that sorted join otherwise. A CROSSING is one watch too: a single-runway watch whose sources all end LINGERS (`RunwayWatchLinger`, same key, Holding mode; multi-runway watches never linger) until the aircraft is clear on the far side (half-width + 60 m), 30 m farther out on the side it started, or 60 s — never restore the stop-and-restart that spoke a second full status mid-crossing. A normal crossing lingers TWICE: at the hold-line gap (ended `reason=resumed` when the pavement names the runway again) and at the far edge (ended `reason=turned-away`, then `ev=watch stop`); `reason=clear` only when no tick saw the aircraft on the pavement. Its first status is ALWAYS spoken (never re-add a "lining up, stay silent" baseline, which recorded traffic on short final as already announced), waits up to 3 s for an aircraft over the pavement whose climb rate is not known yet (`LandingPending`, its first sample — never spoken), and follows the mode's interrupt rule (`RunwayEventsInterrupt`): on the runway (`OnRunway`, `LiningUp`, `TakeoffWait`) runway events interrupt — one withheld within `InterruptProtectMs` (3 s) of a "Stop" or another runway event is neither spoken nor latched, is re-evaluated every sweep and interrupts at the first evaluation after the window at which nothing more urgent, and nothing as urgent but nearer, is due, and is never moved to the queued channel (PR #247 focused re-review I1); at a hold (`Holding`) and while TURNING OFF after landing (`Vacating`: ONLY the runway landed on — `GroundTrafficRouteContext.LandingRunway`, the rollout's runway — under the aircraft on a landing-exit route, entered at ≥ `VacatingMinGsKts` (3 kt) and held down to `VacatingHoldGsKts` (1 kt) so decelerating through the turn never flips it; a parallel the exit route crosses is `OnRunway`) they are queued, so a runway status never cuts off taxi guidance's exit instructions; an unheld crossing or a stray runway entry is `OnRunway`. The SAME watch changing from a queuing mode (`Holding`, `Vacating`) into an interrupting one (`OnRunway`, `LiningUp`, `TakeoffWait`) — Continue at a hold, or stopping on the runway after landing — re-arms the first status ONCE per watch, CRITICAL-ONLY (`RunwayWatchScopes.ShouldRearmOnModeChange`; `ev=watch status-rearmed … reason=entered-runway|stopped-on-runway`) — from `Holding` only when the hold was RELEASED within `RunwayWatchScopes.RearmAfterHoldWindowMs` (10 s, inclusive; a judgement of how long a queued hold status can take to be spoken and heard, not a measurement) of that status being handed to the announcer, the window in which a PROMPT Continue can have cut it off (later, re-arming would only interrupt the Continue instruction with a status already heard; PR #247 re-review M4), from `Vacating` with no window: spoken, interrupting, only with something on the runway or on short final, otherwise completed silently and marking NOTHING known — because the queued status may have been cut off by the very `AnnounceImmediate` that moved the pilot, and since nothing was actually spoken, an occupant/final that showed up since the last spoken status must still reach the ordinary event path as a fresh one instead of being absorbed here with no callout (PR #247 B5 follow-up K2). The hold's RELEASE is the moment the crossing's linger BEGAN from the hold (`_holdReleasedUtc`, about the Continue press) — never the change to `OnRunway`, which comes only as the aircraft reaches the pavement, 11–15 s after Continue from a median hold line, and so almost never admitted a crossing, whose "Continuing." is exactly what cuts the hold status off — or, with no linger (a destination hold going straight to `LiningUp` or the backtrack), the change itself; a release time is dropped once a watch is adopted with no linger in progress, so a hold taken up again under the same key never lends it to a later change (PR #247 focused re-review N1). The watch ADOPTED THIS TICK — never the possibly-stale cycle a slow sweep completes with — decides BOTH whether a queuing evaluation waits for the re-armed status (PR #247 re-review M5; waiting on the stale cycle's own mode alone never ended once the pilot went back to a queuing mode before any interrupting evaluation completed, muting the whole runway watch for good — PR #247 re-review follow-up) and whether that status is critical (a stale `OnRunway` cycle completing once the pilot was moving again, `Vacating`, interrupted the exit instructions — PR #247 focused re-review N2); once that watch no longer interrupts, the re-armed status takes the silent K2 completion described above. It also decides the CHANNEL of every other runway line that can interrupt — the ordinary first status and every new occupant, new final or final turning short; "no traffic seen on the runway now" is always queued (PR #247 focused re-review S7: "on the runway, interrupt" and "never over the exit instructions" are facts about where the pilot is NOW; a sweep requested at a hold and answered once the pilot was on the runway queued a short final) — while the scan and the words stay the evaluated cycle's, so what is said can be up to one sweep old; the cycle's own mode is read only by the re-armed status's wait (it waits out a queuing cycle's sweep while the adopted watch interrupts). The re-arm is a mitigation for the FIRST status only: any runway line queued LATER — a new occupant or final queued at a hold or while vacating, "no traffic seen on the runway now" — can still be cut off by another interrupting line, and nothing re-arms it. A known occupant or final is forgotten only after `KnownAbsenceGraceMs` (3 s) unseen, so a one-sample classification drop never re-announces it — UNLESS the runway it is recorded under has itself left this evaluation's scan (a position-only widening that has since narrowed back down, above): that one is purged SILENTLY on the spot, never through the grace timer (PR #247 B5 follow-up K3). Known traffic is recorded by runway KEY (`WatchedRunway.Key`) — never the spoken designator, which for a position-only watch names the nearer end and flips at mid-runway, so the purge happens only when a runway leaves the scan, never on a name flip — in SEPARATE occupant and final records (`_knownOccupantRunway`, `_knownFinalRunway`), each cleaned only with its own kind; an id with no recorded key is left to the grace timer (`GroundTrafficLogic.IdsOutOfScope`), and the purge itself runs AFTER that evaluation's scan has refreshed every seen id's key, never before — an occupant standing inside an intersection is on both runways at once, and purging on the STALE key purged it and announced it again in the very evaluation that purged it (PR #247 re-review follow-up). Never share one record between them: a landing aircraft is a known occupant AND still a known final for the grace period after touchdown, and a shared map let the final's cleanup purge the occupant — every aircraft landing on the watched runway was announced twice (PR #247 re-review M1). "No traffic seen on the runway now" is PER RUNWAY (`GroundTrafficLogic.EmptiedRunwayKeys`): queued when the LAST known occupant recorded under that runway's key has been unseen for the grace period, never while an aircraft is seen on it, and never by the purge — purged traffic never feeds it; it names THAT runway, adding "Traffic still on final." only when that runway has a final, never every runway the watch scans (PR #247 re-review M2). It has its OWN gate, `GroundTrafficSuppression.SuppressRunwayWatch`: takeoff assist silences it only once the ground speed is known ≥ `RunwayWatchTakeoffCutoffKts` (30) — the line-up wait is exactly when landing traffic matters — and a watch the gate closes on is SUSPENDED, not ended: the same key back within `RunwayWatchScopes.WatchResumeGraceMs` (15 s) resumes it with no new first status (`ev=watch suspend`/`ev=watch resume`) — a landing rollout's ~3 kt gate line flipped under a creeping pilot and restarted the watch each time. On resume the sweep-readiness reference is RE-ARMED to the resume moment, not left at the watch's original start (PR #247 integration follow-up R2): a sweep requested BEFORE the suspension, whose entries then arrived DURING it unwatched (dropping an aircraft on final) and whose completion only lands AFTER the resume, has its cycle's own gate check pass (it was open at request time) — only the readiness reference stops it, so leaving it at the original start reported a false "no traffic seen … or on final." A local route context's OWN runway list is always used, even an empty one; only with no local context do the airport's runways come from `RunwaySupplier` (a runway-only `TaxiGraph.Build`, cached per ICAO, cleared on a database switch). Airborne traffic is attributed to its best-fitting runway among ALL runways (`ClassifyAgainstRunways`; the 300 m + 12 % cone is wider than 1,228 fs2024 parallel-runway pairs), and an aircraft low over the pavement is `Landing` ONLY with a KNOWN climb ≤ `LandingMaxClimbFpm` (300 fpm) and no ground contact within `LandingGroundMemorySec` (60 s) — a departure just off the ground is never "landing". The first status waits for a sweep REQUESTED after the watch started — the monitor has its OWN rotating request ids (`REQUEST_GROUND_TRAFFIC` 600–607, `GroundTrafficSweepCompleted`; never TCAS's), drops a completion that is not the outstanding id, uses a small radius (`SweepRadiusMeters`) and never has more than one sweep outstanding. → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md)

## TRF-4

- The monitor's ground-traffic callouts go through `TrafficSpeechPolicy.Plan` (the Alt+G summary is not one of them: it speaks directly with `AnnounceImmediate` and stays ungated, TRF-1): ONLY `Warning` ("Stop"), `RunwayCritical` (runway events while ON the runway) and `Caution` ("Slow down") interrupt; only a STRICTLY more urgent interrupt may cut another off within `InterruptProtectMs` (3 s) — an equally urgent one no longer may either (PR #247 integration follow-up R3: faster polling made a second "Stop" routinely follow the first by about a second and cut it off) — and a withheld interrupt is neither spoken nor latched: it is re-evaluated every sweep and interrupts at the first evaluation after the window at which nothing more urgent, and nothing as urgent but nearer, is due, and it is NEVER moved to the queued channel (PR #247 focused re-review I1: R3 first gave it the alert slot, where it was latched on hand-over with no protect window of its own, so the next interrupt — even a less urgent one — cancelled a second "Stop", or an on-runway runway status naming a one-mile final, before it was heard, and it was never repeated); other alerts are queued, at most one per `AlertLineSpacingMs` (3 s); informational lines always go out; and every one-shot latch is committed in `TrafficCallout.OnEmitted` when the line is HANDED to the announcer (never while it is suppressed) — NOT when it is voiced: any later `AnnounceImmediate`, another feature's or the monitor's own safety line, can drop a queued line unheard, and the runway watch's critical-only re-arm is a mitigation for the watch's FIRST status only, not a cure — a runway line queued later can still be dropped unheard. "One interrupt per evaluation" was not enough: evaluations run about a second apart and every `AnnounceImmediate` cancels the lines queued behind it. A Caution/Warning re-escalation after a silent de-escalation needs a HIGHER zone than last spoken or the 15 s window (`EscalationRepeatWindowMs`) — except that a WARNING re-entry is also spoken once the aircraft is `EscalationReclosureFt` (50 ft) closer than when "Stop" was last spoken, and a withheld Warning escalation is never recorded silently (`ZoneToRecordWhenWithheld`), so creeping back toward traffic after a "Stop" is warned again while boundary flicker at a constant distance is not. Caution keeps R8: complying with "Slow down" never earns another one inside the window, and a Caution that keeps closing reaches the always-spoken "Stop" at the Warning line. The old CLAUDE.md claim that the window "may suppress Awareness only" is retired. Moving-away is a RATE (`IsMovingAway`, 20 ft per 3 s) OR, for traffic moving at 3 kt or more, MOTION (`IsOpeningByMotion`: the relative velocity opening the gap at 1 m/s or more, or its lead ALONG the route growing that fast — the rate needs a previous evaluation, so traffic pulling away was "Stop" on the first one); a Warning withheld because the traffic moves away is never recorded (`ZoneToRecordWhenWithheld`, as for one the repeat window withholds), so "Stop" speaks once the traffic stops OPENING inside the Warning distance — recording it swallowed that "Stop" for good, already on the reviewed base (05e5d3ea), not only after the motion port. A "Stop" withheld BECAUSE the traffic was opening is then HELD (`IsMovingAwayOrHeld`, `ev=stop-hold`) while that traffic moves at 3 kt or more and the pilot is not closing on it at 0.5 m/s or more (closing on BOTH the straight-line gap and the 2 s along-route lead, `LeadRateMps` — never the one-second lead alone: in a turn the pilot's own route position jitters and round a bend the straight-line gap closes while the leader pulls away, so one such sample said "Stop, … very close" about an aircraft pulling away; VirtualPilot traffic A/B: 15 false calls fixed over 3,478 taxis, 0 new), and speaks once it stops, the pilot closes, or — whatever the speeds say — the gap reaches `StopHoldFloorFt` (200 ft, PR #247 integration follow-up R1: without it a pilot closing more slowly than the release speed on a leader still moving at 3 kt or more was never warned, however close it got; the floor is scoped to the HELD half only, never to traffic genuinely opening right now) — following a departing leader out of a queue drew "Stop" with the gap still growing; never "record it until the traffic stops" (a leader slowing to 4 kt would never re-arm). A Caution/Warning withheld OUTSIDE the forward arc is recorded by the same rule, so turning to face very close traffic that was behind still earns "Stop". Converging needs the forward arc or a moving pilot (`ConvergingAllowed`). With a route, "Slow down"/"Stop" need a real threat (`GroundTrafficLogic.IsRouteThreat`): near the route ahead, very close, or MOVING traffic (3 kt) on a predicted collision course — never a PARKED aircraft's straight-line closest approach, which assumes the pilot keeps going straight through a bend (the PR author measured 421 false calls over 100 airports). That trade is deliberate and has a cost: a parked aircraft OFF the route never earns "Slow down", only "Stop" inside the fixed 250 ft — so a pilot rolling at 3 kt or more with ANY ground aircraft within ±30° of the nose inside the speed-scaled Caution distance keeps the sweep at 1 s (`NeedsFastPoll`), which brings that "Stop" within a second of the line (at 12 kt the 3 s cadence gave it at about 200 ft). Never buy the "Slow down" back by restoring the parked closest-approach threat. Only the FIRST aircraft on the route ahead gets on-route and zone callouts, and only traffic OCCUPYING the route — stopped on it or moving along it (`OccupiesRoute`) — can be that first (a crossing aircraft hid the stopped one beyond it): one more than 10 m further along is queued behind it (`IsQueuedBehindFirst`) and gets neither — except "Stop", which is never withheld (`WithholdsZoneBehindFirst`) — and head-on traffic is never queued behind it. A queued aircraft's zone is recorded, so one that becomes the first is not called at the zone it had and, from Caution, loses its "on your route" too. The monitor has a headless harness (`IGroundTrafficSimSource`, `TickForHarness`, a simulated clock; `GroundTrafficMonitorHeadlessTests`, `GroundTrafficMonitorRuleTests`): every rule reads the injected clock (`_utcNow`), never `DateTime.UtcNow` — on a real clock a test's ticks all fall inside one 3 s alert spacing and hide defects — and a fake sweep is answered only after its request returns, since the monitor learns the id it waits for from that call. A rule with two protections needs a test per protection wherever one can tell them apart: the runway watch's first status has one for the readiness gate (a watch restarted under the same key) and one for R2's re-arm of it on resume (PR #247 integration follow-up R2: a sweep requested BEFORE the suspension, whose completion lands after the resume, which its own cycle lets through) — a sweep requested before any watch existed is stopped by both, so it pins neither — and a RE-ARMED first status has one for its wait for a sweep requested in the interrupting mode (the M5 wait, PR #247 focused re-review S8), one for that wait's bound (it waits only while the adopted watch interrupts — replay G) and one for its critical decision (the adopted watch's, never its sweep's mode — X3/N2) (PR #247 focused re-review S9). Of the completion's own cycle, the watch-gate check has no test that can tell it apart: since R2 a sweep requested WHILE the watch was suspended is stopped by the re-armed readiness gate as well, so for the first status that check is defence in depth (PR #247 focused re-review M1); the watch its request saw is pinned by S7's first test (its wording) and by S8's (its mode, the M5 wait). → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md)

## TRF-5

- "… ahead is moving." comes from a LATCHED departure (`QueueMovementPolicy.Step`) and is spoken only for the NEAREST aircraft directly ahead — never a one-sample speed edge, which was lost whenever the call was outranked or another sweep moved the previous speed on. "Move up" (`QueueMovementPolicy.EvaluateNudge`) is an instruction ATC has not given, so it speaks only where `GroundTrafficRouteContext.AllowsQueuePrompt` (Taxiing on a joined route) and the pilot is not on runway pavement, only to a pilot below 1 kt, never with anything ELSE within 250 ft ahead — the aircraft whose departure armed it does not count TOWARD THAT DISARM RULE ONLY, and only while it is still MOVING (above `GroundTrafficLogic.QueueStoppedGs`, 1.5 kt — `QueueMovementPolicy.NearestOtherAheadFt`; it arms a few feet from where it sat, so counting it disarmed the nudge a second after arming; once it stops again it counts like any other aircraft, so a leader that crept 20–40 ft and stopped inside 250 ft disarms the nudge instead of drawing a "Move up. 200 feet to the traffic ahead." into a gap there is no room for — PR #247 re-review M3) — at most 3 times 20 s apart; it arms only when the aircraft moved off ALONG the route and disarms at 2 kt. The SPOKEN distance names whichever aircraft is nearest ahead, that one INCLUDED — it is genuinely the traffic the pilot will close on — never the disarm-only distance, which once made a leader still just ahead get reported as "The traffic ahead has taxied on." (PR #247 B5 follow-up K1). While the departed leader is still moving but still within 250 ft, the nudge neither disarms nor speaks: it waits, armed, and speaks once the gap has opened (`EvaluateNudge`; a leader creeping on at 1.6–2.5 kt drew "Move up. 210 feet to the traffic ahead.", inside the Warning distance — PR #247 focused re-review N3). → [taxi-guidance.md](../taxi-guidance.md)
