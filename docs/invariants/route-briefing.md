# Route briefing — rules in full

Each section is the complete text of one rule. Its one-line form, under the same ID, is in `.claude/rules/route-briefing.md`, which Claude Code loads when it reads matching code. Background: [gemini.md](../gemini.md).
The text is verbatim from CLAUDE.md as of `1f37801a`; a trailing "→ doc" pointer is the original's. Cross-references such as "the bullet below", "above" or "under Core" point at CLAUDE.md's old single list, whose rules now live in several files: search `docs/invariants/` for the rule's key name to find it.

## BRF-1

- The route briefing's OpenStreetMap graph (`Navigation/Briefing/OsmPlanningGraph`) is PLANNING-ONLY: built by `TaxiBriefingGraphSource.BuildAsync`, called from `TaxiBriefingPlanner`'s `PlanAsync`, and rendered under the tier label `TaxiBriefingRenderer.OsmLabel` ("OpenStreetMap, planning only — taxi guidance cannot use this") — or, when the online source it picked is X-Plane's apt.dat, as `BriefingTier.XPlane` under `TaxiBriefingRenderer.XPlaneLabel` ("X-Plane airport data, planning only — taxi guidance cannot use this") with its own note `OsmPlanningGraph.XPlaneNote`, never labelled OpenStreetMap — and never stored on a field a form or `TaxiGuidanceManager` can reach. It exists only because a briefing does not steer; the augmentation rule "never steer on online geometry" is unchanged. The online fetch is everything within 5 km of the reference point, so the graph keeps only what lies inside the navdata airport box grown `CurrentAirportResolver.BoxMarginMetres` (300 m, through `GrownBox` — a segment with either end inside, a stand or hold inside): unclipped, a small field beside a hub was planned on the hub's stands and taxiways (73 fs2024 airports without taxi paths lie within 5 km of one with ≥ 30 stands — 4NY2 beside KLGA, 2N7 beside KTEB, BNH beside KBOS). Named segments are cut into pieces no longer than `OsmPlanningGraph.DensifyMetres` (25 m), because online ways are long straight lines and a stand beside the middle of one had no node within the 100 m stand reach. NO parking goes into its `TaxiGraph.Build`: the parking pass would stamp the nearest taxiway vertex of every stand `Parking`, retyping a hold-short vertex (the hold stops being seen and moves), an exit junction (`GetLandingExits` drops the exit), and making the own position read "current position, stand 7" at stand 3; the stands stay in the bundle for the stand picker only. A stand name carrying two numbers ("Apron 2 Stand 5") keeps its own words (`OsmPlanningGraph.ToSpot`) — `StandId.Parse` would join the digits into stand 25, which a SayIntentions "Gate 25" would then match. → [gemini.md](../gemini.md)

## BRF-2

- The briefing's taxi section gives each leg up to three parts, in order and each on its own line (owner, 2026-09-27). First, the ROUTE PARAGRAPH, built on the scenery-computed route: names, order, turns, sides and distances only from the block, and nothing from the AI's own knowledge except an unavailable leg's general-knowledge route. Never two versions (owner, 2026-09-26: the two-section form was too long, and the real-world-only form invented taxiway names, KMEM "V1" and KATL "V3"/"V4"). Each route paragraph says it is the expected route on the pilot's scenery and that SayIntentions or ATC gives the actual clearance: the planner's route is the SHORTEST one, and controllers route by flows, one-way taxiways and end-around taxiways. That scenery phrase belongs only to a route the block actually gives (final review, 2026-09-27): a general-knowledge route ends by saying only that SayIntentions or ATC will give the actual taxi clearance, never that it is the pilot's scenery route, and a leg with no route at all gets no scenery phrase. Second, one CHECK LINE that checks the leg against the real airport and says what it checked against: `GeminiService.RouteCheckFromMemory` unless a web search in that briefing found AND READ the airport's charts (`RouteCheckAgainstCharts`) — with web search on, the AI is told to do its lookups — both airports' NOTAMs first — before it writes any part of the briefing, because Claude's `ParseResponse` keeps only the text after the last tool block, so a chart lookup made partway through would silently drop the sections already written. It never claims an agreement OR A DIFFERENCE it cannot support (final review, 2026-09-27: "unsupported agreement" alone let it overclaim a difference just as confidently); and when it leaves a point out because the scenery gives no name for it, it must not call that leg an agreement — it says only that not everything could be checked, without naming what. Third, an optional SUGGESTIONS paragraph (`RouteSuggestionsOpening`, "Real-world suggestions, not from your scenery:"), at most three short sentences: preferred exits from the charts, restrictions for the aircraft's size, current operational information, and at most one sentence that controllers usually route differently. Never a full alternative route. It comes after the check line, or after the route paragraph when a leg has no check line to follow. `GetRouteDescriptionPrompt` must be told whether ITS OWN request has web search (`webSearch`; Claude's retry without the web_search tool passes false). The flag swaps exactly one sentence, because a check line claiming live charts it never had is a false assurance. Every runway the route crosses is always given, including one where no hold short point could be placed. The prompt's note filter must never become an exhaustive list that can silence it, and the block's hold line never says "No runway crossings" over such a crossing (`TaxiLegBriefing.UnheldRunways`). The check line and the suggestions may name only taxiways, exits and stands in that leg's lines, including its "Taxiway names at" list (`TaxiLegBriefing.AirportTaxiways`, every edge name on the graph the leg was planned on; a taxi-in list printed as `TaxiBriefingRenderer.SameListAsTaxiOut` is the taxi-out's list, and the prompt says so), and drop a point rather than name anything else. Aircraft classification for it is SimBrief-only (never the loaded aircraft or `WING SPAN`). Navdata taxiway widths are advisory notes, never a routing constraint: the commonest navdata width, 82 ft = 24.99 m on 62 % of fs2024 taxiway rows and 65 % of fs2020's, is judged in whole feet, the unit navdata stores it in, so the minimum's own whole-foot value (49 ft for 15 m, 82 ft for 25 m) is never "below" it. → [gemini.md](../gemini.md)

## BRF-3

- The briefing's stand picker (`Navigation/Briefing/BriefingStandPicker`) looks for SayIntentions' gate in the ORDER Taxi Assist's own SayIntentions import uses (`TaxiAssistForm.TryResolveExternalDestination`): NAME, then the scenery's online ALIASES, and SayIntentions' published position (the pin) only when no name or alias matched at all — owner decision 2026-09-26, which REVERSED the earlier "the pin wins" rule, so never re-gate a name or alias match on the pin's reach. It follows the import onto a stand of ANY kind (owner decision, 2026-09-26, applied to the pin the same day; the import's list carries fuel/vehicle stands only when it comes from navdata — GSX's gate list never does — and de-ice pads are a separate destination type there): when the name or alias — or, with neither matching, the pin — finds only an EXCLUDED kind (military ramps, seaplane docks, fuel and vehicle stands — ParkingTypes' families — and de-ice pads), that stand is briefed with `"this scenery marks that stand as a fuel stand"`; but an ordinary stand always beats an excluded one, at every step, and the representative choice never lands on one, on type 1, or on a stand the SayIntentions step just refused. A name or alias match whose own reach (`min(150 m, 2 × radius)`, 60 m for an unknown size) does not cover the pin is still briefed, with `"SayIntentions' position is 1.2 km from this stand"` (`TaxiBriefingRenderer.FormatDistance`). Among several namesakes the tie-break is ordinary before excluded, then nearest the pin (no pin: a gate first, then list order) — it must NEVER become list order alone, which briefs a GA "Spot 8" as "Gate 8" at ~20 airports — and an unconnected first gives way only to a namesake at least as plausible (`AtLeastAsPlausible`, bounded so one kilometres FARTHER from the pin than the first never stands in for it); at the pin, the next stand in reach is used, and ONE sentence names both with their distances (never a separate "lists as" note calling the stand used SayIntentions' gate). The import differs, deliberately left so: it takes its own list's first match, lets an excluded stand's NAME beat an ordinary stand's ALIAS, takes the nearest stand of any kind its list carries at the pin, resolves without asking whether the stand connects to the taxiway network, and skips a stand of unknown size in its position match (the briefing gives it 60 m). A stand found by the pin alone is briefed `…which this scenery does not have; the stand nearest SayIntentions' position is Y` (the import's own position wording) — never `lists as`, which is the ALIAS step's, and which claimed the scenery calls the stand by SayIntentions' name. → [gemini.md](../gemini.md)

## BRF-4

- The briefing's leg ends — a stand and the aircraft's own position — are placed by `TaxiBriefingPlanner.LegEndNode`: the nearest node when its piece of the taxi network also holds the leg's other end (the taxi-out's runway entrance resolved from that node — and, for a piece other than the largest, lying on the runway pavement, `EntersTheRunway`, never merely the lineup search's plain node up to 120 m off the centreline — or a briefable exit's route start on the taxi-in; on a component mismatch `RunwayEntry` searches the start's own piece for an entrance at or behind the lineup point before the backtrack search, which skips entrances under 40 m along) AND either no largest-component node is within reach or that nearer node is a stand's own or on a stand lead-in — for the aircraft's own position only within 40 m of it (`OwnPositionStandMaxMetres`: a stand is where the aircraft is parked only when it is AT the stand) — (an aircraft on the main network beside a narrow runway was otherwise snapped to the piece across it), else the largest component's nearest node; and never a node whose piece lies across a runway from the point (`PieceLiesAcrossARunway`: the way to the node and on along every one of its edges crosses a runway, by `RunwayRouteClassifier`), which with no largest-component node in reach leaves no node at all rather than a start across the runway. For the aircraft's own position the largest component's node is judged the same way (`refuseNetworkAcrossARunway`): across a runway it is never the start — the nearer node of a piece on the point's own side that reaches the runway is taken instead, else none; a stand's lookup does not judge it (the stand figures below are unchanged by that). Residual: when the nearest node of all lies across the runway (the largest component's, or a third piece's), the aircraft's own node on its side, of any piece including the largest, is not searched for and the leg falls back to a representative stand — the safe direction. Never go back to the largest component alone: the graph has no runway edges, so pieces meeting only across a runway are separate components, and on fs2024 (2026-09-28) 7,987 stands at 1,857 airports sit on such a piece off the largest one by the lineup search alone — 6,967 with no largest-component node within 100 m, briefed as not connecting or replaced by a representative stand, and 1,020 briefed from a largest-component node, i.e. from the wrong piece — and 12,088 once the planner's backtrack search and runway-pavement test are counted (LFBP, VIJU, ENAT, UKHH, KPRC). Route starts still pass `excludeBridgeOnlyStandStubs: true`. → [gemini.md](../gemini.md)

## BRF-5

- The owner's real-world taxi question (`GeminiService.RealWorldTaxiQuestion`) is an INSTRUCTION to the AI, never output: the prompt must never again tell the AI to "substitute" the data into it (a live KMEM→KATL Gemini briefing wrote the filled-in question out under both "Real-world practice" headings, 2026-09-26), and `RouteBriefingText.RemoveEchoedTaxiQuestion` must stay in BOTH providers' `DescribeRouteAsync`. It removes only the question (whole, either sentence, quoted, labelled, split over two lines) and a line it empties — and only where the echo STARTS its line, after indentation, a list marker or a heading ending in a colon or dash: unanchored, it deleted the AI's own sentences that used the question's words ("Ground will provide the step-by-step taxi route after you vacate…" became "Ground will "); its anchors are pinned against the constant, so rewording the question fails the tests until the anchors follow. → [gemini.md](../gemini.md)

## BRF-6

- The route briefing takes SayIntentions' data only for THIS flight (`SayIntentionsArrivalGate.IsThisFlight`: flight file exists, origin and destination match the flight plan). Its assigned runways (`flight_plan_departing_runway` / `flight_plan_arriving_runway`) win over the flight plan's, and the leg's first note says so ("…the flight plan names 18R" / "SayIntentions has assigned this runway too") — on a leg that could not be computed too. Its gate comes through `GetAssignedStatusAsync` — the SayIntentions window's own call — so the file's `assigned_gate`, else the `getParking` answer, each with its OWN position; a parking-service gate whose position is not at the arrival airport (`CurrentAirport.Resolve`; within 5 km of the reference point only when the database cannot answer) is never briefed as SayIntentions', and one with NO position is looked up by name in the arrival airport's scenery, never replaced by a representative stand while a stand there carries the name (live KMEM→KATL: `getParking` "B3" with no position, the flight file "Gate B3" nine seconds later — the service named the arrival gate; owner 2026-09-26, reversing that morning's refusal). Every distance in the TAXI ROUTES block and its notes is in ONE unit, the pilot's ground distance setting, named on the block's second line — never a hard-coded unit (live KMEM→KATL: "2.2 kilometers" beside "6,025 feet"). The computed routes' turns come from `BriefingTurns` — a stretch sum, never one junction's angle — with no direction into the first taxiway or onto the departure runway, and none across an unnamed stretch longer than a stand lead-in (`BriefingTurns.MaxUnnamedStretchMetres`, 100 m), whether a connector between two taxiways or the approach into the stand: across one, the heading change is apron, not a turn (live KATL C 22; 615 of 11,579 fs2024 taxi-outs misbriefed without it). → [gemini.md](../gemini.md)
