# Visual guidance's landing threshold and glide path angle — rules in full

Each section is the complete text of one rule. Its one-line form, under the same ID, is in `.claude/rules/visual-guidance-runway.md`, which Claude Code loads when it reads matching code. Background: [visual-guidance.md](../visual-guidance.md). The other visual guidance rules are in [visual-guidance.md](visual-guidance.md).

## VG-30

- `VisualGuidanceThreshold.Of(runway)` is the frame every measure starts from: `NavigationCalculator.LandingThreshold`, the runway end's position (`runway_end.lonx/laty`, the start of the pavement) moved `Runway.ThresholdOffset` feet along the runway's true course, and the course. `VisualGuidanceManager.Initialize` sets it once; the glide path's height, F's distance, the mile calls, the warnings' along-track position, the decision, the capture law's legs, the cross-track and the recording's `start` line all read it, and no `VisualGuidance*.cs` file reads `Runway.StartLat` or `StartLon`. The landing flare assist (`LandingFlareAssistManager.ApplyActiveRunway`) uses the same `LandingThreshold`. → [visual-guidance.md](../visual-guidance.md), "Where the path starts and how steep it is"
- `LandingThreshold` returns the end's position exactly for a zero offset (most runway ends), and ignores an offset that reaches the far end (KMWH 18: 3,333 ft on a 3,321 ft runway, in both navdata builds); on a runway of unknown length (0) the offset applies.
- Why: until 2026-11-06 visual guidance took the runway end as the threshold and aimed short by the offset (KATL 27R 512 ft, 27 ft low; KJFK 22R 3,471 ft, about 180 ft low). About one hard-surface runway end in eight has a displaced threshold (5,049 of 42,470 in MSFS 2024, 4,754 of 36,116 in MSFS 2020). The offset data is right: on displaced runways with a glideslope the sim's antenna sits a median 1,017 ft past the painted threshold (722 ends, MSFS 2024; 1,018 ft over 701 in MSFS 2020), as on plain runways (1,021 ft past the end), so the glidepath bias calibrated on plain runways carries over. About 20 ILS ends per build have the antenna before the painted threshold; there the offset is trusted, which can only move the aim later on the runway.
- Measured in the approach harness's DATUM family on runway C (3,471 ft displaced): before, its six finals landed 2,000 to 2,540 ft short of the painted threshold, 158 to 190 ft RMS below the path; after, they touch down 930 to 1,460 ft past it, within 9 ft RMS of the path, the same numbers as runway A. The 267 older runs are textually identical.
- Pinned by `RunwayLandingThresholdTests` (no offset, KATL 27R's 512 ft along the course, an offset reaching the far end, an unknown length) and `VisualGuidanceRunwayDatumTests` (F's distance and "On path" on a 3,471 ft displaced threshold, the recording's start at the painted threshold under sv-SE).

## VG-31

- `VisualGuidanceVertical.PathAngleDeg(ils, papi)`: the ILS glideslope's angle (`ils.gs_pitch`, `Runway.GlideslopeAngleDeg`) when it lies within `MinPathAngleDeg` to `MaxPathAngleDeg` (2° to 8°), else the PAPI's or VASI's (`runway_end.left_vasi_pitch`, else `right_vasi_pitch`, `Runway.PapiAngleDeg`) within the same band, else `DefaultPathAngleDeg` (3°). The path's height, the target's feed-forward angle, its 2° limit, the warnings' half degree and the recording's `gp=` all take it. → [visual-guidance.md](../visual-guidance.md), "Where the path starts and how steep it is"
- Why: about 1,600 runway ends per navdata build have a PAPI angle other than 3° and no ILS angle (1,616 in MSFS 2024, 1,641 in MSFS 2020; most at 3.5° or 4.0°), where visual guidance flew 3° until 2026-11-06: 320 ft under a 4° PAPI at 3 nm, with the PAPI showing the pilot low.
- Why the ILS wins: it is what the sim's glideslope flies, and the PAPI angles are less reliable. Where an end has both, 288 of 2,863 disagree in MSFS 2024 (10 of 2,592 in MSFS 2020), some plainly wrong: London City 09 and 27 PAPI 3.0 beside a 5.5° ILS, LOWL 26 PAPI 9.0 beside a 3.0° ILS, LSZA 01 PAPI 4.17 beside a 6.65° ILS. A wrong PAPI on a runway with no ILS cannot be caught; one outside the band falls back to 3°.
- Measured in the approach harness's DATUM family on runway D (no ILS, PAPI 4°): its six finals' path error fell from 62 to 307 ft RMS to 3 to 9 ft.
- Pinned by `VisualGuidanceRunwayDatumTests` (the order and the band, including London City's and LOWL 26's angles; "On path" on a 4° PAPI with no ILS; `gp=3.50` in the recording) and `RunwayPapiAngleReadTests` (the left light's angle, else the right's, else 0, through `LittleNavMapProvider.GetRunways`).
