## Panels and hotkeys

Panels (Aircraft menu → Synaptic A220-300; `GetPanelStructure`) follow the cockpit and the
A220 flow: the overhead in power-on and preflight order, then the glareshield left to right,
the center panel, the pedestal and the utilities. Every panel opens on the controls a normal
flight uses, in the order the day reaches them (the aircraft's normal checklists and the
pilot-guide flows, power-on to power-off); the rest follow in cockpit order and guarded
switches come last. Fire Protection reads per fire (the push, then its bottles) and the Audio
Control Panel per radio (transmit, volume, receiver); positions for the rest:
`tools/a220-gen/reference/cockpit-layout.txt`. `A220PanelStructureTests` pins the whole tree.

- **Overhead**: Electrical, Hydraulics, APU, Equipment Cooling, Fire Protection, Fuel, Air
  Conditioning, Bleed and Packs, Anti-Ice, Pressurization, Emergency, Window and Probe Heat,
  Flight Controls and Recorder, Calls, TAWS (with Aural Warning Inhibit), Wipers, Interior
  Lighting, Exterior Lights, Signs.
- **Glareshield**: Captain Side Panel (chrono, master warning/caution), CTP Captain (QNH,
  STD, baro unit, minimums, COM and NAV standby tuning, nav source, crosstune; altimeter and
  minimums status rows), Transponder and TCAS, Flight Directors, CTP First Officer (QNH, STD,
  baro unit, minimums, nav source, crosstune, chrono; altimeter and minimums status rows).
- **Center Panel**: Standby Instrument (standby baro and STD), Gear and Brakes.
- **Pedestal**: Engines, Flaps and Speedbrake, Trim and Parking Brake, Audio Control Panel
  (per radio, VHF 1 to 3: transmit select, volume, receiver; the selected transmitter as a
  status row).
- **Utilities**: Simulation Options, Engine Data, Doors, IRS.

What the panel controls write — the cockpit behaviour XML is the authority ([A220-19]):
- "(guarded)" appears exactly where the cockpit has a guard ([A220-41]).
- Probe Heat Ground Test and the engine fire pushes are presses that write 1 ([A220-42]).
  The aircraft clears probe heat (its lamp lights); the engine fire pushes stay pushed —
  one-way in v1.0.10, measured live: a second press changes nothing, and only reloading the
  flight resets one. The APU and cargo fire pushes and the six bottles are latched
  (Normal/Pushed combos). No fire control is ever actuated by the ECL "do it for me".
- Speedbrake Lever: Retracted, Quarter, Half, Three quarters, Full, Max (emergency), written
  with the lever's own `SPOILERS_SET` scaling; each detent lights its own checklist bit,
  measured live ([A220-40]).
- Standby Baro (hPa or inches): walks the ISI knob — one probe click, then each aimed burst
  as one write of its click count, which the knob applies exactly (measured live) — reads it
  back from the stock altimeter 3 (`KOHLSMAN SETTING MB:3`) and says the landed value; Standby
  Baro STD is pulsed
  and reads `KOHLSMAN SETTING STD:3` back: "Standby altimeter STD", or the setting the ISI
  kept when the press did not land ([A220-43]).
- Equipment Cooling: Inlet Auto/Off (0 = AUTO, 1 = OFF, measured live), Exhaust Valve
  Only/Auto/On. Wipers: Off/Intermittent/Slow/Fast.
- VHF 1-3 Volume (0% to 100% in the knob's 29 detents) and VHF 1-3 Receiver (Off/On) set the
  knobs' own L:vars, which the aircraft turns into the stock COM volume and receive (measured
  live: On = receiving); the receiver is written as the wanted state, the volume on the knob's
  scale, and each is read back once you stop on a value — spoken only when it did not land
  ([A220-44]). VHF 1's volume reads 100% in a fresh flight: the aircraft's own start value.
- Not on a panel, on purpose: the oxygen mask test (its L:vars only drive the blinker
  animation), sidestick priority, CPDLC keys, CTP soft keys, ISI menu keys, the reversion and
  cockpit door panels, light-brightness knobs, the ACP keys and knobs other than VHF 1-3 (no
  variable), circuit breakers.
- The `msfsba` column of `tools/a220-gen/reference/cockpit-controls.csv` (and the `*` marks
  in `cockpit-layout.txt`) predate this structure until the next regeneration; the
  generator's map already describes it.

Hotkeys use the shared bindings:
- Input mode (`[`): Ctrl+S / Ctrl+H / Ctrl+A / Ctrl+V open the FCP speed, heading,
  altitude and vertical speed/FPA windows (knob walks, A220-7 to A220-12); Ctrl+P the
  autopilot window; Ctrl+B "Altimeter and minimums" ([A220-33]); Ctrl+N the radios
  window; Shift+M the FMS window; Shift+T the EFB. The AP, A/T, APPR and LOC toggles are
  the shared keys.
- FCU push/pull keys (input mode `[`, Shift+1..4 push / Ctrl+1..4 pull — heading,
  altitude, speed, V/S): the A220's knobs have no pull and their pushes are knob internals,
  so each key is the A220 control that does that key's job (`A220FcpKeys`): speed push/pull
  = FMS / manual speed (the outer ring, written as a target), heading push/pull = sync /
  HDG, altitude push/pull = VNAV / FLC, V/S push/pull = level off (ALT) / V/S. The mode
  buttons TOGGLE, so a key never presses a mode that is already engaged ("FLC already
  engaged."); every press is read back. Knots/Mach, fine/coarse and feet/metres stay in
  Ctrl+S / Ctrl+A.
- Output mode (`]`): Shift+H / Shift+S / Shift+A / Shift+V the FCP readouts; Alt+F the
  flight directors; Shift+1..6 V1, VR, V2, VREF, VAC and the flap speeds from the PFD;
  Ctrl+0 approach capability; Alt+E the CAS window and Ctrl+E the CAS call-outs
  ([A220-36]); Shift+G gear and brake temperatures; B the altimeter as the PFD shows it;
  X the squawk and mode; Ctrl+W the ND's next waypoint; D and Shift+D distance to
  destination and to top of descent from the FMS's active plan; Ctrl+Shift+C the ECL
  "first officer"; Ctrl+M the monitor manager. Alt+N/P/I say which keys carry that data
  instead. The full list is `HotkeyGuides/Synaptic_A220_Hotkeys.txt` (the in-app Hotkey
  List).

## FMS window (Shift+M) + ECL "first officer" (Ctrl+Shift+C)

- **One socket**: both forms scrape through the display pump's `A220DisplaysClient`
  (`CallAgentAsync`) — never a second DisplayUnits inspector connection.
- **Input protocol** (all live-proven): MKP typing = `1 (>H:A220_KBD_{ID}_1)` calc
  writes (seq-prefixed); field commit = in-page synthetic mouse click on the value
  node (`clickFmsField`); window nav = `H:A220_CTP_{FMS|CHKL|MAP…}_1` + in-window
  text clicks; EXEC/CNCL = `L:A22X Flight Plan Execute/Cancel` write-1 flags.
  **Never trigger the aircraft's hardware-keyboard Entry Mode** (it kills all sim
  input for a blind user); the H:-event path bypasses it entirely.
- Guided flows are ONLY the four ruled ones (SimBrief uplink, wind request, FUEL
  page, PERF DEP SET VSPEEDS) — scripted click sequences with per-step verification
  and honest stop announcements; labels beyond `FPLN UPLINK…` are unverified until
  the flight pass.
- **A clipped list row is still clickable — never add scrolling for it.** `document.elementFromPoint`
  returns null for a row scrolled out of its SVG clip region, which is every entry
  past the ~8th in a long list (32 STARs / 15 approaches at LGAV, the legs table).
  Those rows are live React nodes with handlers attached and React listens at the
  document root, so `fireClick` hit-tests first and then **falls back to dispatching
  straight at the row `<g>`** — PROVEN live 2026-07-29 by selecting `XORK2P`, the
  LAST of 32 LGAV STARs, through the public `clickWinText` API. Reading was never
  affected (clipping is not `visibility`, so the scrape always returned every row);
  only the coordinate hit-test was. Do NOT "fix" this by firing DSK-knob scroll
  events — it adds a whole stateful scroll-position problem for nothing.
- **ROUTE ▸ LEGS is a TABLE and gets its own grouper** (`A220FmsLegParsing`, pinned by
  `A220FmsLegParsingTests` against the live LCLK→LGAV capture). The generic
  label/value pass promotes each unclaimed token to its own row, so one leg read as
  four meaningless buttons ("266° 29.9", "GENOS", "2.00", "/-----") — the thing that
  made the flight-plan page unusable. Measured layout: each leg is TWO rows ~48 px
  apart — track+distance at x≈10 plus `RNP`+value at x≈578/619 on row *y*, then the
  waypoint at x≈17 plus the green speed/alt constraint at x≈303 on *y*+29. The
  track row is **absent** for the first leg and after every discontinuity, so it is
  optional, never assumed. `ParseLegs` returns the token indices it consumed and
  `ParseFms` skips them, which is what stops the fragments re-appearing. Pinned
  chrome (`→… HOLD… FIX… THRUST… MSG… ACTIVATE SEC OFFSET` + the UTC/DTG/ETE/ETA/FUEL
  headings) is drawn OVER the scrolling list so its y interleaves with the legs —
  it is excluded by IDENTITY, never by position. A `y < 200` guard drops the window
  header band, without which the `FMS1` header (x≈8, left column like a waypoint)
  parses as leg one.
- A repeated waypoint ident is normal (KEA appears 4× on one route: STAR end,
  transition, missed approach, hold) — every leg row carries its **occurrence** or a
  click always actions the first one.
- **The EXEC prompt is `L:A22X Flight Plan Modified`, announced by the DEF — never
  scraped.** The A220's EXEC is a PHYSICAL MKP key with its own lamp, and MKP.xml
  gives that lamp's driver directly (the EXEC button's `SEQ2_CODE` is
  `(L:A22X Flight Plan Modified)`); the FMS window never renders the word "EXEC" at
  all. So the original `ExecAvailable = tokens.Any(t => t.Text == "EXEC")` was
  PERMANENTLY FALSE and the "EXEC available" cue could never fire — the user
  reported never once hearing it. Two changes: `ExecAvailable` now derives from
  `Mode == "MOD"` (the renderer's own mode strings are ACT/SEC/MOD) for the
  breadcrumb, and the var itself is a `Continuous`+`IsAnnounced` monitor that speaks
  *"Flight plan modified. Press EXEC to activate."* on the RISING edge from
  `ProcessSimVarUpdate`. Announcing from the def, not the form, is deliberate: the
  form only polls while it is open, and a pending mod matters whether or not the
  pilot is looking at the FMS window. Do NOT re-add a form-side EXEC announcement —
  entering MOD would then say the same thing three ways, which is why the form's
  mode callout deliberately SKIPS the MOD transition and covers only ACT↔SEC. The
  falling edge is silent by design (it means the pilot just pressed EXEC or CNCL —
  announcing a direct user action is what the screen-reader rules forbid), and the
  var stays in the Ctrl+M manager so it can be switched off. Both directions are
  test-pinned, including that an "EXEC" token in ACT mode must NOT read as pending.
- **Deleting a discontinuity goes through the aircraft's OWN scratchpad, never a
  direct plan mutation.** The A220 scratchpad is a store shared across every DU view
  via the ViewListener event `A220.Scratchpad`
  (`RegisterViewListener('JS_LISTENER_SIMVARS', null, true)` +
  `triggerToAllSubscribers`), and its `text` has THREE states that the Fusion Input
  component branches on when a field is clicked: a string **submits**, `""`
  **copies** the field's value out, and **`null` deletes** — `null` is the cockpit's
  `--DELETE--` armed state. The discontinuity row's own slot handler is the FMS's
  `setLegFix(idx, null)`. **The delete is a three-phase C#-sequenced gesture —
  `armDelete()` → poll `armStatus()` until `ARMED` → `deleteDiscontinuityClick(n)`
  — never a single agent call.** `triggerToAllSubscribers` round-trips through the
  engine's event pump, so the armed state lands in the view a beat AFTER
  `armDelete()` returns; the original one-shot agent function clicked in the same
  JS tick as arming, the Input still saw an EMPTY scratchpad, and its click handler
  ran the **copy** branch — a silent no-op that still reported "DELETED" (the
  2026-07-29 "can't delete discontinuities" bug; field SUBMITS never hit this
  because real MKP typing fills the store seconds before the commit click).
  `armStatus` is fed by a passive second ViewListener tap subscribed like the
  aircraft's own store, so `ARMED` means the state genuinely arrived. The click
  path resets the scratchpad to `""` after ~600 ms so a refused delete can never
  leave DELETE armed under the next click, and the form VERIFIES the outcome by
  re-scraping and comparing the discontinuity count — it never announces success
  from the click result alone. The FMS then raises its normal MOD that the pilot
  must EXEC — MSFSBA never edits a flight plan behind the FMS's back, and never
  deletes silently (Delete on a non-discontinuity row says so instead of
  guessing). `Describe` states the affordance in the row text ("…, Delete to
  remove") because a blind pilot has no visual cue that the row is actionable.
- **Pressable-vs-data is decided by React-fiber onClick DEPTH, and page rows render in
  READING ORDER** (2026-08-10, from the "FMS can still be a bit of a mess" report). The
  agent's one fiber walk per non-gray token (`fiberInfoOf`) now also reports how many
  hops up the first `onClick` sits: a REAL pressable (page tile, sub-tab, soft key,
  LOAD/IRS/GNSS keys) owns it at depth 1 (the ACT dropdown at 3), while every data cell
  merely inherits the PAGE-level click catcher at depth 4+ — so "has an onClick
  ancestor" alone is useless, the threshold (≤3, `CLICK_DEPTH_MAX`) is the
  discriminator (measured on the DBASE and POS pages). Tokens tagged `cl` are the only
  ones `ParseFms` classifies as buttons when any tag is present; before this, DBASE
  table cells read as "BD-500-1A11, button" and dates as "06AUG26 02SEP26, button".
  Two coupled rules ride the same marker: (1) a scrape WITH `cl` tags + NO vbox rects
  means the page genuinely has nothing editable (the old all-editable fallback made
  every read-only DBASE value say "edit box"); scrapes WITHOUT tags — dialog scrapes,
  fixtures, an older agent — keep both fallbacks. (2) The form interleaves fields,
  buttons and orphan lines by their screen y (`FmsField.Y`/`ButtonRows`/`OrphanRows`)
  so a page reads top-to-bottom like the real screen — the old fields→buttons→orphans
  grouping scattered "DATA BASES" far from its own table. Legs pages still put the
  route first (deliberate — the soft keys draw OVER the list, so y-interleaving there
  would shuffle waypoints between keys).
- **CLOSED dropdowns are detected STRUCTURALLY via React fibers** (fixed 2026-07-30 —
  this was the last known FMS-form gap). A closed Fusion dropdown renders ONLY its
  current value, so no text, colour or box distinguishes it from a data row and a blind
  pilot could not know a chooser existed. Its component always carries an `options`
  array + an `onSelect` handler (`dropdownInfoOf`), so `fms()`/`fmsDialogTokens()` tag
  the value token (`dd`, plus `n`), `WinToken.Dropdown`/`FmsField.IsDropdown` carry it
  through, and the row reads "FLT PHASE, dropdown: CRUISE, Enter opens the list".
  Enter OPENS the list (the staged scratchpad is deliberately NOT typed into a chooser)
  and reports honestly if it fails to open. The fiber key is resolved once and cached,
  and only NON-gray tokens are walked, so the per-poll cost stays low. Live-verified:
  148 page tokens → exactly 3 real choosers (plan selector, UTC prediction, SPD/ALT
  display), PERF → 8, no false positives on plain values.
  **Do NOT announce the closed dropdown's option COUNT**: the props array can include
  options the aircraft HIDES — the plan selector reports 3 (ACT/SEC/MOD) but opens with
  2 — and a count contradicting what then opens is worse than none. The OPEN list
  announces the true count itself.
- **An open dropdown dismisses via `agent.dropdownClose()`, NEVER `taskMenuClose()`** —
  the latter requires the task menu's own frame (black fill + white stroke, 250-520
  units) and returns NO_MENU for a dropdown, so Escape on an open option list could
  never close it (found + fixed 2026-07-30; `DismissOverlayAsync` now routes by overlay
  kind). Each overlay kind has its own cancel: dialog → its DONE/CNCL, menu → the menu
  backdrop, dropdown → a backdrop point clear of the option column.
- **A field commit INSIDE a dialog must use `agent.clickDialogField` (dialog-scoped),
  never `clickFmsField`** — the window-wide version counts occurrences differently from
  the dialog-scoped rows the pilot chose from AND its value hunt (dy 10-60, dx<45 from
  the label) can land on a PAGE token showing through beneath the floating dialog. That
  is the same floating-dialog trap as the read side, but on the WRITE side, where it
  types the pilot's value somewhere they never chose. For the same reason the readback
  after a dialog commit reads the DIALOG rows, not `ScrapeAsync` (the page) — otherwise
  every dialog commit fell through to a vague "page changed" and the pilot never heard
  the course they just set.
- **`PB/D WPT` and `ALONG TRK WPT` are NOT pressable items** — they hold an INLINE entry
  field and carry no `onClick` of their own (bundle-confirmed), so pressing them just
  closes the menu and changes nothing. `taskMenuModel` flags them (`inp`, from a
  60–200-unit stroked rect in the band; live-verified to flag exactly those two and
  nothing else) and the form renders them as TEXT rows that refuse with the reason. A
  silent dead press is the worse failure. Driving them properly would mean submitting
  their input's own `onSubmit({bearing, distance})` via fibers — not built yet.
- **A follow-up dialog's content must be scoped by DOM ANCESTRY (its own `<g>`
  subtree, `agent.fmsDialogTokens()`), NEVER by a rectangle clip of the page.** A
  dialog FLOATS over the list, so a geometric clip pulls in whatever page text lies
  beneath it — live 2026-07-30, the `INTC CRS - RDS` dialog (250,548 482×488) swallowed
  RNP/2.00//-----/DISCONTINUITY/FIX…/COPY TO SEC/DTG/ETE/ETA from the legs list, which
  is the "messed up format" report. Scoped read = 0 leaked tokens (verified). `ClipTo`
  survives only as the fallback when the scoped read fails.
- **Going DIRECT does offer the course** (user requirement, verified live): the leg
  menu's `→…` runs `ee.directToLeg(idx)` and then opens the **`INTC CRS - <ident>`
  dialog** — a RADIAL read-out plus an EDITABLE `CRS` field (`onSubmit: ee.setCourse`,
  in its own vbox) over a course rose of crossing airways, with DONE. So the chain is
  Enter on the waypoint → Direct to → set CRS via scratchpad+Commit → DONE → EXEC.
  Same shape for **HOLD…** (`INBD CRS/DIR`, `QUAD/RADIAL`, `MAX/HOLD/BEST SPD`,
  `LEG TIME`, `LEG DIST`, `EFC TIME`, RESET, EXIT HOLD — 34 clean tokens) and for
  CROSSING…/FIX…/AIRWAYS…/PB/D WPT. **Every one of these creates a MOD that needs
  EXEC**, and a probe must discard it with the page's CNCL soft key.
- **Dialog readability rules earned from those two dialogs:** a `<text>` composed of
  several tspans must have its runs SPACE-JOINED (`textOfEl`) — raw textContent glued
  a title into "HOLD ATRDS"; a value's UNIT is a separate gray label to its right
  (`FindTrailingUnit`, closed set) and must be attached to the value or "200" and "KT"
  read rows apart; a dialog built around a GRAPHIC (the INTC CRS course rose draws ~30
  loose airway/tick labels) must collapse its unclaimed labels into ONE "Other labels
  (n): …" row so the CRS box and DONE aren't buried — collapsed, never hidden; and the
  open-announcement NAMES the editable fields ("Type in: CRS.") rather than counting
  them, because the dialog is the pilot's only chance to set what it asks for.
- **Every LEGS-page action is addressed by the AIRCRAFT's own `legIdx`, read from
  React's fibers — never by ident text + occurrence + geometry** (rebuilt 2026-07-30
  after the second in-sim report: menus opened for the wrong duplicate waypoint).
  Text+geometry addressing cannot survive a list that repeats idents or shifts under
  an edit. The fibers carry the truth: each row's component props hold `legIdx`, and
  its widgets expose the real handlers. So `agent.legs()` returns `{idx, id}` per
  visible row (`id: ''` = discontinuity), `openLegMenuAt(idx)` CALLS the row icon's
  own React `onClick`, and `deleteLegAt(idx)` CALLS the row Input's own `onDelete`
  (the FMS's `setLegFix(idx,null)`). No synthetic mouse events, no scratchpad
  arming, so neither can land on a neighbour or write the scratchpad into the plan.
  `Row.LegIdx` carries it in C#; text addressing survives only as the fallback when
  the fiber read fails. **The ident node is identified STRUCTURALLY, not by x**: the
  track/distance line (x=10), `THEN` and `HOLD AT` share the row's left column AND
  its `legIdx`, and the track line is the EARLIER DOM node — the first cut of this
  reported "266° 29.9" as a leg name and missed every icon. Only the ident sits
  inside the row's Fusion Input, i.e. the component whose props have BOTH `onDelete`
  and `value` (`isIdentNode`).
- **A discontinuity can SHARE its `legIdx` with a neighbouring waypoint — never
  dedupe `legs()` rows by idx alone, and every same-idx action must state WHICH
  row it means** (live 2026-07-30, the worst A220 incident so far: an idx-keyed
  dedupe dropped the gap row, shifted every row below by one, and DEL on the
  discontinuity called a REAL waypoint's `onDelete` — each press silently removed
  another waypoint; the pilot EXEC'd and the plan collapsed into a ~710 nm
  direct while the app reported "the delete did not go through"). The rules now
  enforced: `legs()` dedupes only co-located texts (same idx within ~12 canvas
  units — same-row repeats), never same-idx rows at different y;
  `rowNodesFor(idx, wantDisco)` disambiguates same-idx candidates by the
  ▯-placeholder test (true → the gap's slot, false → the real ident);
  `deleteLegAt(idx, true)` REFUSES (`WRONG_ROW|<ident>`) rather than run a
  non-placeholder row's delete, and echoes what it called (`CALLED|<text>`) so
  the C# log can prove which handler ran. `DeleteLegRowAsync` logs every refusal
  and no-op (with a `legs()` dump) to `debug.log` under `a220_fms`, and speaks
  the FMS's own amber reason when the aircraft refused. Do not weaken any of
  these back to "first DOM match wins" — a wrong-row delete is silent plan
  corruption, the exact failure fiber addressing exists to prevent.
- **`Row.LegIdx` is paired to the aircraft's rows by CONTENT
  (`A220FmsLegParsing.AlignLegIndices`), NEVER by a positional zip** (live
  2026-07-30 evening, the third addressing failure in one day: the form assumed
  the grouper's leg rows and the fiber `legs()` rows line up one-for-one, but the
  fiber walk omits rows the grouper shows — the origin airport and the runway
  leg — so every index shifted by 2; the discontinuity between the duplicate
  BPKs was addressed as TOTRI, `WRONG_ROW` refused every delete, and a click on
  one twin BPK would have acted on the other, which the first-token menu guard
  cannot catch). The alignment is a greedy monotonic walk pairing waypoint rows
  by exact ident and discontinuities by the empty/placeholder ident, with a
  SHORT lookahead window (3) so a fiber-only annotation row is skipped but a
  repeated ident far ahead can never capture the cursor; an unmatched grouper
  row gets `-1` (degrades to text addressing / an honest spoken refusal) and
  does NOT advance the cursor, so one mismatch cannot desync the rest.
  Duplicate idents pair in order — that is what makes twin waypoints safe.
  Pinned by the `AlignLegIndices_*` tests against the live shifted plan; the
  two sequences are INDEPENDENT parsers of the same screen and will disagree
  again — never reintroduce `legIdx[legRow++]`.
- **A revision-menu header is the ident PLUS navaid detail** — `KEA 115.00` for a
  VOR (live). The form's wrong-waypoint guard compares the FIRST TOKEN only; an
  exact compare slams valid menus shut on every VOR/NDB fix.
- **Procedural approach legs (`CI03L`/`FI03L` — course- and final-intercept
  pseudo-fixes, and also `D190T`/`D190H`-style radial fixes) have NO revision menu
  in the aircraft** (proven live 2026-07-30, LGAV 03L): their row icon exists and
  its React `onClick` runs, but the TaskMenu dispatcher (`TaskMenu/Leg/index`)
  switches on the FIX TYPE — Airport(0)/Vhf(4,8,9)/Ndb(5,6)/Waypoint(10,1,2) get
  menus, a fix-LESS leg gets the generic "Other" menu, and every other fix type
  hits `default: return;` and mounts nothing. It is NOT a scroll/visibility
  problem: a real fix (KEA) below the visible fold opened its menu fine, so never
  "fix" this by scrolling. `OpenLegMenuAsync` announces the honest "aircraft
  offers no revision menu for this leg" message; don't turn it back into a generic
  failure. (For reference: the legs list is a virtualized Fusion list component
  whose scroll offset is a React hook — dispatching that hook scrolls it, found
  during this investigation, currently unused.)
- **Direct-to / vertical-direct / intercept-course for ANY leg — including the
  menu-less procedural fixes — lives in the DIRECT-TO DIALOG**, opened by the
  route page's pinned "→…" soft key (`Dialog/DirectTo.tsx`, header "→ FPLN";
  verified live 2026-07-30: every leg listed with a "→" button (`ee.directToLeg`)
  and a "VERT →" + AT-altitude entry (`ee.verticalDirectTo`), plus a type-any-
  ident entry (`ee.directToFix`), an OFFSET field, and the CRS intercept-course
  field (`ee.setCourse`) — the "adjust course to final" entry). Opening the dialog
  raises NO MOD; acting in it does. The form speaks the "→…" soft key as
  "Direct to…" (`SpokenButton`) and the dialog title as "Direct to" — never let
  these regress to the bare arrow glyph, which a screen reader renders as nothing.
  The MKP `DIR` quick-access key is NOT this dialog — pressed with the FMS window
  up it CLOSED the window entirely (live) — don't wire it.
- **The Direct-To dialog has its OWN renderer** (`RenderDirectToDialog` +
  `A220FmsScreenParsing.ParseDirectTo`, pinned by
  `ParseDirectTo_GroupsLegsEntryAndFields`): the generic pass rendered its
  [→][ident] [VERT →][alt] table as glyph/orphan soup (the "confusing dialog"

## FMS review (2026-09-24, live at EGNT) — fuel page, direct-to, crossings

Measured against the running aircraft; every rule is pinned by
`A220FmsFuelAndDialogTests`.

- **A refused entry leaves its Input "pending" — read the Input's VALUE, not its
  text.** The bundle's Input sets its pending flag before submitting and clears it
  only on success; while pending it draws the SHARED SCRATCHPAD (amber for the ~3 s
  of the error box, cyan after) instead of its value. MSFSBA clears the scratchpad
  after a refusal, so the box drew an empty node and the field VANISHED from the
  form (the "fields disappear" FUEL-page report). The agent now substitutes
  `format(value)` for a pending echo (`inputDisplayValue`), and reports an empty
  node at its text position (+7/+5 units) so it pairs like a filled value. A pending
  echo is recognised only when the fill differs from the Input's own `textColor` —
  the origin runway is cyan BY DESIGN, and treating it as pending cost the legs row
  its "origin" tag.
- **One label per value.** Column headings (WT(LB), CG(%MAC), FUEL PLANNING(LB), the
  INIT page's AVG WIND) paired BELOW with the same box their row label paired INLINE,
  so every fuel entry read twice under two names. Pairing is now exclusive (inline
  beats below, then nearest dx); a label only ever takes its OWN best value, never a
  second-best, so the agent's `bestValueNode` click still lands on the row's box. A
  table cell paired below its heading is named row + column ("ZFW CG(%MAC)"), a
  label split over two lines ending in "/" is joined ("RESERVE/CONTINGENCY"), and a
  boxed value no label claims becomes a field named after its row plus its unit
  ("RESERVE/CONTINGENCY %"), clicked by POSITION (`clickFmsAt`).
- **A framed box is not always an entry.** The RESERVE weight is `controlled` with
  no `onSubmit` — a computed read-out. The agent flags it `ro`; it reads as plain
  "label: value". Inputs also carry their `min`/`max` (`mn`/`mx`), which the form
  speaks when an entry is refused ("ZFW not accepted: INVALID ENTRY. ZFW accepts
  81750 to 128000.") — ONE utterance; the old path spoke the error and then cut it
  off with the read-back. An error counts as THIS entry's only if the typed text is
  still on the scratchpad (the store keeps its last error for ~3 s).
- **The refusal box's own text is not a value.** `fms()` drops tokens inside the
  amber error frame; it used to read "GWT, edit box: INVALID ENTRY".
- **Field-click anchors are GRAY labels only**, counted like `ParseFms` counts them.
  The CROSSING dialog's altitude chooser shows "AT" right above the "AT" label, the
  anchor landed on the chooser, and a plain AT crossing altitude could never be set
  (NO_VALUE). Inline pairing reaches 180 units (DEPARTURES' OTHER AIRPORT box is 172).
- **Terminal (procedure) waypoints have NO revision menu** (GIRL1Y's NTW03/NTS08:
  the Suspense'd fix lookup fails and the menu closes itself; enroute GIRLI opens
  fine) — so CROSSING… is unreachable for them. Every legs row's green constraint
  is its OWN scratchpad Input (props `onSubmit` + `transition`): `clickLegConstraintAt(legIdx)`,
  reached by Commit on a waypoint row when the scratchpad looks like a constraint
  (`A220FmsForm.IsConstraintEntry`: digit- or slash-first with a slash — "/9000A",
  "/7000", "250/", "250/FL120"; idents are letter-first). Live: "/5000A", "/7000",
  "210/" all accepted.
- **Constraint vs PREDICTION is type size.** A legs constraint `<text>` mixes a
  large-type (tspan `ContentText`) constraint with small-type (`LabelText`)
  predictions — "↑210/7000" was a 210 kt constraint over a PREDICTED 7000. The
  agent wraps small-type numeric parts in `~…~` (`markPredicted`, green texts only)
  and `FormatConstraint` says "predicted". Flattened, a prediction read as a
  restriction.
- **Direct-To dialog**: the typed-waypoint entry box sits ~50 units BELOW its gray
  arrow (bundle arrow y=15, Input y=42) — both `ParseDirectTo` and
  `clickDirectToEntry` searched the arrow's own row and the row vanished. Each leg's

- **Dialog lists**: `fmsDialogTokens` carries the same fiber facts as the page scrape

  button. SELECT WPT options are named from their band (region + type + frequency +
  name), never from gray tags or the dialog's CNCL on the same row.

## Transports (recon-verified)

- **L:var names contain SPACES** (`L:A22X APU Switch`). `SetLVar`'s space/colon guard
  would route them to the unreliable data-def write, and `CalcPathVerified` never goes
  true for non-FBW defs — so **every A220 write goes through `ExecuteCalculatorCode`
  directly** in `HandleUIVariableSet`, which claims (returns true for) EVERY settable
  control. The MobiFlight RPN parser accepts spaces inside `(>L:…)` — proven live
  (taxi-light round trip incl. the downstream stock `LIGHT TAXI` flip) with
  `tools/A220CalcProbe` (standalone SimConnect→MobiFlight command sender; build it
  alone, not in the .sln).
- K: events also fire via the calc path (`{seq} 0 * {n} (>K:EVENT)`); every calc
  string carries the seq prefix so MobiFlight's identical-command coalescing can never
  drop a repeat press.
- Reads are ordinary data-def registrations (spaces are fine on the read path).
- Coherent debugger recon: all five DUs render in ONE React view
  (`VCockpit02 - DisplayUnits`, `MSFS_REACT_MOUNT` with `PFD_1_MOUNT` /
  `ND_2_MOUNT` / `EICAS_2_MOUNT`); MKP ×2, CTP ×2, ISI, FCP ×4, `efbA220` ×2.
  P3's FMA/ASA/CAS scrape targets that React tree. `Coherent.call('EXECUTE_CALCULATOR_CODE')`
  does NOT work from these views — don't use it as a probe channel;
  `SimVar.SetSimVarValue` from Coherent JS does work (emergency fallback transport).

### What the altitude walk got wrong, and why

Three separate faults, each able to report failure on a perfectly reachable altitude:

1. **The link-less session never got the grid walk.** `StartAltitudeSetAsync` bailed to
   the old measured fallback whenever `ReadAfdxLiveAsync()` returned null, because the
   stock SimVar was believed untrustworthy. It isn't. The read-back now prefers the 30 Hz
   CommBus and falls back to the 1 Hz SimConnect cache, so the grid walk is what
   *everyone* gets; the display agent only makes it faster (300 ms settle vs 1.3 s).
2. **The ring flip was never verified.** The write is sound, but a ring that did not take
   makes every later click 10× or 1/10 the assumed size, and the walk would grind against
   the wrong grid until it ran out of rounds. It now measures what a burst actually moved
   and re-selects the ring once if the step is off by more than 2×
   (`A220Afdx.StepIsWrongRing`).
3. **The coarse stage stopped "close enough".** Its exit test was `|delta| < step/2` —
   500 ft — while the final verdict demands 50 ft. An off-grid start within 500 ft of a
   round thousand therefore fired **zero clicks** and announced *"Altitude did not reach
   5000 — selector reads 5085 feet"* for a target one click away. Every stage now exits
   only when it is genuinely on its value (5 % of a step). `GridClicks` could always make
   that click; it was never asked to.

Pinned by `SynapticA220AfdxTests` (`GridClicks_ReachesARoundThousandLessThanHalfAStepAway`,
the `StepIsWrongRing_*` facts).

## AFDX CommBus tap (2026-07-31) — FD state, FD commands, trim, FCP read-back

Three things a blind A220 pilot needs are **not SimVars and not L:vars**. The aircraft's
WASM broadcasts them as JSON over the MSFS **CommBus**, and the DUs subscribe to them
there (`instrument.js`: `ii("A22X.<store>", rate, defaults)`, `src/avionics/lib/afdx/*`).
The display agent now registers a SECOND `RegisterViewListener('JS_LISTENER_COMM_BUS')`
subscriber — the same call the aircraft's own bus wrapper (`class Dn`) makes in that very
page, so the pattern is proven there — for three stores, and ships them in every
`snapshot()` alongside the SVG scrape:

| Store | Rate | What it unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| `A22X.Autoflight Data` | 30 Hz | `l_fd`/`r_fd` = REAL flight-director on/off; `cmd_lateral`/`cmd_vertical_fpa`/`cmd_vertical_pitch` = the FD command bars; `lateral`/`vertical` = the FG mode enums (TO/GA…) |
| `A22X.FCP Data` | 30 Hz | `spd_sel_ias`/`spd_sel_mach`/`hdg_sel`/`alt_sel_ft`/`vs_sel` — the FCP knob walk's read-back |
| `A22X.Flight Control Data` | 20 Hz | `pitch_trim` (+ the `pitch_trim_dn`..`pitch_trim_up` takeoff green band) and `rudder_trim` — the EICAS trim block's own source |

**`RegisterViewListener` is ASYNCHRONOUS — subscribe from its ready callback.** It takes a
callback fired once the listener is actually connected, and a `.on()` made before that is
dropped on the floor. The aircraft's own wrapper (`class Dn`) gets away with subscribing
immediately only because its listener is created at page load while its `.on()` calls
happen much later, during component construction; ours creates and subscribes in the same
tick. Getting this wrong is SILENT — the tap simply never delivers — and it cost a whole
live session (2026-07-31) in which FD state, trim, the altitude-unit truth and the FCP
read-back were all quietly running on fallbacks while three "fixes" were shipped on top of
them. So: subscribe from the ready callback (and immediately as well, harmlessly), RETRY
while nothing has ever arrived, and expose a diagnostic — `A.afdx()` returns a `diag`
block (listener / ready / subs / tries / ever / ageMs / err) which `A220Afdx.DescribeTap`
turns into a spoken cause on the Alt+F readout. **Any future scrape/tap of this kind gets
its diagnostic built at the same time as the feature, not after it fails.**

C# side: `Aircraft/A220/A220Afdx.cs` holds the block DTOs plus every formatter (pure,
pinned by `SynapticA220AfdxTests`); `SynapticA220Definition.Displays.cs` stores the
latest blocks per poll and exposes `LatestAutoflight` / `LatestFlightControl` /
`ReadFcpLiveAsync()`.

**Flight directors.** `L:A22X L/R Flight Director` is bound to `A220_ButtonMomentary` in
`ModelBehaviorDefs/Synaptic/A220/Interior/Glareshield/Autopilot.xml` — it is the button's
**press pulse**, so it reads 0 no matter what the FD is doing. That is exactly why the old
panel toggle never told the pilot whether the FD was on, and why a level write to it
("set 1 = on") was meaningless. Now: the panel entries are momentary **buttons**
(`A22X_L_FD`/`A22X_R_FD`, pressed via `PulseLVar`), and the STATE is two display rows
(`A22X_L_FD_STATE`/`A22X_R_FD_STATE`) rendered from `l_fd`/`r_fd` through
`TryGetDisplayOverride`. **Alt+F** reads the whole picture out loud: which sides are on,
the engaged FG modes (naming Takeoff and Go-around explicitly), and what the FD is
commanding.

**FD command sign conventions** were decoded from the aircraft's own rendering maths, not
guessed — a wrong sign in a guidance readout is confidently wrong rather than obviously
broken. They are asserted in `SynapticA220AfdxTests`:

- `cmd_lateral` — commanded BANK in degrees, **+ = right**. The FD bird's screen rotation
  is `roll + cmd_lateral` and `irs.roll` is left-positive (MSFS convention), so the
  command is satisfied at `roll == -cmd_lateral`.
- `cmd_vertical_fpa` — commanded FLIGHT PATH ANGLE, **+ = up** (bird at
  `-(pitch + fpa) * 9.4`, same form as the FPV symbol).
- `cmd_vertical_pitch` — commanded PITCH ATTITUDE, **+ = nose up**. Drawn inside the
  attitude group at `-pitch_target * 9.4`, i.e. above the horizon for a positive value.
  This is the **TO/GA pitch cue** and is null outside those modes — so TOGA flight-director
  guidance IS available and IS readable.
- The A220's FD is an **FPV-referenced flight-path director**, and the PFD only shows it
  when FPV data is valid AND that side's FD is on (`showFd`).

**Trim.** `efcs.pitch_trim` is the stab trim in the units the EICAS prints
(`t.toFixed(1)`), and `pitch_trim_dn`..`pitch_trim_up` is the green takeoff band that
CONFIG STAB TRIM tests against — so the read-out answers the warning, not just reports it
("3.1 units, OUTSIDE takeoff range 4.2 to 6.8"). `rudder_trim` is **+ = nose right** with
**±1 = full scale** on the EICAS pointer (`translate(t * 58)` across a 58 px half-width),
and the cockpit prints no number for it at all — hence percent of full travel. Both show
in **Pedestal → Trim** (`A22X_STAB_TRIM`, `A22X_RUDDER_TRIM_POS`), and the pump speaks
them once the pilot STOPS trimming, gated on the shared Shift+T trim-announcements toggle.
Aileron trim is deliberately NOT exposed: the EICAS renders its block with
`visibility: "hidden"` on this build.

Notes and limits:

- The two trim defs and the two FD-state defs are **carriers**: they exist so MainForm has
  a var to refresh, and their own value is only the fallback shown if the Coherent link is
  down. `L:A22X Horizontal Stabilizer` is the trim ANIMATION ratio, not the cockpit's
  units — never present it as the trim setting.
- With no data the rows read **"Unknown" / "Not available"**, never "Off" or a plausible
  number.
- One fact, one voice: the PFD's scraped `FD OFF` legend is suppressed while the CommBus
  block is live (both come from the same page, so this is belt-and-braces, not a second
  source of truth).
- A store the WASM stops broadcasting goes **stale after 3 s** (the systems stores after 6 s and the flight plan after 10 s, [A220-38]) and returns null — mirroring
  the aircraft's own self-reset — rather than freezing on a value C# would read as truth.
  The captain's CTP and nav stores keep 3 s for the knob walks' fast read, but the `sys`
  block ships them on the systems stores' 6 s, so a quiet CTP does not flicker the
  captain's rows to "not available".

## Invariants (do not revert)

- **Never register the `L:A22X Aural *` playback flags** (88 of them) — the aircraft
  speaks V1/radio heights/TAWS/takeoff-config itself; announcing the flags would
  double the audio. Exceptions that share the prefix but are real controls: `Aural
  Warn Inhibit` (+ its Lamp) and `Aural Internal Test`. Pinned by
  `SynapticA220DefinitionTests.NoNativeAuralFlag_IsEverRegistered`.
- **APU start is HOLD-to-start**: the combo's Start action sustains `2` for ~3.3 s
  (seq-prefixed 300 ms re-writes) then releases to `1` (Run) — inside the control
  write so the future ECL actuation shares it. `StopAllMotion()` (wired in
  `SwitchAircraft`) kills the timer.
- **FCP dialogs walk, never set** — RE-PROVEN against the live WASM 2026-08-10, so don't
  re-probe without new evidence: `HEADING_BUG_SET <val>` / `AP_SPD_VAR_SET` /
  `AP_ALT_VAR_SET_ENGLISH` / `AP_VS_VAR_SET_ENGLISH` all leave the FCP's own state
  untouched (the STOCK SimVar may move — `AUTOPILOT ALTITUDE LOCK VAR` took a 12000
  write while the FCP still showed dashes — so a stock var cannot confirm a SET write,
  though it does track each knob click, which is what the walk reads back: A220-8); the inc/dec events IGNORE their parameter (always one
  step); and the aircraft's entire JS→WASM CommBus call surface (grep `.call("A22X.`
  across html_ui — Display Tune / SSPC / Refuel / Configure) has no FCP channel. The
  FCP instrument.html views are pure renderers of `A22X.FCP Data`.
- **The ALT selector SNAPS TO ITS GRID on every click** (live 2026-08-10: 2625 ft + one
  coarse inc → 3000, NOT 3625): feet-coarse lands on round 1000s, feet-fine on round
  100s, so every multiple of 100 ft is exactly reachable in feet mode. The altitude
  dialog therefore runs a GRID walk — coarse ring for the thousands, fine ring for the
  hundreds, ring restored afterwards — not the measured walk, whose `cur + n×step`
  model cannot represent snapping (that model is what kept "converging" to values that
  weren't on the grid: the "always something close but not quite right" report). The
  measured walk stays for every other knob AND as the altitude fallback when the
  selector is stuck in metres; with the Coherent link down the grid walk still runs,
  reading the 1 Hz SimConnect cache (A220-8). Metric grid note: this
  session measured the metres-coarse step as 100 m where 2026-07-31 recorded 150 m —
  treat the metric step as UNKNOWN-until-measured (the measured walk does exactly
  that); never hardcode either number.
- **The VS thumbwheel is DEAD until VS or FPA mode is engaged** (live 2026-08-10:
  with no vertical mode, `AP_VS_VAR_INC` changes nothing and `vs_sel` publishes null;
  one `AP_VS_HOLD` later the same click moves it). The VS/FPA dialog ENGAGES the mode
  first, then walks — the LVFR A330 engage-before-write lesson again. If `vs_sel` is
  still null after engaging, it says so and STOPS: walking would converge on the stock
  SimVar, which this FCP never writes for VS.
- **The `] a/h/s/v` FCP readouts prefer the FCP CommBus block + the FG FMA enums**
  (`A220Afdx.LateralStatusPhrase`/`VerticalStatusPhrase`/`AutothrottleStatusPhrase` —
  real mode names: FLC, ALTS armed, FMS1, autothrottle SPD), falling back to the
  annunciator L:vars/stock vars only when the display link is down. The stock mirrors
  are conversion-noisy (alt 4101 vs FCP 4000), and `alt_sel_ft`/`vs_sel` null are REAL
  states (dashes / wheel inactive) that must be spoken as such, never rendered as a
  stale number.
- **The FCP walk reads back from `A22X.FCP Data` (30 Hz), not the 1 Hz SimConnect
  batch** (2026-07-31 — the user reported the dialogs "take ages and rarely reach the
  selected value precisely"). Both halves of that complaint came from the read-back:
  each round spent ~1.3 s waiting for a 1 Hz sample plus a whole extra round just to
  calibrate, and a sample that had not refreshed yet read as "the knob did not move".
  Now: ~0.2-0.3 s settle, the per-knob step is cached for the session in `_fcpStepCache`
  and re-derived every round as `moved / clicks`. Keep the SimConnect cache path (and its
  1.3 s settle) as the no-Coherent fallback — do NOT converge fast on a stale source. The
  step must stay measured, never assumed: a burst the sim partly swallows self-corrects
  through the same divide. **Clicks go out ONE PER CALL, never in same-string batches** —
  the 8-per-string batching this paragraph once described was measured on 2026-09-21 to
  apply as a SINGLE click (four `AP_ALT_VAR_INC` in one string moved 2100 → 2200).
  **Correction (2026-09-21):** this bullet used to say the stock `AUTOPILOT * VAR`
  SimVars are "not what this FCP publishes". They are — altitude, heading and speed each
  track their knob click for click (see the measurement table above). Believing otherwise
  is what pushed every session without a Coherent display link onto the inferior
  fallback walk.
- **The walk AIMS, it does not hunt** (`A220Afdx.AimClicks`, test-pinned): fire
  `round(|delta| / step)` clicks to land on the nearest value the knob can actually
  select, and treat a result of 0 as "already on the closest selectable value — stop and
  say so". Never go back to `floor()` plus a one-click creep: on a coarse step it
  straddles the target forever (neither bracketing value is inside the tolerance) and
  reports a value hundreds of feet out. Live case: the user asked for 6000 ft and got
  5085 ft, because **the FCP altitude selector was in METRES** — 5085 ft is exactly
  1550 m, and the day-one "492 ft per click" measurement is 150 m. A round number of feet
  is usually not selectable at all in that mode, so the altitude walk NAMES the mode in
  its result rather than leaving the pilot with a random-looking number.
- **A walk must never quit before it has moved the knob.** "Nothing closer is selectable"
  is only believable from a step measured ON THIS WALK — a step inherited from
  `_fcpStepCache` (or a bad earlier measurement) that exceeds the remaining delta makes
  round 0 compute ZERO clicks and announce a result without touching anything. That was
  the live "I set heading 270 and it stopped at 239" (2026-07-31). With nothing measured
  yet, fire one click and find out.
- **Never batch several `(>K:EVENT)` statements into one calc string** to save round
  trips. Whether the WASM samples more than one of the same event per frame is unverified,
  so a batch may apply whole, partly, or once — and since the walk derives its step from
  the distance covered, an inconsistently-swallowed batch feeds it a different step every
  round (live: "often it stops before reaching"). One event per call. The speed comes from
  the 30 Hz read-back, not from batching.
- **The ALT knob's PUSH is the fine/coarse ring** — `Autopilot.xml`'s `FCU_ALT_PUSH` has
  `BTN_VAR = L:A22X FG Altitude Fine` with `BTN_TOGGLE`. On COARSE the selector steps
  ~492 ft (≈150 m), so most round altitudes are not selectable at all and the walk can only
  offer "nearest selectable 6070". The altitude walk therefore selects FINE first (and says
  ", fine steps selected"), with a Fine / coarse button in the dialog to put it back. Two
  live observations pin the coarse step: 5085 ft and 6070 ft, which differ by 985 ≈ 2 × 492.5
  — not reachable on any 100-ft grid.
- **Walk direction is calibrated, never assumed**: if the first move goes the wrong way
  the inc/dec events swap for the rest of the walk (the MD-11 selector-polarity lesson).
  This is what makes the stabilizer-trim walk safe — driving a stabilizer confidently the
  wrong way, one burst after another, is the one failure here that actually matters.
- **Stabilizer trim is settable** (`A22X_STAB_TRIM_SET` numeric box in Pedestal → Trim):
  there is no set event and no trim L:var, so it is the same calibrated walk over
  `ELEV_TRIM_UP`/`_DN` (documented as RATE inputs — "drives the trim rate input this
  tick") reading `efcs.pitch_trim` back off the CommBus. The number to type is the one the
  EFB's takeoff-performance page gives: its own code renders that figure as
  `abs(trimUnitsNoseUp).toFixed(1) + "UP"`, interpolated from a weight (90–149.5 k lb) ×
  CG (14–35 %MAC) table, values 1.3–7.3. The walk refuses outright when the display link
  is down — with no way to read the trim back, walking it would be open-loop on a
  stabilizer. The result appends the takeoff-band verdict, which is also the cross-check
  that the EFB's scale and the EICAS's scale are the same one.
- **The Ctrl+H heading dialog carries the FULL lateral cluster** (2026-07-29 user
  ask, dialog-completeness rule): HDG select / NAV / Approach mode buttons (same
  events + `AnnounceModeResult` state keys as the Ctrl+P window) alongside
  heading-sync and half bank — a value dialog never ships with only its knob.
- **Parking brake is Continuous+IsAnnounced** — a hardware/keyboard toggle must
  announce ("Parking Brake: Set"); it was silently OnRequest-only once (2026-07-29
  fix). Panel-combo sets stay single-spoken via the global UI-echo wrap.
- **NAV radios are STANDBY-set only** — no NAV active-set, no NAV swap, anywhere
  (`NavRadios_AreStandbyOnly` pin). Manually activating an ILS frequency breaks the
  A220's NAV-to-NAV transfer, including the automatic FMS retune at 400 ft on a
  go-around (A220-FOT-22-30-012).
- **No spoiler-arm control** — the aircraft masks the stock `SPOILERS_ARM_*` events;
  there is no arm function (`NoSpoilerArmControl` pin).
- **Mode presses are never followed by silence** (user ruling): real FG transitions
  are spoken by the monitor; `AnnounceModeResult` adds the deferred honest
  "no change" call. TOGA (in the Ctrl+P window per ruling — it's a before-takeoff
  action) diffs an FG snapshot and always speaks.
- **Silent readout vars ride the batch**: `Continuous + IsAnnounced=false` gets NO
  subscription at all (stale cache) — cache-fresh quiet vars must be
  `IsAnnounced=true` and swallowed in `ProcessSimVarUpdate` (`_silentReadoutKeys`,
  the MD-11 `_silentReadouts` pattern).
- **The MKP CLEAR key is a ONE-CHARACTER backspace, never a scratchpad clear**
  (MKP bundle `popChar`, read 2026-07-30): each press removes one char, and a press
  on an ALREADY-EMPTY scratchpad **arms `--DELETE--`** (`text: null`). And a REFUSED
  field entry leaves its typed text in the scratchpad (the Fusion Input only clears
  on success) while its "INVALID ENTRY" amber state self-clears after 3 s. The old
  "press CLEAR once, then type" commit therefore concatenated every entry after a
  refusal into fresh garbage — the live 2026-07-30 FUEL-page report ("invalid entry
  msgs sit there and prevent entry"). The commit path now: agent `resetScratchpad()`
  (the aircraft's own store event) → confirm empty via the tap (`spState()`) → type →
  **verify the buffer equals `PredictScratchpadBuffer(sent)`** (mirrors `pushChar`:
  24-char cap; PLUS_MINUS appends '-' then toggles a trailing sign — pinned by
  `A220ScratchpadPredictionTests`) → click → clear again after the readback. Alt+C /
  Backspace in the form run the same store-reset clear.
- **The commit click's value hunt must SKIP GRAY tokens and prefer the label's OWN
  ROW — a bare nearest-dx hunt commits onto the neighbouring field.** Live
  2026-07-30 the FUEL page logged `label='ZFW' click=CLICKED:GWT` and
  `label='BLOCK' click=CLICKED:TAXI`: the entry tables right-ALIGN their labels
  into a column (ZFW/GWT/TOW/LW share an x; BLOCK/TAXI/TRIP likewise), so the NEXT
  LABEL DOWN sits at dx≈0-15 from any label while that label's own inline entry box
  is 60-75 units to the right — the hunt "correctly" picked the nearest thing, which
  was another label. Two rules, both in the shared `bestValueNode` helper (and
  mirrored by `ParseFms`, which must agree or the form READS one field and CLICKS
  another): a gray token is a LABEL and can never be a value; and INLINE (the
  label's own row) outranks BELOW before nearest-dx is consulted. Verified by
  porting the hunt to a geometry harness over the live layout: old rules →
  GWT/TAXI, new rules → each field's own entry box. This was the FOURTH distinct
  defect in one entry path (lossy typing, CLEAR-is-a-backspace, the 130-unit gate,
  this) — when an entry "does nothing", log the CLICK TARGET before theorising.
- **MKP typing must be CLOSED-LOOP — a paced open-loop send DROPS keystrokes.**
  Proven live 2026-07-30: typing `109270` with a flat 110 ms gap between
  `H:A220_KBD_*` events produced `10970`, then `09270`, then `1020`, then `19270` —
  a DIFFERENT character lost each time, so it is a lossy transport (calc-path event
  → engine pump → MKP instrument), not a too-short delay that a bigger constant
  would fix. `SendScratchpadTextAsync` now confirms each character against the
  scratchpad tap (`spState()`) before sending the next, resends once on a miss, and
  gives up after two so the caller's whole-buffer verify can restart the entry. Never
  "fix" a dropped-keystroke report by raising a delay; the loop is the fix. (This is
  also why the whole-buffer verify exists — it is what CAUGHT the drops, which
  previously reached the aircraft as a silently wrong number.)
- **FUEL page semantics (bundle `Fuel.tsx`, 2026-07-30):** FOB is **not enterable on
  the ground** — the FOB input renders only airborne with FMS FOB MODE = CALC (mode
  dropdown CALC/SENSED; `wow` gates it); on the ground fuel goes in via **BLOCK**
  (+ TAXI, RESERVE %), which are conversely **ground-only** (`disabled: !wow`). The
  reserve WEIGHT box is `controlled` with no onSubmit (display-only; clicking it with
  text answers INVALID ENTRY) — the PCT box beside it is the enterable one. Entry
  ranges follow the aircraft's weight-unit setting (`A22X.Fuel Data` store `metric`):
  ZFW 86,000–154,000 LB / 39,000–69,850 KG, ZFW CG 20–40 %MAC (1 decimal), BLOCK ≤
  38,350 LB / 17,400 KG, PAX 0–160 — an out-of-range entry (e.g. pounds typed while
  the FMS is in KG) is exactly an "INVALID ENTRY". The WT column header row reads
  "WT(LB)" or "WT(KG)" — that is how a blind pilot tells which unit the page wants.
- **The inline label→value-box pairing gate is 170 units, not 130** — the FUEL page's
  "NUMBER OF PAX" box starts 162 units right of its label (label x=8, input x=170);
  at 130 the field was unreachable. The gate lives in THREE places that must stay in
  sync: `ParseFms` (`inlineBoxed`), agent `clickFmsField`, agent `clickDialogField`.
  Pinned by `A220FmsFuelPagePairingTests`.
- **FMS-form list selection is restored by IDENTITY, not display text alone**
  (`ApplyRows`): an edit-box row's display changes while its value is edited (the
  Fusion input renders the live scratchpad text in place), so the old exact-text
  restore threw the reader to the top of the page mid-entry (user report
  2026-07-30). Order: exact display → (Kind, Label, Occurrence) → old index when
  most old rows survive (same page) → top only on a genuine page change.
- Flight Stage enum is **0-BASED** (0=Hangar … 7=Final) — CORRECTED 2026-07-29:
  the EFB's "AIRCRAFT START STATES" buttons write this same L:var with Cold and
  Dark=0 / Taxi=1 / GPU-APU=2 / Ready for take off=3 (proven in the efb-a220.js
  source via the live string decoder), and the EFB and WASM must share the scale
  for the feature to work. The earlier "1-based, gate read 3 = Apron" inference
  was a ready-to-fly gate spawn writing 3 = Runway/ready state — both scales fit
  that single observation, so it wasn't decisive. Verify in-sim: taxiing should
  announce "Flight stage Taxi", climb "Climb" (not one step late).
- **Ctrl+M: the A220 is in `DefAnnounceMuteSets`** (2026-10-14), so the wrap silences
  every `ProcessSimVarUpdate` branch by its row, and the existing per-branch
  `Muted(varName)` checks stay as belt and braces. Call-outs spoken from the display pump
  or a timer are outside that wrap and check their synthetic `A220MonitorRows` row
  ([A220-39]); the per-frame take-off feed is exempt from the wrap (`IsMuteWrapExempt`)
  so the Rotate and V2 rows, not the feed var, decide.

EFB DOM facts (live recon 2026-07-28, EFB "Version 1.0.0"):

- Real HTML, **plain JS — NOT React** (zero fiber keys); Bootstrap-grid markup with
  inline obfuscated sanitizer handlers on inputs. Field commit = native `.value`
  write + bubbling `input`+`change`+**`keyup`** + `blur()`. **The `keyup` is
  load-bearing (bundle-decoded 2026-07-29, AGENT_V 3):** every Settings text-field
  save handler is bound to `el.onkeyup` — the SimBrief ID one does
  `DataStore.set('user_setting_A220_SIMBRIEF', v)` + `Coherent.call(
  'COMM_BUS_WASM_CALLBACK','SetSimbriefUsername', v)`, which is what BOTH the FMS
  SIMBRIEF uplink and the loadsheet SIMBRIEF IMPORT depend on — and the
  Takeoff-perf ICAO/OAT/wind/alt sanitizers + their recalc hang off `onkeyup` too.
  `input`+`change` alone sets the text visibly but never saves ("entered my
  SimBrief ID but uplink/loadsheet don't work" bug). All ten `onkeyup` handlers
  are zero-arg closures reading `.value`, so a generic bubbling `Event('keyup')`
  is safe — no KeyboardEvent key data needed. On EFB startup the stored ID is
  re-pushed to the WASM (`SetSimbriefUsername`) only if non-empty, so a properly
  saved ID survives sessions.
- `#iniEFB > #renderer` holds one wrapper div per page; exactly one has class
  `visiblePage`. Pages by inner root id: `lockscreen`, `dashboard`, `equip`,
  `flight`, `loadsheet`, `takeOffPerfViewer`, `ldgperf`, `settings-bg`(+`settings`),
  plus class-identified `ofp-navigation` (OFP viewer) and `termcharts` (Charts).

  Panel State" after equipment clicks — agent emits it as an `alert` item + its

- The engine fire pushes stay pushed: in v1.0.10 a pushed engine fire push stays in, its
  fuel and hydraulic shutoff valves stay closed, and pressing it again changes nothing.
  Reload the flight to reset it (measured live 2026-10-16).

  service interphone and the mechanic call do nothing to the aircraft's systems: no checklist
  item senses them and no CAS message follows, because the aircraft's systems module has no

  APU bottle, CVR, service interphone and mechanic call; the rest by the module's own
  strings). They stay on the panels because they are the cockpit's, and a later version of
  the aircraft may wire them.

  lit live, and the installed v1.0.10 checklist pack has no item that senses them. The probe
  heat test itself works — its lamp lights, with the status message ADS PROBE HEAT GND ON.

  no decimals), so it cannot see a single 0.01-inch knob click; that is why MSFSBA reads the
