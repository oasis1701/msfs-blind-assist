- **ActiveSky** — a third-party weather add-on for MSFS. BA can read richer weather from it if you turn on **Enable ActiveSky integration** on the Weather tab. See [ActiveSky](weather.md#activesky).
- **AGL (above ground level)** — height above the ground directly below you. Output Q reads it. Compare MSL.
- **Airbus** — the European aircraft maker. Its airliners in BA include the A320, A330 and A380.
- **AIRMET** — a weather warning for lighter aircraft about hazards such as moderate turbulence or icing. Compare SIGMET.
- **Apron** — the paved area where aircraft park, load and refuel. Also called the ramp.
- **ATC (air traffic control)** — the controllers who give you clearances on the ground and in the air.
- **ATIS** — a recorded broadcast of an airport's weather, runways in use and altimeter setting, identified by a letter such as "information Bravo".
- **Autopilot (AP)** — the system that flies the aircraft by itself, following the modes you select.
- **Autothrust (A/THR) or autothrottle (A/T)** — the system that controls engine thrust to hold a speed or thrust setting. Airbus says autothrust, Boeing says autothrottle.
- **Backtrack** — taxiing along a runway in the opposite direction to the one you will take off or have landed in, usually to reach the start of a runway with no taxiway at its end, or to leave one. In the taxi planner, tick **Full-length departure (requires backtrack)** when a runway needs one.
- **Boeing** — the American aircraft maker. Its airliners in BA include the 737, 777 and 787.
- **CDU (Control Display Unit)** — Boeing's name for the keyboard and screen you use to program the flight computer. See MCDU.
- **Centreline** — the painted line along the middle of a runway or taxiway.
- **Clearance** — permission from ATC to do something specific, such as taxi, cross a runway, take off or land.
- **Destination runway** — in BA, the runway you choose with Input Shift+D. It feeds distance readouts, Visual Guidance, the landing assist and exit planning. See [Choosing a Destination Runway](approach-and-landing.md#destination-runway).
- **Docking** — the final few metres of parking at a gate, where you stop exactly on the stop mark. See [Docking Guidance](gates-gsx-and-docking.md#docking).
- **ECAM** — Airbus's warning and system display system. The upper part is the E/WD (engine and warning display), the lower part the SD (system display).
- **EFB (Electronic Flight Bag)** — a tablet in the cockpit used for performance calculations, loading, ground services and charts. BA's own flight planning window is opened with Output Shift+E; each aircraft's tablet is covered in [Aircraft EFBs](planning-your-flight.md#aircraft-efb).
- **EICAS** — Boeing's equivalent of ECAM: the engine indications and crew alerts display.
- **Exit (runway exit)** — a taxiway you leave the runway by after landing. A high-speed exit leaves at a shallow angle so you can turn off fast. See [The Landing Exit Planner](approach-and-landing.md#landing-exit).
- **FCU (Flight Control Unit)** — the Airbus autopilot panel on the glareshield, with knobs you push (managed) or pull (selected). Compare MCP.
- **Flare** — raising the nose just before touchdown to slow the descent for a gentle landing.
- **Flight director (FD)** — commands shown to the pilot telling them how to pitch and bank to follow the active modes.
- **Flight level (FL)** — altitude on the standard altimeter setting, in hundreds of feet: FL350 is about 35,000 feet.
- **FMA (Flight Mode Annunciator)** — the strip at the top of the primary flight display showing which autopilot, flight director and thrust modes are active or armed. See [General Flying Concepts](general-flying-concepts.md).
- **FMC, FMS, FMGC** — the flight management computer that stores your route and performance data. Boeing calls it the FMC; Airbus's version is part of the FMGC. You program it through the CDU or MCDU.
- **Glideslope (GS or G/S)** — the part of an ILS that gives you a descent path to the runway, usually 3 degrees.
- **Go-around** — abandoning an approach and climbing away, to try again or divert.
- **GSX (GSX Pro)** — a third-party add-on that provides ground services such as pushback, jetways, catering and boarding. BA has an accessible window for it. See [Gates, GSX and Docking](gates-gsx-and-docking.md).
- **Hard pan** — a BA option that sends a steering tone fully to one ear instead of panning part-way.
- **Hold short** — an instruction to stop before a line, usually a runway's holding point, and not go past it until cleared.
- **Holding point** — the marked place where you stop before entering a runway. Some are named, such as "holding point A1".
- **IAS (indicated airspeed)** — the airspeed the instruments show; what the aircraft "feels". Output S reads it.
- **ILS (Instrument Landing System)** — radio guidance to a runway: a localizer for left and right, and a glideslope for up and down.
- **Input mode** — BA's hotkey mode for commands that change something or open a window. Press the left bracket key to enter it. See [Output Mode and Input Mode](getting-started.md#hotkey-modes).
- **Intersection departure** — taking off from a point partway down the runway instead of the very start, which leaves less runway ahead.
- **Lineup** — positioning the aircraft on the runway centreline, pointing down the runway, ready for takeoff.
- **LNAV and VNAV** — Boeing's modes for following the FMC route laterally (LNAV) and vertically (VNAV).
- **LOC (localizer)** — the part of an ILS that guides you left and right onto the runway centreline.
- **Managed mode** — Airbus term for letting the flight computer control speed, route or altitude profile. Compare selected mode.
- **Mach** — speed as a fraction of the speed of sound, used at high altitude: Mach 0.78.
- **MCDU (Multipurpose Control and Display Unit)** — Airbus's keyboard and screen for programming the flight computer. Input Shift+M opens it on most Airbus aircraft. The A380 replaces it with the MFD.
- **MCP (Mode Control Panel)** — the Boeing autopilot panel on the glareshield, with a button for each mode. Compare FCU.
- **METAR** — a routine weather report for an airport, issued about every half hour or hour. Output Shift+M opens the METAR report.
- **MobiFlight WASM module** — a free add-on you put in your MSFS Community folder. BA needs it to operate controls on several aircraft, including the FlyByWire and Fenix Airbuses.
- **Monitor Manager** — BA's window for silencing individual automatic announcements, opened with Output Ctrl+M. See [The Monitor Manager](core-concepts.md#monitor-manager).
- **MSL (mean sea level)** — height above the sea, which is what the altimeter shows when set to QNH. Output A reads it. Compare AGL.
- **NOTAM** — a notice about a temporary change at an airport or along a route, such as a closed taxiway.
- **Output mode** — BA's hotkey mode for readouts and windows that only tell you something. Press the right bracket key to enter it. See [Output Mode and Input Mode](getting-started.md#hotkey-modes).
- **Pan** — moving a sound between your left and right ears. BA's steering tones use pan to tell you which way to turn.
- **PFD and ND** — the primary flight display (attitude, speed, altitude, FMA) and the navigation display (route, map, weather).
- **Pushback** — being pushed backwards off a stand by a tug before taxiing.
- **QNH** — the local altimeter setting that makes the altimeter read height above sea level. Given in hectopascals (1013) or inches of mercury (29.92). Output B reads your setting.
- **RA (resolution advisory)** — a TCAS instruction to climb, descend or hold your vertical speed to avoid a collision. You must follow it. BA announces RAs on aircraft that provide them. Compare TA.
- **Readback** — repeating a clearance back to ATC so they can confirm you heard it correctly.
- **RNAV or RNP** — navigation by GPS and the flight computer rather than by radio beacons. An RNAV approach is flown this way.
- **Rotate (VR)** — the speed at which you raise the nose on the takeoff roll.
- **Runway incursion** — an aircraft, vehicle or person on a runway where they should not be. Hold short instructions exist to prevent them.
- **SayIntentions** — an AI-driven air traffic control service for flight simulators. BA can read its transmissions and build a taxi route from its clearances. See [SayIntentions](networks-and-integrations.md#sayintentions).
- **Selected mode** — Airbus term for flying a value you dialled on the FCU. Compare managed mode.
- **SID** — a published departure procedure from a runway to the start of your route.
- **SIGMET** — a warning of significant weather hazardous to all aircraft, such as severe turbulence, severe icing or thunderstorms.
- **SimBrief** — a free online flight planner. BA can load your SimBrief plan and brief it to you. See [SimBrief](planning-your-flight.md#simbrief).
- **Squawk** — the four-digit code you set in your transponder so ATC can identify you on radar. Output X reads it.
- **Stable approach** — an approach that is on path, at the right speed, configured for landing and at a normal descent rate by a set height.
- **Stand** — a parking position for an aircraft, either at a gate with a jet bridge or out on the apron.
- **STAR** — a published arrival procedure from the end of your route to the start of the approach.
- **TA (traffic advisory)** — a TCAS alert that traffic is close. No avoiding action is required, but look out for it. Compare RA.
- **TAS (true airspeed)** — your actual speed through the air, which is higher than IAS at altitude. Output T reads it.
- **Taxi** — moving the aircraft on the ground under its own power.
- **TCAS** — the traffic collision avoidance system, which warns of nearby aircraft and gives resolution advisories. See [TCAS Traffic](takeoff-climb-and-cruise.md#tcas).
- **Threshold** — the start of the usable landing part of a runway. A displaced threshold is set back from the end of the pavement.
- **Top of descent (TOD)** — the point where the flight computer plans to start your descent. Output Shift+D reads the distance and time to it on many aircraft.
- **Transition altitude and level** — the heights at which you change between the local QNH and the standard altimeter setting.
- **Transponder** — the radio that answers ATC radar with your squawk code and altitude.
- **V1, VR and V2** — takeoff speeds. V1 is the decision speed: after it, you continue the takeoff even with an engine failure. VR is rotation speed. V2 is the safe climb speed with an engine out.
- **VATSIM** — a free online network where real people act as air traffic controllers. BA can announce its activity through vPilot. See [VATSIM](networks-and-integrations.md#vatsim).
- **Vertical speed (V/S)** — how fast you are climbing or descending, in feet per minute. Output V reads it.
- **Visual Guidance** — BA's two-tone guidance for flying a final approach by hand. See [Visual Guidance](approach-and-landing.md#visual-guidance).
- **Vref** — the reference landing speed for your weight and flap setting. Approach speed is usually Vref plus a few knots.
